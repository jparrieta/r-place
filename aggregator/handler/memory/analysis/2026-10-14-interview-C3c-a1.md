## 1. Order between rooms: a fifth problem, or a problem of this canvas?

It is not a fifth problem for every organization. It is a problem for any organization whose work sits next to others' work, on a surface that no authority allocates.

Puranam, Alexy and Reitzig define an organization by its boundaries and its goals (pp. 163, 166). They rule out "goalless networks, and crowds (but not mobs)" (p. 164). The canvas as a whole is such a network. So their frame leaves order between rooms out on purpose. For each room, though, handling its neighbours is a condition of reaching its own goal.

My declarations already state the condition:
- **Negotiated order:** "Strauss's negotiated order lives inside an organization whose rules frame the bargaining; here no rule frames it and no party can deliver its members."
- **Territoriality:** "no authority recognizes any claim, so a claim holds only as long as others honour it."

The upkeep-to-neighbours arrow (A04) already shows two routes into neighbour talk. In 401 rooms carrying both themes, upkeep came first in 235 (share 0.586; null mean 0.501, 95th percentile 0.539).
- **Erosion.** On the early side, 56 of 93 rooms hold that order. Neighbours appear because they overwrite the work. That depends on the canvas's rule that anyone may change any pixel. A surface with enforced property would not produce it.
- **A full surface.** On the late side, only 6 of 15 rooms hold it. These rooms ask for space before they have a mark. Any finite shared surface would produce this route.

Three things in the rooms would tell the two answers apart:
- **Fullness at held size.** If neighbour talk starts earlier and more often as the canvas fills, a finite surface drives it. The late side is 15 rooms, 12 at held size, so this test is thin.
- **The hosting rooms** (the candidate element P19). These rooms build under a host's rule. If they ask the host instead of negotiating with neighbours, the problem is the missing frame. Any platform with property rules would remove it.
- **Whose work a border serves.** "please take it like one pixel away from the army bomb" [bangtan/c0085, h71.72] protects each room's own mark. That is the candidate new problem. "We are trying to create a heart with both flags in it" [AmericanFlaginPlace/c0396, h62.03] is a joint goal. That is the four problems one level up, Meta-organization, and nothing new.

Live and let live comes from Ashworth (1980, recalled) on trench warfare. Trenches are another shared, finite surface with no framing authority. So as I recall the source, this problem is not unique to the canvas.

## 2. A finding on reward, or a frame that cannot lose?

It is a finding about the rooms, not a test of the frame. The finding:
- a reward offered before the work appears in 9 rooms;
- credit after the work appears in 156 rooms;
- celebration of the finished work appears in 442 rooms.

The frame's definition allows inducements "explicit or implicit" and "extrinsic or intrinsic". It applies only "if its constituent agents need to be motivated to contribute" (p. 165). Any work done with no visible reward counts either as intrinsic motivation or as agents who needed none. No observation of absence can count against it.

Two observations would have counted against a testable version of the frame. That version needs the condition made visible: members saying in their own words that they need motivating. Examples are fatigue, quitting, "nobody's here", or urging others to give up.
- **Recovery with no inducement.** Members say they are tired or giving up, no inducement of any kind follows, and the work resumes anyway.
- **An inducement that changes nothing.** Rooms that offer an inducement when the work stalls fare no better than rooms that offer none.

The one offered reward I quoted fits the frame's prediction. The inducement arrives when the work slows: "Its currently slow going. As an insentive, for every person who joins in and comments below I will post 5 pig gifs! No limit!" [Pigifs/c0036, h48.94].

This test can be counted. Quitting under strain is in 20 rooms, cheering the builders on in 23, and prizes in 7. I have not counted their overlap or their order. The test also needs "intrinsic" defined in advance. For example: pride or fun counts only when a turn names it as a reason. Until both are done, my reward claim is a description, and the frame's reward claim is untested here.

## 3. Could a public record show the expectation of credit at work?

Yes, more easily for a room than for a member. The turn would be a call or plan, made before or during the work, that names future credit as a reason to place.
- **Room level.** The record has this turn. Signing the sub's name on the mark is in 35 rooms; leaving the work unsigned is in 2. A thread arguing whether to add the sub's name is a room arranging, in public, to be credited later.
- **Member level.** The turn would be a reply to a call that ties joining to being named. One form is a member asking, before placing, whether helpers will be listed. The stronger form is the breach: a member who placed, was not credited, and says they will not help next time. A breach shows the expectation by its failure.

I have seen no member-level turn of either kind in what I read at this stage. I did not search for one. A search prompted by the frame's reward definition would be an abductive read, logged before it runs.

Completion ritual's disconfirm clause is a different test. It asks whether rooms that mark the work show more later solidarity. Carrying the work to new canvases is in 145 rooms. That clause tests what the ritual does afterwards, not whether members expected credit beforehand.

Its largest member is "building and reporting the mark", in 237 rooms. The building is stigmergy; the reporting is talk. My record is talk, so it sees the reports, not the trace at work. Under stage C3's section 6, that means the cut was wrong. It would need a recut, and A01, A03 and A07 would be recounted on what remains.
- **If Heylighen extends stigmergy to traces people read and argue about,** my declaration claims a difference that is not one. I believe he extends it to human collaboration such as Wikipedia, but I cannot verify that here. Then only the declaration changes.

**Second: Ahrne and Brunsson (2011).** I recall their decided elements as membership, hierarchy, rules, monitoring and sanctions. I gathered Decided order's members against that list. **Third: Collins (2004).** The declaration says his rituals need bodies co-present. I recall that he treats emotional energy as what draws people back to the next ritual. If so, Completion ritual is a reward mechanism by its own source's theory. The reward mapping would then rest on Collins, not on my wording.

**What a different source changes in general:**
- The declaration changes: borrowed, adapted or coined, and the `changed` and `mistaken_for` clauses.
- The third-order themes and the counts stay.
- The tier is recut only where the source shows a theme joins things the field keeps apart.

## 5. Visibility returns: should it be an element?

Not yet. What I have:
- It is coined and in 117 rooms.
- Its members: recruiting canvas visitors (76 rooms), pushing posts up the rankings (22), drawing lapsed fans back (7), making passers-by notice a demand (5), the creators' notice (4), press coverage (4), spelling slogans with post titles (3), fearing newcomers (3), a tile as payment (2).
- It carries the residue of six pass 5 rooms: parahumans, TheAdventuresofTintin, shishamo, hunterxhunter, luciomains and scp.

Three reasons hold it back:
- **The largest member may be a call.** Recruiting canvas visitors is 76 of the 117 rooms. The stage C2 reviewer placed it beside the call, so it may be Mobilization's outward arm. The returns proper (notice, press, lapsed fans, feared newcomers) are a handful of rooms each.
- **My reward mapping is weak.** The return goes to the room, not to a member. Recruiting is closer to the frame's inducements "to motivate entry and continuance of membership" (p. 165), which means getting agents. That is allocation more than reward.
- **No order test has been run on it.**

What I would need first:
1. **A split, by a rule written first:** what the room does to be seen, apart from what comes back to it.
2. **An order count** under the connection statement, against the 500-shuffle null: does the first return come after the mark is announced or reported?
3. **A gate fitted to the claim.** The members carry gate "all", but a return needs a work, so the gate should be "work".
4. **The six residue rooms read again** under that rule.
5. **A loop test.** Do newcomers who say the canvas brought them later answer calls? That would be the model's first grounded loop, and it is the point March makes: "an influx of the naive and ignorant" (p. 86). The fear side is 3 rooms, and March's claim about learning is beyond what talk can show.

## 6. After the literature: what I trust more, and what I trust less

**More:**
- **Refusals carry reasons and acceptances come bare (F-014).** The prediction could have failed, and half of it did: refusals were not longer.
- **The bound on decided order (F-006).** It holds at room size. It now places thebluecorner by its setting, where before it was only a residue.
- **The spine as organizing without authority.** Both works that contradicted it turned out to differ in setting. Bechky names that open question herself (p. 17).

**Less:**
- **The concept.** It is renamed as borrowed, and its head-count function failed (F-015).
- **Counts that read stance from the theme.** Stage C3b showed that the theme a reply sits under does not track its stance. The refusal arrow (A16) is rated strong on an order count of themes. Its mechanism is now better grounded by reading than its count is.
- **My own declarations.** Mobilization's and Completion ritual's declarations use the frame's words, "allocate" and "pays". So the frame stayed out of my theme names but not out of my declarations. "Pays" went too far: prior.md and the frame's definition both say so.
- **The recalled sources.** Stigmergy's source could split the strongest part of the model.
- **The goal.** No code tracks it. The starter (R01) and the room settling its mark (P02) carry more than the coding supports.

The reason is the same for both sides. What I trust more passed tests that could have failed. What I trust less rests on my own words, or on a coding assumption that a read has now shown false.
