## 7. Starts and templates as theories

Felin and Zenger's own definition supports dropping "contrarian". A theory starts "with a question or problem". It prompts "a novel hypothesis or conjecture about paths to a solution", and leads "to experimentation", refined "partly informed by experimental actions" (2017, p. 262). Contrarian belief comes in as what makes a theory valuable: "To be unique, economic theories have to be founded on beliefs not held by others" and "Valuable theories require a contrarian belief" (p. 262). They also give a mark of a theory: it is falsifiable. Quoting Popper, "Every 'good' theory is a prohibition: it forbids certain things to happen" (p. 264).

So my C3a ruling of "about different things" does not hold. The better ruling is that the setting differs. Some starts and templates work as theories in their sense; most do not show it.

**What tells a theory from a plain proposal.** At the start the two look the same. "Let's build our flag here" is already a guess that it can be built and held there. They differ in three things the record can show:
- a reason attached to the plan, about the canvas or about other rooms ("it will hold because ...");
- a prohibition that follows from the reason: no expanding past a line, no attacking a neighbour, keep it small;
- after a loss, a turn that reads the outcome against the reason, then a changed plan. Rebuilding the same plan in the same place is not a revision, and neither is quitting.

F-014 shows where the reasons surface. Acceptances come bare. A plan's reason gets written mostly when someone refuses: "Why? That'll just cause them to want to attack us and they're not even getting in our way" [uoft/c0115, h60.07]. That turn states a guess about how neighbours will respond, and it forbids an act. So theories are visible mainly in refusals and in revisions after a loss. A theory that nobody contests and that never fails leaves no trace in the threads.

**The room that shows it most clearly: opticgaming.** This account comes from my stage A1 and B1 notes; I have not re-read the room at this stage.
- The conjecture: "On /r/place , the bottom left corner is green and labeled GREENCORNER . I say we make an OG logo and change it to GREENWALL. Anyone down?" [opticgaming/c0191, h6.43]. It is a guess that an existing green mark can be reused.
- The test: a brick Greenwall over the Dota 2 logo was proposed and begun. It was then dropped by a Discord decision. The reason is off the record.
- The revision: the room asked Green Lattice for room and approval. Green Lattice explained its limits, and the room accepted a site south of the lattice.
- After that, the wall grew past its organisers: strangers copied the bricks. That is self-selection to a theory (2017, p. 266), from outside the room.

day9 is the clearest revision after a loss inside the model. The pass 5 story says the move "sends it back to choosing a spot and cutting the design to what can survive". day9 lost its border at hour 48, moved within two hours, called members to a new plan, and finished at hour 53. That sentence is my story's reading, not a count.

udub shows a template revised in public: "Maybe make it 20x20? Or just a purple W?" [udub/c0008, h55.02], then "20x20 is possibly a better idea... Will update template image and upload here." [udub/c0009, h55.09].

**The rooms that refute it: found marks and inherited flags.** The goal arrives already built, with no conjecture and no experiment. "I saw one in the top right that said /r/anime. It was rather small, but it was there." [anime/c0177, h69.66]. "SWEET!" [Dorset/c0014, h68.53], posted in a thread on finding Dorset's marks. F-017 counts 43 rooms that found their mark placed and 152 with an inherited goal.

**A mixed case: FloydVsVoid.** It relocated and rebuilt its prism after losses while chanting "The Prism Will Shine!" [FloydVsVoid/c0058, h33.84]. The place was revised; the picture was held as faith. In Popper's terms, much of Repair work, in 526 rooms, may be guarding a theory against refutation rather than testing it. I have not tested that.

**What it would bound in their claim.** They say "a key outcome of hierarchy is systematically advantaging one actor's theory of value over another" (2017, p. 265). Here no hierarchy exists. The template does that job: the first template posted tends to be followed. That fits A11, but A11 is theorised, with its order at chance (0.602 against a null of 0.583).

## 8. Goal emergence in the model

Yes, but not as a new element beside the ones that exist. I would widen P02 from "the room settles what its mark will show of it" into setting and revising the goal. I would also draw the arrow the model lacks, from the move back to the goal. That would close the model's first loop.

The goal's parts already sit in elements:
- R01, the starter;
- P02, settling the mark;
- P20, the host's line;
- K02, judging too few hands;
- P10, the move;
- P13, the close.

A separate element would count those acts twice. Two things are missing: a home for the goal as one object, and the arrow from a loss to a new goal. P10 says the room "moves: a new spot, a smaller version, a fresh start", but no arrow leaves P10.

**What it would be made of.** These are the third-order themes F-017 counts:
- **Setting the goal:** proposing the room's mark (166 rooms), settling the mark's design (281), putting decisions to a vote (18), building the homeland's flag (96), drawing national emblems (39), finding the room's mark placed (43), adopting a sign others began (6).
- **Revising the goal:** weighing fighting against moving (17), relocating after losing the spot (116), choosing upkeep over expansion (43), concentrating effort on fewer pieces (29), quitting under strain (20).

The contest in the host sub stays in Boundary work and P20. Arguing the canvas's worth (76 rooms) and objecting to the canvas flood (72) are about whether the sub hosts the goal, not about which goal.

Gathering these themes would be a fourth-order recut. Under rule 11 a recut needs a literature showing that a theme holds two things the field keeps apart. That literature exists. Puranam and colleagues keep the goal apart from the four problems. Repair work holds the task itself and also "choosing upkeep over expansion", which is a choice of goal. Territoriality holds space and also "relocating after losing the spot".

**The arrows.**
- **Into the goal:**
  - the starter, R01. Today A02 runs from R01 straight to the calls;
  - the room's own sign, a condition not in the model. The candidate K04, "the room acts as a body it was before the canvas", comes nearest;
  - a find, the mark found placed.
- **Out of the goal:**
  - to the calls, P01. This reroutes A02's mechanism: calls gather around a goal;
  - to the template, P04. This is A11.
- **The loop:**
  - keeping the mark (P05);
  - then a loss;
  - then judging too few hands (K02);
  - then the move (P10, via A10);
  - then a revised goal;
  - then the calls again.
  - A10 is thin: 21 rooms carry both, and the order holds in 11 (0.524 against a null of 0.298). The loop would rest on few rooms at first.

**What it would claim, specific enough to be wrong.** A room's goal is set early, by a start or a find. It is argued over its pixels, not chosen between rival goals. It changes only after a loss, and the new goal is smaller or elsewhere, not different in kind. It fails if:
- rooms change their goal by argument, with no loss;
- after a loss, rooms rebuild the same goal unchanged;
- after a loss, the new goal differs in kind.

**What I would need first.**
1. **A connection statement that can see a second occurrence.** The current statement reads only first concepts: "the first concept of FROM comes before the first concept of TO". It cannot see a loop. The test needs a statement like this: after the first move concept, a new or cut design comes before the next call. It also needs its own shuffle null.
2. **A reading under rules written first.** The theme a record sits under does not track its stance. "Settling the mark's design" in 281 rooms includes chat about the design that changes nothing. Only reading can tell a revised goal from talk about the old one.
3. **A gate fitted to revision:** "work", meaning a room with a work to lose. The current members carry "all".
4. **A check on the host contest:** does it change any room's goal, or only where the talk goes? If only the second, it stays in P20.
5. **The right stage.** Section 5 forbade revising the structure to fit the four problems claim. This change belongs to a later stage that may change the model, not to C3c.

**How the two answers connect.** If starts are theories in Felin and Zenger's sense, the widened element is the room's working theory of what it can build and hold. The arrow from the move is its revision after refutation. The name would then be borrowed. What the material adds is a setting: the theory is tested within hours, in public, and the evidence comes from strangers overwriting the work.
