## 1. Rare decided order: how strangers organize, or what leaves words?

The record already holds three tests that separate the two readings.

**The chat test.** If rooms decided their structure in a chat and left no trace in the thread, decided order should show up less where a chat exists. It shows up more. That holds within each room-size third, so room size does not explain it. The next table gives the share of rooms with any decided order, split by whether the room moved planning into a chat. Rooms are split into thirds by their number of concepts.

| room size | with a chat | without a chat |
|---|---|---|
| smallest third | 2 of 40 (5.0%) | 4 of 195 (2.1%) |
| middle third | 10 of 97 (10.3%) | 5 of 138 (3.6%) |
| largest third | 18 of 139 (12.9%) | 7 of 96 (7.3%) |

The chat does not swallow decided order. The two travel together.

**The clock.** Decided order comes late:
- 13% of its concepts fall after hour 72;
- for calls to place the share is 3%, and for repair 6%.

Rooms that decide a structure do it after the build is under way. They ask for a leader when calls scatter, and they hand the work over at the close.

**FP-08.** Asking "who is in charge?" costs one line, and a thread is where it would be asked even if the leaders sat in a chat. It was asked in 14 of 705 rooms. Absent decisions leave no words, but an absent question is evidence: nobody needed to ask.

**My lean.** The rarity is mostly real. Strangers on this clock organized mostly without deciding how. Decided order is a late, partial addition, mostly in larger rooms that already have a chat. I hold this at about two to one, not more, for three reasons:
- 46 rooms is a small base.
- The chats are not in the record.
- The coders dropped uncoded items.

To raise my confidence I would read the 33 rooms where members bring a chat's decisions back to the thread (T3-152). The question is whether those decisions are decided order (leaders, votes) or plans (spots, templates). I would also look at rooms with standing authority. Mods are named in 349 rooms, and a room with a moderator may have no reason to ask who leads.

## 3. Letting FP-04 and FP-08 bear on the model without a share

I would carry both as conditions on how evidence is read, never as weights.

**What FP-04 means for an arrow.** A war word alone does not show hostility. An arrow whose instance is a war word needs an act on the canvas behind it: pixels covered, a repair called. Today's check shows where the war words sit:
- Repair work holds 20.9% of them, and Negotiated order 17.9%.
- Repertoire of contention holds 5.5%, Live and let live 3.0%, and Rivalry under 2.4%.

The register is mostly the speech of defence and border talk, not of attack. A visible play marker (lol, /s, :P) appears in only 2.8% of these concepts. Stance stays invisible, so I would not lean on it either way.

**If the war words are often play:**
- **Live and let live barely moves.** Its evidence is restraint, said mostly in words of peace ("build around it") and shown in the work's shape.
- **Repertoire of contention keeps its acts.** An erased logo is erased whatever the mood. Play changes what an attack means, not what it does, so the arrow from contention to repair holds either way.
- **Rivalry is the most exposed, and it may even gain.** Taunts, chants and victory claims are competitive play by nature. Kilduff's rivalry is a competitive relationship, not hatred, and play fits it better than a reading of enmity.

**The named-words rule.** It tells T3-010 (borders) from T3-149 (truces), and both sit in Negotiated order. A misread war word moves an item within one construct, not across two. The fourth tier absorbs that doubt.

**FP-08** goes beside Decided order as a claim: leadership is taken for granted. It also blocks one inference. The model may not read the absence of fights about leaders as evidence that someone led.

## 4. The names

The contract asks for construct names at this tier: a name in the material's words is "a description one tier too high". So I would keep construct names and carry the rooms' phrase as the gloss, as I did. But in some cases the rooms' phrase says more than the borrowed name. The next table lists them.

| construct | the rooms' phrase | what the phrase adds |
|---|---|---|
| Stigmergy | "a half done logo" | the trace is a call; the borrowed name comes from insect nests |
| Repair work | "keep an eye on it" | most of the work is watching, not fixing |
| Live and let live | "build around it" | on the canvas, restraint is a shape, not a ceasefire as in Axelrod's trenches |
| Territoriality | "we were here first" | the ground of the claim: arrival plus work |
| Completion ritual | "we did it" | strangers making a "we" in two words |
| Visibility returns (coined) | "NEW SIGN = NEW SUBSCRIBERS" | the room's own accounting of what being seen brings back |

Two acts have no construct yet, and I think they are Gioia's nascent concepts:
- **"On it."** The one-line commitment (FP-01). It is joining at almost no cost, with no obligation and nothing owed back. No literature name fits it as well as the rooms' phrase.
- **"While they sleep."** The night shift and time-zone timing (T3-141 and FP-03). Rooms treat the world's clock as both a resource and a threat: "before the yanks all wake up and take over". "Temporal coordination" loses the strategy in that sentence.

If stage C3 withdrew a source, I would coin names from these phrases for Stigmergy, Live and let live and Visibility returns. I would keep Negotiated order, Reciprocity and Meta-organization even then: the rooms' words for those acts are generic ("diplomacy") and the literature's names fit.
