**Where the ranking would change if a recalled source says something other than I recall.**
- **Lundin and Söderholm could rise to the top three.** I believe their theory gives temporary organizations a sequence from an action-based start, through building commitment, to a planned end. If it does, it explains the whole sequence and the close, not just the last element. My declaration also says their transition is planned from the start; if that is wrong, the declaration's difference clause fails.
- **Collins could rise to about rank 5.** That happens if emotional energy draws members back without bodily co-presence. It would then explain the close and the carrying of work to later canvases (145 rooms).
- **Granovetter could move from contradicted to explaining.** That happens if thresholds can rest on expected participation, not on observed participation. Judging the room's hands by its readership would then fit a threshold model.
- **Grassé and Heylighen could fall to "contradicts the construct as cut".** That happens if stigmergy excludes talk. Puranam's transparent task architecture (verified, p. 172) would take their place for the half-built picture.
- **Axelrod could rise above Strauss.** That happens if most refusal reasons are fear of retaliation, such as "That'll just cause them to want to attack us" [uoft/c0115, h60.07]. My codes cannot say, because they record only whether a reason was given.
- **Pomerantz and Levinson could fall to "names".** That happens if preference organization marks dispreferred answers by delay and softening but not by reasons. The pattern in F-014 would stay; the mechanism would be mine.
- **Schegloff and Sacks: the citation may be misplaced.** I believe "conditional relevance" comes from Schegloff (1968), not from the 1973 paper. If so, the rank stays and the citation for "the answer is due" changes.
- **Olson could move from open to explaining small cores.** Puranam cites Olson for "privileged groups" (p. 171, verified in Puranam). If Olson's privileged group fits the rooms carried by one or two members, his account explains those rooms.
- **Ahrne and Brunsson: the rank stays, the counts move.** If their list of decided elements differs from mine, the members of Decided order change, and the F-006 count must be recomputed.

## 10. The most central dynamics

I ordered the dynamics by how many rooms carry them and by how far their order beats the shuffle null. **No loop is grounded in the model.** Every "feeds back" below comes from the stories, not from a count.

1. **A start gathers calls, and calls bring hands that build.**
   - *Sequence:* a member proposes or begins a mark (R01), calls go out (P01), members build and report the mark (P03). Within a call, joining comes as one bare line.
   - *Feeds back:* reports of progress may prompt the next call. Not tested.
   - *Carried by:* A02, fair: 64 of 89 rooms, 0.719 against a null mean of 0.473. A01, strong: 137 of 190, 0.721 against 0.467. A12, theorised. F-014, on attack calls only.
   - *Best room:* metroid. "Help us draw Metroid over at /r/place!" [metroid/c0001, h4.27] was answered two minutes later with the location and a placed piece [metroid/c0002, h4.29].
   - *How sure:* sure of the order. In 25 of 89 rooms calls come before any start. A01 is partly constitutive. The mechanism of joining is theorised.

2. **Building becomes keeping, and keeping outruns the hands.**
   - *Sequence:* the mark stands (P03), the room watches and repairs it (P05), then turns to scripts and spare accounts (P14) and asks other rooms for help (P16), then joins coalitions (P17).
   - *Feeds back:* calls to defend are calls, so the room keeps calling. Not a counted loop.
   - *Carried by:* A03, strong: 182 of 291, 0.625 against 0.392. A06, strong: 160 of 203, 0.788 against 0.603. A13, fair: 171 of 240. A19, thin.
   - *Best room:* day9, a within-case room in which the whole sequence runs.
   - *How sure:* sure for A03 and A06; less sure past help between rooms.

3. **Keeping meets the neighbours, and order between rooms is negotiated.**
   - *Sequence:* there are two ways in. On the early side, erosion comes first and then talk (56 of 93 rooms). Late rooms that arrive at a full canvas talk first, asking for space (only 6 of 15 hold the erosion-first order). Talk settles borders (P08). A settled border gives members a line to keep (P07). Talk over a border opens trade in help (P16).
   - *Feeds back:* a breach reopens the talk, when rooms hold others to agreements.
   - *Carried by:* A04, fair: 235 of 401, 0.586 against 0.501. A05, thin. A14, thin. Negotiated order is in 467 rooms.
   - *Best room:* unturned. "We have to stop this takeover!" [unturned/c0059], then "Maybe we can arrange something with r/rarepuppers to get them to build somewhere else?" [unturned/c0060].
   - *How sure:* moderate. A04 beats its null only modestly, and the late side is 15 rooms.

4. **A call to attack, and the room's refusal.**
   - *Sequence:* a call turns hands against a rival's work (P09). The room answers with refusal or restraint (P07), invokes a truce or a deal (P08), or carries the contest on in talk (P18). A refusal comes with a reason that states the room's norm or the attack's cost.
   - *Feeds back:* the stated norm may govern later calls. Not tested. Claim C02, a tie from before the canvas that names the rival, is untested for order.
   - *Carried by:* A15, strong: 93 of 129, 0.721 against 0.487. A16, strong: 82 of 104, 0.788 against 0.529. A17, fair. A18, fair: 56 of 68. F-014: reasons in 23 of 38 refusals against 2 of 14 acceptances (p 0.003).
   - *Best room:* nyjets. "Wipe out the Dolphins. They're building." [nyjets/c0086, h47.85] was answered one minute later: "Nah, keep /r/place neutral." [nyjets/c0087, h47.87].
   - *How sure:* high on the reason asymmetry as I read it. Lower on A16's order count, since it rests on themes that do not track stance and the arrow is partly constitutive.

5. **The close runs in order.**
   - *Sequence:* the work is done (P03), then celebrated and credited (P11), then recorded (P12), then carried on at the close (P13).
   - *Feeds forward:* past the clock, 145 rooms carry the work to new canvases. That is a goal set after the end.
   - *Carried by:* A07, strong: 182 of 282, 0.645 against 0.433. A08, strong: 225 of 289, 0.779 against 0.565. A09, fair: 113 of 145.
   - *Best room:* stevenuniverse, told whole from its first call to its record at pass 3.
   - *How sure:* sure of the order. Part of it is close to definition: a room celebrates after it finishes.

6. **The host's line.**
   - *Sequence:* calls flood the host sub (P01). Moderators gather canvas posts into megathreads, remove posts or change the rules (P20). Members object to the flood; the goal is contested in the host in 152 rooms.
   - *Feeds back:* the line sends the room's canvas organizing into a megathread or a new sub.
   - *Carried by:* A20, strong: 132 of 155, 0.852 against 0.538.
   - *Best room:* argentina. "17 de 25 threads en portada del sub son sobre el mural de r/places..." [argentina/c0273]. My translation: "17 of the sub's 25 front-page threads are about the r/place mural."
   - *How sure:* the order is strong but partly constitutive, because the line answers the flood by definition.

7. **A loss, doubt, a move, and calls again.**
   - *Sequence:* the work is overrun (P05), the room judges it has too few hands (K02), it moves to a new spot or a smaller version (P10), and it calls again for the new spot. That last step is in the story but not in an arrow. It is the loop the model lacks.
   - *Carried by:* A10, thin: 11 of 21 rooms, 0.524 against 0.298. F-015 says the judgment of too few hands rests on the readership.
   - *Best room:* day9. It lost its border at hour 48, moved within two hours, called members to a new plan, and finished at hour 53.
   - *How sure:* low. Only 21 rooms carry both elements, and the return to calling is not drawn.

8. **Decided order where a body governs territory.**
   - *Sequence:* a faction forms on the canvas, appoints officers and lays down rules, rules on guests, and splits over doctrine.
   - *Carried by:* F-006 (bounded): 7 of 39 rooms against 39 of 666, p 0.014 at held size, with the gap in the smaller rooms. Claim C01.
   - *Best room:* PurplePixels. "Looking for positions to fill!" [PurplePixels/c0057, h8.58]; "Good idea! Would you like to be Recruitment Overseer?" [PurplePixels/c0137, h8.81]. thebluecorner is the room that most threatens the model.
   - *How sure:* sure of the count at held size, but the numbers are small. The sequence from formation to split comes from stories, not from a count.

**Left out:** the argued design settled by a template (A11). Its order is at chance across rooms (0.602 against 0.583) and holds only inside reply threads.
