## What was hard

The hardest distinction was between practices that used the same canvas objects. A call to make Pink Diamond, a report that it was cracked, a repair to keep it legible, a concession that shortened it for Polandball, and a war claim about the same pixels are not one practice. I kept making, reporting, maintaining, negotiating, and fighting separate because their interactional work differs.

The local Lapis takeover was also easy to confuse with Lapis canvas art. I kept it separate whenever the passage enacted altered quotations, personas, subreddit design, or moderator fiction rather than placing pixels. Shared character vocabulary and simultaneity did not establish one practice or a causal relation.

Post-lock comments alternated among mourning, pride, relief, blame, and moral criticism. I treated these as contrasting participant accounts. I did not describe them as a community change of mind because different speakers voiced them and no passage establishes collective conversion.

## Complaints

Branch B makes a whole-case account from readings no single person performed. The forest helps with sequence but cannot recover the shared attention of a whole-week reader. Requiring causal claims while dividing interpretation by arbitrary slices makes cross-slice relations appropriately rare; I found no defensible cross-slice causal link and said so rather than converting sequence into causation.

The search returns the first regex match per item, so broad case words such as “destroy” produce many ordinary-show false positives. The requirement to inspect matching items is necessary, but a query hit total is not analytically meaningful without those reads.

## Branch-B limitations

One coder read each slice, so there is no agreement measure. Slice boundaries are arbitrary relative to episodes; whole threads stay together, but replies in an owned thread can occur during later slices. Relations were assembled from slice cases and selected item reads rather than one whole-week reading. The forest displays reply structure and time, but adjacency alone is not causation. No causal relation was carried across a slice boundary; cross-slice chronology is reported as sequence.

The final slice returned to ordinary fandom and supplied no assigned event code. That supports a bounded closing edge, not a claim that all members stopped remembering the event. Likewise, complete searches within this corpus cannot establish absence in Discord, private messages, neighboring communities, or deleted material.
