## What was hard

The hardest distinction was between shared work and claims of consensus. Many posts use “we” and “our,” while comments disclaim authority, describe missed messages, undo intended changes, and call the room unorganized. I treated those as contrasting layers and uneven participation, not proof that no shared practice existed.

Border exchanges were also difficult because accusation, negotiation, apology, repair, and renewed takeover coexist. Reply structure preserved sequence but never supplied causation by itself. I used only later passages that answer, name, or recall earlier ones for relations, and described later disappearance reports as sequence where no actor was supported.

The room's own April Fools song reversal had to remain separate from the canvas despite occurring during the same event window. Image-only posts likewise required restraint: titles and participant descriptions were codeable, but linked images were not treated as observed evidence.

## Data problems

No reader held the full branch-B week: each slice had one coder, there is no agreement measure, and the boundary was arbitrary with respect to ongoing border and Discord activity. Context openings and after-window descendants help with edges but do not replace missing exchanges. Relations carried across 01|02 are explicitly marked in the case account.

Discord is the main evidentiary gap. The corpus says decisions and “everything” moved there, but only returned plans, measurements, corrections, directives, and the invite are visible. Deleted bodies and inaccessible linked images further limit claims about visual appearance and local exchange context. The r/smashbros recommendation thread continues to the edge of the window.

Python repeatedly emitted a non-fatal macOS warning after attempted cache creation under `/tmp` was refused. Coders also reported rejected shell heredocs before execution because the shell attempted temporary writes outside the run directory. No task state was written outside the run directory; later helpers stayed inside it.

## Theme boundary review

The two themes that nearly merged were `sounding alarms and defending the weez` and `making, breaching, and repairing border deals`. They stayed apart because defense recruits protection against a perceived attack, whereas border work proposes, accepts, violates, or repairs a negotiated boundary.

I reviewed all grouping in slice order and recorded the decisions in each theme's `regrouped_from` field. The main regrouping split a broad neighbor category into treaty work, practical approaches/making room, and post-event welcoming. I also separated the Rivers-death exchange from celebration because those turns develop and resist a joke. After the counter-reading I reviewed the grouping again and made no further changes: exhaustion already stood alone, and the contrary local erasure passage did not turn celebration into the same practice.

## How the reading held up

As branch-B lead I did not read raw threads during fleet coding and never worked from memory of a raw thread. Before the gate I read the briefing and all openings for orientation. After consolidation I worked from the ledger, both complete slice cases, the pooled codes in prescribed slice order, and the forest; during the counter-reading I opened every returned item through `read item`.

I did not go back to raw material because I could not remember it, and neither slice became indistinct in favor of the other: the ledger and `_lead_notes.md` externalized the handoff, while the incremental code reader kept slice order visible. The run had a scheduler-required checkpoint after batch 02 collection and then resumed. On resumption I reread `_lead_notes.md` and `ledger.md`, did not recollect either batch, and continued with consolidation. The checkpoint was procedural, not a crash or lost-state interruption.
