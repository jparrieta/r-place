## What was hard

The difficult boundary was not “event versus no event” but two contemporaneous April Fools practices: the room's own PirateStone/SpongeBob makeover and r/place. Their vocabulary overlaps through pirates, Patches and the word “place,” but shared words do not make one practice. I kept them separate unless a passage actually recruited the makeover into canvas conduct, as `62s34x` did.

Within the canvas material, proposals, operational calls, locating redirects, neighbour bargaining and reciprocal help nearly collapsed into one coordination theme. I kept them apart because they do different work: nominating a possible target is not recruiting to an established build; answering where the logo is is not bargaining over a border; and relaying another community's help request is not negotiating Hearthstone's own location.

The late title “Forsen Boys in PLACE” (`63182u`) was the thinnest interpretable conduct. Its removed body defeated any attempt to say whether it was a proposal, report, joke, attack or alliance, so it remains a single-code theme.

## Data problems

Branch B gives up a whole-week reader. One coder read each of 113 slices, there is no agreement measure, and arbitrary slice boundaries may divide an exchange. The account therefore uses slice cases for post/comment comparisons and relations, pooled codes for grouping, and the forest for sequence; the forest does not turn adjacency into causation.

Removed bodies limit `63182u`, `635qvp`, `635zjb` and `63ddoh`. Direct images and Discord discussions are outside the textual record. The archive cannot show silent pixel placement, private coordination, or whether unanswered proposals were attempted.

The counter-reading searches returned many lexical false positives: “Patches” in ordinary decks, “replace” matching place, “card back” in reward talk, Rocket League as a game purchase, and 800 as dust arithmetic. ## Complaints

The instrument correctly exposes uncoded stretches through the forest, but a 113-slice branch-B account still requires the lead to infer cross-slice thematic continuity from first-order summaries written by different readers. This is weaker than one reader holding the exchanges, especially where a long thread remains active across many later slice spans. The contract's strict relation rule usefully prevents that weakness from becoming causal prose.
