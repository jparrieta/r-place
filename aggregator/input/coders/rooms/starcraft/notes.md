## What was hard

The hardest distinction was between a hostile description and an endorsed practice. “Invading,” “under fire,” “take over,” and “baddie faction” occur in live disputes, but other passages negotiate borders, prohibit retaliation, or challenge the labels. I kept the positions in tension instead of converting different speakers into a community change of mind.

It was also difficult to state outcomes without seeing the canvas. “We did it,” “we held,” and reported alliances are participant claims supported as claims; they are not independent visual verification. Image-only posts and templates were not interpreted.

The April Fools material required a second boundary: Blizzard's mutation, local pranks, and care for a returning contributor are codeable community practice but are not canvas conduct. They remain separate themes and chronology episodes.
