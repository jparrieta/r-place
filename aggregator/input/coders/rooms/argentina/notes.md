## What was hard

No reader held the complete week. Twenty-three coders each read one slice, and the lead assembled relations from pooled codes, slice cases, the ledger and the forest. Exchanges continuing across arbitrary slice boundaries were therefore harder to interpret than within-slice exchanges. I marked the one relation used across batches 04 and 05 in the case and did not use adjacency alone as evidence.

The central difficulty was separating bounded agreement from room-wide control. `dfpeb4t` genuinely reports an “imagen oficial consensuada” maintained by Placebot, and `6348si` genuinely reports a Discord decision moving Tango. Those passages do not erase the repeated public requests for an Estado Mayor, leader, sticky, megathread or usable channel. The final account therefore says that particular designs were controlled while the wider campaign remained contested.

Another difficulty was keeping the room's own Argentina/Brazil April Fools restyling separate from canvas action. Its codes remain under “playing the Argentina–Brazil subreddit swap joke,” not under raids or alliances.

## Data problems

The branch-B design gives no whole-week agreement measure. One coder read each slice; some active threads straddle slices and were visible to the later coder only through marked openings. The forest restores sequence but cannot turn divided reading into a single reading.

The `62mo4s` and `62swan` displays were transport-clipped during their coders' whole-thread work, leaving specific saturation limits recorded in batches 04 and 08. Batch 16 also encountered a clipped display but recovered its middle by paging the served output. Deleted and removed bodies are widespread, and linked images and videos were not evidence unless participants described them in text.

Discord, voice, IRC, GitHub, Taringa and external canvases are evidenced venue choices, but their internal conversations are unavailable. Reddit sometimes receives decisions or status reports, yet attendance, deliberation and many assignments cannot be checked.

The pooled `timeline.md` contains valid item-linked facts but is not globally chronological because some fleet coders rewrote their copy before collection. At batch 12 collection I struck the uncited editorial line `Slice 12 additions (facts from this slice, in time order):`; it was a heading/read, not a one-line fact with an item id. No other facts-file line was struck. The retained fact lines themselves were reviewed at every collection.

The forest reports 178 uncoded stretches of forty or more characters within coded items and mean coded-item coverage of 0.79. Those stretches are visible analytic choices, not missing corpus text.

## Counter-reading search

Before searching I recorded this competing account: stable shared command and an agreed design may have governed the effort. If so, accepted leaders or one main sticky, repeated compliance with an official image, and reliable settlement through Discord should be present.

Two scoped searches covered leader/Estado Mayor/sticky/main-thread language and official/consensual/reference-image/Discord-decision language. The broad expressions also returned unrelated political, work, diet and economics uses of *jefe*, *oficial*, *todos de acuerdo* and *sticky*. I inspected the returned snippets and opened the relevant event items through `read item`; null results are therefore limited to the event-facing matches, not the full semantic range of those words.

The strongest contrary item is `dfpeb4t`, followed by `6348si`. They establish bounded consensus and a bounded Discord decision. Repeated requests in `62q0uw`, `62qoq0`, `631blx`, `631o58`, `dfqp5e2`, `dfrmavm` and `dfrud8c`, plus the missing official Discord in `dfrvf1h`, prevent extending that finding to stable room-wide command.

No thread changed disposition, and no raw thread had to be opened: every relevant hit sat in a thread a fleet coder had already read whole.

## Anomalies and leads

Participants report using Placebot, a hivemind and secondary accounts, while another speaker later contrasts “somos buena onda y no usamos scripts” with a canvas full of bots. These are different speakers and positions, not proof of a collective reversal.

`dfsajcm` says the subreddit had never been so friendly during the preceding 72 hours. This is an unusually explicit participant explanation of the event's social effect, but the ordinary late slices and concurrent complaints about “cáncer de los píxeles” prevent treating it as a measured community-wide change.

The unanswered pxls.space proposal `63bjdy` is the thinnest visible afterlife. Its external canvas was not available, so the record cannot decide whether action occurred there.

## Theme review

Grouping review: after writing the account I reviewed all 17 themes against their practices and variations; I did not regroup. The themes were opened and extended incrementally in slice order, and every live code belongs to exactly one.

The two themes that nearly merged were “celebrating what Argentines made together” and “turning the pixel week into shared memory.” I kept them apart because the first is live accomplishment and morale during placement, while the second recounts, preserves or commemorates the event as an object in time.

## How the reading held up

I did not work from memory of a raw thread during fleet coding: branch B prohibited the lead from reading raw threads, so I worked from the batch ledger and later from the slice cases, pooled codes and forest. I did not go back to a raw thread because I could not remember it. During counter-reading I deliberately reopened returned items because the method requires reading search results, not because I had forgotten an earlier raw reading.

Earlier raw threads did not go indistinct relative to later ones because no lead-level raw reading preceded step 5. What did become diffuse was the accumulation of slice accounts; the ledger and `_lead_notes.md` were the retrieval record used to restore each boundary and data limitation.

The run was interrupted and resumed after batch 23 at a scheduler checkpoint. On resumption I reread the complete lead contract, the relevant base-contract sections, `_lead_notes.md`, the batch ledger entries, pooled codes slice by slice with their slice cases, and the forest. I did not recollect batch 23.

## Complaints

The fleet is necessarily awkward for claims about development: arbitrary slice boundaries divide exchanges, while no reader can compare every raw thread. The contract correctly weakens cross-slice relations, but that also means a richly coded pool cannot supply the kind of whole-week felt continuity a single reader might have.

The fact-file collector permits coder rewrites and leaves the lead to verify that earlier facts survived. The warnings were useful, but a mechanically append-only facts interface would make that check less error-prone.

Broad counter-reading terms such as *oficial* and *jefe* produce large amounts of ordinary political and work discussion. The snippets made screening possible, but a search result type that printed its contents directly in the Python interface would avoid the initial uninformative `<FindResult ...>` representation.
