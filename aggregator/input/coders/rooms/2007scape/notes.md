## What was hard

The hardest interpretive problem was keeping three levels apart: what people visibly did in this room, what they said happened on the canvas, and what inaccessible images, Discords, scripts, or successor sites may have contained. The bot/script dispute in `63839n` is the sharpest example: direct assertions conflict, and the supplied text cannot adjudicate the technical fact. Border talk was also difficult because “we,” “truce,” “war,” and “attack” could be one speaker's claim rather than an agreed position. I therefore retained attributed positions and did not turn contrasting comments into community change.

The other hard boundary was between ordinary proposals, actionable building, small-pixel correctness, and repair. Coordinates, sequencing, and progress made a proposal into building; restoring damage or guarding work made it repair; arguing about the `q`, period, border width, or signature pixel stayed tiny-pixel correctness even when the same item used the word “fix.”
