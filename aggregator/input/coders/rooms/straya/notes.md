## What was hard

The hardest analytic boundary was between a practice visible in r/straya and control over people changing the canvas. Speakers repeatedly called plans official while also describing “factional politics,” outsiders, stale displays, and repairs beyond their control. I treat those as accounts of limited control, not proof about every placer. Image-only titles, deleted bodies, and conflicting reports of Discord or poll decisions required the same restraint.

The two themes that nearly merged were **getting builders onto the plan** and **guarding and repairing what we made**. They stayed apart because the first aligns people around designs, priorities, messages, and venues, while the second acts on damage or threat to keep an existing object standing.

The code I would defend hardest is `c0033`, “repairing the beetle to show the roo stands for friendship.” The thinnest kept is `c0013`, “framing the pictured pixel contest as the fight of a lifetime.”

## Anomalies and leads

The strongest recurring anomaly is the coexistence of “official” plans and visible inability to stop friendly fire. The r/Parahumans relationship also moves across unusually different ordinary-language forms—enemy, truce partner, repair recipient, co-builder, defender, mate, and proposed sidebar honouree—without justifying a claim that the whole community changed its mind. ## How the reading held up

This was a resumed lead run at a scheduler checkpoint after all six slices were collected. I read the saved `out/_lead_notes.md` before consolidation and used `out/ledger.md` plus the six slice cases as the cross-slice memory.

I did not substitute memory of an unserved raw thread for corpus text. During consolidation I read `package/codes_by_thread.md` one slice at a time beside its slice case. I did not return to material because I had forgotten it; repeated reads were bounded recovery from earlier clipped tool output or the prescribed consolidation review. Earlier slices became less verbally sharp than the final slice, so exact claims were taken from pooled code lines, slice cases, `read item`, citations, and the instrument's tree index rather than recollection. The initial run was interrupted only by the scheduler's deliberate checkpoint, not by a lost corpus-reading cursor.

## Complaints

The scheduler required an interim `notes.md` at the checkpoint even though the generic contract says it is written once at the end; I recorded that conflict then and completed the same file here. A shell here-document also failed because the environment could not create its temporary file, so direct foreground Python calls were used. Facts coders repeatedly described the semantics of edited items despite the archive lacking versions; those lines had to be struck.

The branch-B design necessarily weakens cross-slice relations: no coder sees the whole week, boundaries are arbitrary, and the lead sees pooled codes and cases rather than both raw layers. I therefore marked cross-boundary relations, reported sequences without upgrading them to causes, and kept each qualification beside the claim it limits.
