## Counter-reading

My one-sentence reading is that TreePlace made a small, simple tree stand for resisting the canvas's empires and repeatedly tried to preserve or restore it, while working out its decoration and location.

The competing account was that the room was substantially another territorial fighting group and its language of simplicity only dressed up ordinary canvas conflict. If my reading were wrong, the corpus should contain participants calling for expansion, attacks, destruction, or taking others' space on the tree's behalf, and treating that fight—not keeping or restoring the tree—as their shared purpose.

The strongest contrary item is `62v6u5`: “Trees, we need your help! Combat the giant head in the purple zone with purple pixels!” It changes the account by preventing any claim that TreePlace uniformly stayed outside territorial fighting. What survives is the more limited pattern: most sustained exchanges concern keeping, restoring, locating, decorating, or remembering the tree, and the welcome text itself rejects expansion.

Apart from `62v6u5`, the hits concern preparing to restore after destruction, contrasting the tree with empires, correcting a “destroyed” typo and joking with it, or saying purple tried to keep the tree safe. They add no uncoded contrary passage. An earlier mistakenly double-escaped boundary query returned no hits; that null result tested a literal pattern and supplies no evidence. Even the corrected search's absence of calls to expand or seize space is limited by this small supplied record and cannot establish what happened outside it.
