# Case-lead notes — r/giantbomb

Gate: branch B, 1,201 in-window items and 1,195 items by people against a threshold of 500; three slices.

## What was hard

The hardest distinction was between shared work and shared attribution. The corpus plainly shows people here planning, placing, repairing, bargaining, and celebrating, but it also shows Discord decisions, outside help, and bots. I therefore describe a mixed rebuild and do not assign decisive labor to the subreddit, outsiders, or automation.

The GameSpot merger performance overlapped the canvas in clock time and occasionally leaked into canvas language, but its practice was different: it remade the subreddit as an April Fools fiction rather than making a canvas mark. Shared timing and joking vocabulary were not enough to merge them.

Retrospective A&M–UT explanations were kept as speakers’ own accounts. They clarify what those speakers say happened but do not prove a causal relation or collective decision. Likewise, reply adjacency was used only for sequence unless the later words answered, named, or recalled the earlier turn.

## Data problems

Branch B gives no reader the whole week. One coder read each slice, there is no agreement measure, boundaries are arbitrary with respect to episodes, and inherited work can straddle readers. The forest restores reply sequence but does not convert adjacency into causation. No relation in the final account crosses a slice boundary; cross-slice continuity is reported descriptively.

Discord deliberation is visible only through reports and artifacts returning to Reddit. Linked templates, images, the live canvas, bots, and scripts cannot be inspected as evidence. Removed bodies include `dfp4a94`, the opening bodies of `634paq` and `6351p8`, a cluster near the loss in `6337ke`, and one reply in `635ezr`. Parent coverage is incomplete. A duplicate “Bunch of fuccbois from U of T” reply in batch 03 was coded once, not treated as repeated practice.

The forest reports 108 uncoded stretches of forty or more characters and mean coverage of 0.75 among coded items. I inspected the forest’s sequences when making the account and did not infer content from uncoded stretches.

The Python runtime repeatedly warned that it could not create an external cache under `/tmp`; commands still completed and working state remained inside the run directory. The first attempted lead `emit.close()` used a shell heredoc and failed before Python ran because the shell could not create its temporary file; the successful foreground retry produced the sole first close call.

## Anomalies and leads

Automation is both an organizing practice and an object of rejection. This is a supported contrast among speakers, not evidence that the community changed its mind. The final slice adds no new practice theme even though it adds substantial detail about moving, rebuilding, next-design discussion, bots, and post-event accounting.

The GameSpot April Fools sequence is a substantial case practice but not r/place conduct. It remains in the record because the contract requires the room’s own April Fools conduct to be coded separately rather than dropped.

Visitors speak as Purdue, Huskers, RURURU, Aggies, or Texas-linked participants, but their words establish only their statements in this venue. Claims about their home groups were not generalized beyond explicit offers, reported agreements, or apologies.

## How the reading held up

I did not work from memory of raw threads. In branch B I did not read raw threads while the fleet ran; consolidation used the pooled codes, each intact slice case, the ledger, and the forest. I reopened individual items in step 5 to inspect search hits and ground exact quotations, not because I had forgotten a thread.

Earlier material did not become indistinct relative to later material in one reader’s memory because no lead reader held all three raw slices. That is a limitation of the fleet design, not a claim of retained recall. I reread `out/_lead_notes.md` and `out/ledger.md` after the scheduler checkpoints and before consolidation.

The run was not interrupted by a refusal, crash, or timeout. It was intentionally paused by the scheduler after each dispatch and after all collections so coder work and the lead checkpoint could be saved. The corpus cursor began at zero; no prior served reading was resumed.
