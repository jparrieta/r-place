## What was hard

The hardest distinction was between shared vocabulary and shared practice. “Commies,” “physical removal,” flags, Snek and property language recur in attacking, defending, joking, negotiating and retrospective argument; those codes were not grouped merely because the nouns matched. Attack or conversion, defense, negotiation, outside recruitment, automation and post-event interpretation remain separate themes because the participants were doing different things with the same words.

The other difficult boundary was between a participant's explanation and a causal relation. `dfpuibz` explicitly explains a personal move from attacking to building and defending, while defense threads that simply follow earlier losses support only chronology. The forest made reply structure visible, but I did not treat adjacency as causation.

The counter-reading made conquest harder to subordinate: “take over,” “conquer,” “colonize,” and “physically remove” are substantial event language. I retained attack as a major practice while rejecting the stronger competing account that it organized the whole week, because direct passages also redirect under limited manpower, respect homesteaded pixels, repair first, negotiate, and report truces.

## Data problems

Deleted bodies interrupt several selected exchanges, and image posts often leave only a title. Those gaps were not reconstructed. Discord, GitHub-hosted scripts, /pol/, pxls.space and other clone venues reveal invitations or reports returning to Reddit but not their internal decisions or placements. Several threads continue across slice boundaries or beyond the study window.

Branch B has no whole-week reader and no agreement measure: one coder read each slice, and arbitrary boundaries cut across ongoing threads. The forest restores sequence but not a single reader's memory. Cross-slice developments are therefore reported as sequences unless an item explicitly names the link.

## Anomalies and leads

The first slice contains no visible event conduct through +3.81 hours. Later practice repeatedly shrinks from ambitious conquest or redesign toward smaller flags, Snek, defense and alliances. After closure, participants move quickly from recounting survival to incompatible claims about self-organization, collectivism, admin intervention and group identity. The late physical-removal thread reuses Snek as a model for exclusion rather than returning to pixel coordination.

Human commenters describe bot use and link scripts, but actual automation is not observable. r/placesnek and the Void produce unusually explicit negotiation and non-aggression language alongside the hostile rhetoric elsewhere.

## Grouping review

I reviewed the grouping again after the counter-reading. No regrouping was made: the alternative strengthened the attack theme but did not justify merging attack with defense, negotiation, outside recruitment or late physical-removal analogy. Single-code themes were retained where a code belonged with nothing else. The two themes that nearly merged were “calling outside allies and Discord for help” and “negotiating truces, logos, and borders”; they stayed apart because recruitment seeks capacity, while negotiation works out coexistence and boundaries.

## How the reading held up

I did not work from memory of raw threads: as branch-B lead I did not read them while the fleet ran, and step 4 used the pooled codes, slice cases, ledger and forest. During counter-reading I opened returned items through `read item`; I did not go back because I had forgotten a thread. Earlier openings became less distinct as later slices arrived, while the ledger and `_lead_notes.md` preserved what each slice left open. The run was deliberately paused and resumed after the scheduler's fleet-complete checkpoint; the on-disk journal, ledger and lead notes preserved the state, and I continued with consolidation rather than recollecting or restarting.

## Complaints

The scheduler required a provisional `notes.md` at the fleet-complete checkpoint, while the contract says `case.md` and `notes.md` are written once at the end. I followed the explicit checkpoint instruction and replaced the provisional file with this complete final account. The facts-file prompt also invited coders to append descriptions of edits, but the lead rule allowed facts only; every such description required a visible strike even when the underlying edit marker was factual.

The broad counter-reading vocabulary also produced many ordinary political hits unrelated to r/place. I narrowed the search using event terms, inspected the returned snippets, and read every item used in the counter-reading rather than treating hit counts as findings.
