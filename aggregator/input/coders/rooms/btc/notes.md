## Branch-B limits

No single reader held the whole week. Fourteen fleet coders read fixed whole-thread slices, and the lead grouped their pooled codes beside their slice cases. The integrated chronology and post/comment contrasts therefore carry coder readings rather than a lead's fresh whole-thread reading. One coder per slice also means there is no measure of community agreement, and different speakers' positions cannot be treated as a change in collective opinion.

Slice boundaries are arbitrary with respect to ongoing threads. The relation in which `62vvgn` follows `62trom` by 5 hours 32 minutes crosses batches 05|06; the later post does not name the earlier one, so the case records sequence only. Other long threads continue through later slice spans even though their opening belongs to one coder. The forest makes those sequences visible but does not supply causality.

The two themes that nearly merged were “Carrying the block-size split onto the Bitcoin image” and “Judging canvas conduct through the scaling conflict”; the first changes, contests or refuses factional marks, while the second uses observed conduct to judge people or the community.

## Counter-reading search

Before search I recorded the competing account that r/place primarily enabled cross-faction cooperation and harmless fun. Its expected positive evidence was sustained shared non-factional work, successful cooperation and refusals of factional advertising. The strongest contrary item already in the case was `dfqceu5`, which says “Just Bitcoin.”

The first expression used the room's words agree, work together, Just Bitcoin, harmless fun, having fun, unite and defend, and compromise. It returned 189 items because “compromise” is pervasive in ordinary scaling talk; every hit was read with `read item`. A narrower event-proximity query returned 20 items and a final exact-vocabulary query returned eight; every returned item was read. The event-relevant results were already coded. No searched thread changed disposition and no late code was added. The search preserved a genuine early cooperative strand but did not find an additional in-window episode of sustained cross-faction success. This null is scoped to the corpus and queries, not to unseen images, deleted text or Discord.

## What was hard

The hardest distinction was between a shared Bitcoin project and a project already carrying the scaling fight. The same threads contain both: calls to defend Bitcoin, scripts for factional lettering, neighbor compromises and “Just Bitcoin.” I kept proposals, reported actions, visible responses and claimed outcomes distinct. The retrospective in `dfsafr8` supports that speaker's explanation for switching conduct, not a community-level causal claim.

The post `6362g7` and final-version post `639bfg` are especially thin. Their titles claim accomplishment but their linked images are not textual evidence, and they do not identify the contributing “we.” I nearly treated those as observed completion and did not. I also did not turn ordinary scaling uses of “compromise” returned by the counter-search into event evidence.
