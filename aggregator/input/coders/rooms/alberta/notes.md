## What was hard

The main interpretive difficulty was keeping mobilisation and agreement separate. “Done” at new coordinates and “We made some flags” support successful bounded action, while correction, refusal, basic questions and joking in the same small set of threads do not support a stable community-wide plan. The account therefore states both without turning contrasting speakers into a collective change of mind.

The Battle of Alberta material required another boundary: canvas positions visibly supplied material for hockey banter, but the corpus does not support the causal claim that r/place created that rivalry. Likewise, a participant's “marketing push” explains the sudden craze in that speaker's words but is not evidence that marketing caused it.

The local EpiPen and rickroll episodes were kept as the room's own April Fools conduct. Every code on them identifies them as not about the canvas.

As branch-B lead I did not work from memory of raw threads: I had not read them while the fleet ran. I worked from the openings, pooled codes, both slice cases, the forest, pointers and the instrument's time displays. I returned to the complete slice cases because the ledger's copied edge excerpts were clipped, not because I had forgotten the cases.

Earlier material did not become indistinct relative to later material in my own raw-thread memory because no such whole-room memory existed. The durable ledger and `_lead_notes.md` carried the batch boundary. The scheduler intentionally checkpointed the run after each dispatch and after final collection; these were planned resumptions, not a crash or API-refusal interruption. On each resumption I read the persisted lead notes and ledger and continued from the recorded stage.
