## What was hard

The hardest boundary was between attacking others' work and building this room's own mark because participants often did both in one plan. I kept them separate where the practice was destroying or taking space versus making a Tyler mark, and grouped calls for manpower, coordinates, repair, and maintenance as a third practice. It was also difficult to distinguish a participant's account of why something happened from a supported relation; the case labels explanations as explanations and uses causal wording only for explicit answers or self-reported reasons.

The fleet slices sometimes ended inside a live exchange. I carried the OSU rebuild across 01|02, the scripting and banner defence across 02|03, and the visitor conflict across 03|04. These are weaker than relations from a single reader and rest on slice cases, forest sequence, and quoted words rather than adjacency alone.
