## What was hard

The hardest interpretive problem was keeping three different claims apart: a participant saying the group was attacked, another participant explaining why a conflict began, and evidence that one act caused another. The r/CFB material contains all three without settling a single history. I treated `dfsj25u`'s “genesis of war” as that participant's explanation, `dfql52u` as a competing account, and reply order alone as sequence.

It was also difficult to separate shaping and repairing a sign from defending it. The same pixel can do both, but the practice differs: building/repairing makes letters and borders legible, while defending responds to repeated overwriting. I kept the codes separate on that basis.

The subreddit’s own vegan-themed April Fools jokes were required community conduct but analytically separate from r/place. I retained them under one concrete practice rather than using their shared date to make them evidence about the canvas.

No timeline or lexicon line was struck. Every collected addition was direct metadata or verbatim participant vocabulary with an item id. The collector repeatedly warned that batches rewrote `timeline.md` rather than appending; its merged lead copy preserved prior facts and the final file was reviewed as a factual record.

Discord is both conduct and a limit. Participants explicitly moved some organizing there and said why, but the corpus cannot show its internal decisions. Some participants also reported unusable links.

Branch B gives up a whole-week reader.
