## What was hard

The hardest distinction was between visible shared work and shared consent. Repeated repair, offers, answers, and returned plans establish cooperation, but `dfqmfd3` directly disputes who authorized the Taskbar accommodation. I therefore describe a contested plan rather than a unified change of mind.

The second difficult boundary was between practices that share Dark Souls vocabulary. The subreddit’s April Fools make-believe, canvas banter, adding new pictures, maintaining Solaire, and negotiating with neighbours all use the same familiar names, but they do different things and remain separate themes.

Removed-body event posts often allowed only title-level codes. I kept those narrow and did not reconstruct designs or instructions. The code I would defend hardest is `c0126`, “challenging who decided for the rest of the sub.” The thinnest code I kept is `c0061`, “reporting seeing this over in r/Place.”

## Data problems

No reader held the whole week. Eight coders each read one arbitrary slice, so the whole-case relations assembled from slice cases and the forest are weaker than relations observed by one reader; there is no inter-coder agreement measure. Exchanges can cross boundaries, and `[crosses NN|NN+1]` marks the cross-slice relations carried into the account.

Discord held meaningful coordination. Reddit preserves invitations, templates, returned decisions, and arguments about those decisions, but not the deliberation inside. Many event posts have deleted or removed bodies, linked images are outside the record, and the final canvas itself is not evidence available here.

The forest shows reply sequence and measured latency, not causation. Every relation in the case account also rests on what the turns say. Scores and sticky marks are retrieval snapshots, not facts about the event week.

## How the reading held up

- I did not code or adjudicate from memory of raw threads. I worked from each slice case, `codes_by_thread.md`, the forest, the ledger, and the explicitly reopened counter-reading items.
- I went back to the slice cases and forest because no single reader held the full sequence, not because I was trying to reconstruct forgotten raw text.
- Earlier slices became less sharp than the most recent collect during the multi-turn fleet run; `out/ledger.md` and `out/_lead_notes.md` were the deliberate recovery record. I reread both before consolidation and used the slice cases in order.
- The run was checkpointed and resumed repeatedly by the scheduler between batches and once after the final collection. The corpus cursor itself was not an interrupted branch-A reading; I resumed from collected journals and the lead records and did not recollect any slice.
- The complete reading therefore held at the level of recorded codes, cases, and unresolved edges, not as one person's memory of all raw threads. This is the central branch-B limitation.
