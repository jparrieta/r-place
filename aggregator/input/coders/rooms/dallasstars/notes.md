## What was hard

The most difficult interpretive boundary was between several practices that share the same canvas vocabulary. Finding a location, asking neighbours for room, trading help, and defending the resulting mark often occur in adjacent replies, but adjacency and words such as “help” or “defend” do not make them one practice. I grouped codes by what participants were doing across exchanges: locating and circulating a build, repairing spatial claims, exchanging labour or protection, and defending completed hand work.

The room’s fake Spezza trade also coincided with r/place. It was retained under the contract as the community’s own April Fools conduct, while every finding keeps it separate from the canvas. The fleet coders made this separation before consolidation.

The counter-reading required narrowing a claim that initially sounded too uniform. `dfr0fuc` reports that Stars helpers kept overwriting a neighbour’s blue blocks. The account therefore treats coexistence as an articulated norm and visible negotiation practice, not conduct followed by every participant.

## How the reading held up

As branch-B lead, I did not read raw threads during the fleet run and never worked from a remembered thread in place of served text. Before consolidation I used `openings.md` only for orientation and used `out/ledger.md` and `out/_lead_notes.md` to carry the slice edges across scheduler checkpoints. I did not return to earlier material because I had forgotten it; I read each pooled slice once in prescribed order with its slice case and notes beside it. Earlier raw threads could not go indistinct relative to later ones because no lead held them as a continuous raw reading.

The lead run paused at scheduler checkpoints after dispatch and after final collection. On resumption I read the persisted ledger and lead notes and continued from the collected state; I did not recollect or restart any slice. Both fleet coders reported clean cursor-zero starts. During the counter-reading I used `read item` on every search return. No late-coded hit required a full-thread call because its slice coder had already read that retained thread whole.

No single reader held the whole week, one coder read each slice, and there is no agreement measure. The forest recovers sequence and reply structure but cannot turn adjacency into causation. Cross-slice relations in the account are therefore limited to what later passages explicitly recall or answer.
