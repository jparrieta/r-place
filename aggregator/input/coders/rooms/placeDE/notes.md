## What was hard

The hardest distinction was between shared practice and shared vocabulary. Design proposals, Discord mentions and adjacent replies were not grouped merely because they named the same object or venue; themes were kept around what participants were doing. Relations were harder still because a forest edge establishes reply sequence, not causation. The account therefore uses a later turn only when it names, answers or recalls the earlier work, and labels closure followed by thanks as sequence.

Branch B also makes cross-slice reconstruction intrinsically weaker. No reader held the whole week. The Belgium negotiation and sausage build crosses batches 01–03 and was carried on the basis of named negotiation, the returned revised plan, an instruction saying Belgium would help, and direct completion reports. The movement from the last placement disputes into closure and retrospection crosses later slices but is treated as chronology, not as evidence that the community changed its mind.

## Anomalies and leads

The record contains both expansion slogans and arguments for an artistic high road, both claims that Discord voting was democratic and refusals to join Discord-decided work. These are competing positions, not a temporal conversion by the community. Reports that cleaners or bots erased designs are participant explanations; they do not identify the cause of every overwritten pixel. The survey and successor-canvas posts show the activity becoming an object of study and reenactment after the original canvas closed.

Visual templates are similarly underdetermined by text. The append-only facts workflow also invited coders to append interpretive sentences to files described as facts, requiring extensive visible strikes at collection.
