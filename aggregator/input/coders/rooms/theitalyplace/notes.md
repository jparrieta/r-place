## What was hard

The hardest distinction was coordinated policy versus uncontrolled action. Moderators and script maintainers announce agreements and targets, but participants also describe mindless flag repair, random pixels, attribution errors, and dissent from targets they still help execute. I treated those as limits on reach, not evidence that the common program was fictional.

The koi episode was also difficult because “the attack line won,” relocation remained proposed, and removal was later celebrated. The record supports a contested declared decision followed by removal; it does not show consensus or identify who placed every pixel. The FarLeftSide episode similarly supports participants' claims that a pact was broken and other participants' denial that an authorised pact existed, not an adjudication of the underlying breach.

Image-only posts and removed bodies often made a narrow title code possible while making the pictured object or missing argument unavailable. The heavily obscured `6366en` dispute is the clearest example: surviving replies show arguments about Rome, resistance, fascism, and contribution, but not the deleted speaker's exact case.

## Data problems

The Python runtime repeatedly warned that it could not create an xcrun cache file under `/tmp`; all commands nevertheless completed. No background process or persistent REPL was left running.

## Grouping review

Themes were opened and extended in slice order. I compared the practice performed by codes across exchanges, not shared vocabulary or reply adjacency. Later material caused recorded splits: live rallying from post-event mockery; outcome celebration from credit-giving and retrospective interpretation; general recounting from mourning; and live venue movement from post-closure invitations to continue. After the counter-reading, the late code “saying random flag protectors are not coordinated” extended attribution checking, and “using the script while calling its Vespa choice wrong” extended running the shared program. I reviewed the grouping again and made no further merge: adjacency around closure did not make thanking, mourning, joking, recounting, and inviting continuation one practice.

The themes that nearly merged were “making peace around neighbouring pictures” and “answering outsiders asking for pixels or help.” They stayed apart because the first maintains or contests standing boundaries and agreements, while the second answers a concrete incoming request by accepting, delaying, redirecting, or refusing it.

Code I would defend hardest: `running a shared pixel program` (`c0076`).

Thinnest code kept: `showing the room something found on the canvas` (`c0134`).

## How the reading held up

I never worked from memory of a raw thread. In branch B I did not read raw threads while the fleet ran; I used the ledger at every collect, then read `codes_by_thread.md` slice by slice with that slice's case, and read the full forest in bounded sections. I went back to the slice cases and specific forest nodes when composing cross-slice chronology because the evidence, not my recollection, needed to carry the relation. During the counter-reading I used `find` and then `read item` on every item used in the alternative test.

Earlier and later slices did not become indistinct in the working record because themes were founded and extended in order and the ledger preserved each edge. I would not claim unaided memory of all 357 fleet codes; the instrument files were necessary throughout. The scheduler checkpoint after batch 03 was planned, not an interrupted attempt. On resumption I reread `_lead_notes.md` and the ledger before consolidation. No API refusal, crash, or timeout interrupted the run.

## Complaints

The collect output counts section headings as added timeline “lines,” so its reported +4 and +2 can look inconsistent with the scheduler's +3 and +1 substantive facts. The empty brief is honest but leaves the requested pre-event orientation unavailable. Branch B asks the lead to assemble relations from slices the lead did not read; the required boundary marking helps, but it cannot replace a single reader's knowledge. The contract correctly restricts causal claims, and several tempting storylines therefore remain sequences only.

An earlier scheduler request asked for `notes.md` before the final checkpoint, while the contract requires it to be written once at the end. I preserved the material in the ledger and `_lead_notes.md` then and wrote it here only at the prescribed final stage.
