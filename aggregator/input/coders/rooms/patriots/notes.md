## What was hard

The hardest interpretive boundary was between building, defending, and retrospectively claiming the scoreboard. The same object appears in all three, but the practices differ: participants first make and align it, then maintain it against changes, then explain after closure whose work it was. I did not treat shared vocabulary or reply adjacency as enough to merge these practices.

Attribution was also difficult. Some passages credit Patriots participants, some other football communities, some neighbours, and one remembered defensive script. I kept these as competing or complementary participant accounts rather than resolving them into an exclusive causal story. Apparent vandalism was especially ambiguous because `dfr7k0o` and `dfr8in2` identify at least one apparent attack as the group’s own relocation.

I nearly coded more general r/place explanation and automation talk from search returns. I retained `dfrta7w` because it was an in-window, citable statement about scripts changing the human interaction and directly tested the counter-reading. I did not use the after-window context hit as evidence, and I did not turn inaccessible images or linked scripts into claims about their unseen contents.

## Data problems

Removed and deleted comments, bodyless image posts, unavailable external images and overlays, and opaque Discord conversations leave holes. Discord movement is visible conduct, but its conversation is not in the record. Some continuing-thread context crosses slice spans, so a fleet coder could see only the portion served in its slice context. Forest adjacency records reply structure and time, not causation.

No reader held the whole week. Nine independent coders each read one chronological slice, no slice had a second coder, and therefore there is no inter-reader agreement measure. Slice boundaries were imposed by item budget rather than by episode. Relations were assembled by the lead from pooled codes, slice cases, and the forest and are weaker than relations followed by one reader across the entire room. I asserted no causal relation across a slice boundary: the loss of the first logo and the later scoreboard attempt are reported only as an 8h 21m sequence, and closure followed by a final-state post only as a 6m 51s sequence.
