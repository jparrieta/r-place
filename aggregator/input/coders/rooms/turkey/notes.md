## What was hard

The hardest distinction was between a shared practice and vocabulary shared by adjacent replies. Attack, defense, alliance, flag, and heart recur widely, but they do not all do the same thing. I kept flag defense apart from bargaining with neighbours, and split the Greek–Turkish heart from ordinary bargaining because the heart codes also make a design, celebrate it, and argue over its meaning. I treated reply structure as sequence unless the later passage answered, named, or explained the earlier one.

The other difficult distinction was between an observed result and a participant account. “We won,” “Turkey and Greece achieved it,” “final fruit of our collective labor,” the one-pixel survival story, and claims of front-page success remain attributed to speakers. No image, time-lapse, external community account, or canvas state was treated as independently observed.

The room's own April Fools bokposts were retained as conduct but separated from the canvas. Codes on those passages state that distinction. Automated summaries and bot output were setting unless a participant summoned, used, updated, or discussed the tool.
