## What was hard

The hard boundary was between the room's Horizon subject swap and r/place. Both occurred on April Fools Day and sometimes appeared in the same thread, but the former changed what r/zelda pretended to be and how moderators handled posts; the latter involved a shared pixel canvas. Themes and the account keep them separate.

The second hard boundary was between a common practice and shared vocabulary. Many posts say “help,” name Zelda, or sit next to another call. I grouped them only when they did the same thing: recruiting or maintaining a picture, probing occupied space, performing the subject swap, reacting to it, or enforcing its temporary rule. Reply adjacency alone was never treated as a supported relation.

The late retrospective evidence complicated the initial “local calls” reading. r/PlaceLink and the art megathread show real gathering attempts, but neither establishes that every separate build request belonged to one campaign. The account therefore says “mostly local with late partial coordination,” not either “entirely scattered” or “centralized.”

## Anomalies and leads

The room's separate Horizon swap is unusually extensive relative to the canvas talk: it becomes a moderator-announced temporary rule, recruits ordinary posts into the joke, and generates both parody and sincere cross-game conversation. It should remain analytically distinct from r/place even where a canvas thread jokingly uses the Horizon identity.

The most consequential lead is the late claim that r/PlaceLink held coordination just before shutdown. The same retrospective says an expansion gained only a few pixels before closure. This suggests a late move toward a dedicated venue without demonstrating a sustained week-long command structure.

The live-canvas record repeatedly preserves calls without outcomes. Post-event comments are therefore disproportionately important: they supply maintenance, lost sleep, failed sprites, disputed colors, thanks, and correction of final screenshots that live recruitment posts do not report.

## Counter-reading search

Before searching, I recorded the competing account that one megathread, organizer, template, or common plan unified the separate Zelda builds. I expected repeated cross-thread references to that common plan and follow-up reports of its execution if the working reading was wrong.

I searched for megathreads, “band together,” coordination, organizers, templates, common-plan language, compiled lists, and r/PlaceLink, then read every returned item with the prescribed item reader. Most hits were ordinary game megathreads, game organization, unrelated templates, bots, or after-window discussion. The strongest contrary item was `dftb59z`, which says r/PlaceLink was where efforts began to coordinate just before shutdown. `6314o5` and `dft1bdu` also show a local gathering attempt and a late expansion plan. These were already coded; no late code or thread-disposition change was required. The search changes the reading from “scattered” to “mostly local with partial late coordination.” Its null result is limited to those queries and this partial record and cannot establish that no other coordination existed.
