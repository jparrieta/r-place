## What was hard

The hardest distinction was between building/defending the mark and sorting out mistakes/changing plans. The same pixel could be described as repair, attack, an obsolete template, or a redesign depending on what a speaker knew. I kept exchange-level corrections narrow: a participant who apologised after seeing the plan changed that local exchange, not the whole community's interpretation of every damaged pixel.

The second difficulty was separating common practice from common vocabulary. Leaves, pineapples, `420`, Mona Lisa, and Discord recur, but recurrence alone did not make a theme. Codes were grouped by what participants were doing—recruiting, teaching, choosing a mark, maintaining it, correcting collaborators, bargaining with neighbours, or moving coordination—while outcomes and positions remain varied inside each theme.

Branch B also limits synthesis. No reader held the whole week; each slice had one coder, so there is no agreement measure. Slice boundaries are arbitrary with respect to continuing exchanges, and the forest makes reply sequence visible without proving causation. Relations carried across slices were treated as sequence unless a quoted speaker explicitly named the link.
