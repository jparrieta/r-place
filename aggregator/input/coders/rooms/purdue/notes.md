## Complaints

The branch-B design necessarily asks the lead to assemble chronology and relations from slice accounts it did not originally read as raw threads. The forest and item-level counter-reading make this auditable, but they do not reproduce the interpretive continuity of one full-week reader. The facts-file instruction to retain only facts also required a manual strike when a coder phrased an edit as justification; a structured fact type would reduce that ambiguity.

The counter-reading search can return ordinary uses of words such as “attack,” “destroy,” or “take over” from network, course, housing, and after-window discussions. Reading the hits resolves that problem, but hit counts alone are meaningless and were not treated as results.

## How the reading held up

I did not work from memory of raw threads before the permitted counter-reading stage. Before consolidation I used only the briefing, openings, facts files, slice accounts, and ledger. During consolidation I read `codes_by_thread.md` one slice at a time with its slice case, then read the complete forest in bounded sections. For the account I went back to the forest, the slice accounts, `tree_index.json`, and individual items returned by `find` rather than relying on remembered wording or times.

Earlier material did not become indistinct while later material stayed sharp, or vice versa, because the pooled codes and forest remained the working record. I revisited items when exact quotations or relation claims required verification, not because I had forgotten which thread they came from. The run was intentionally checkpointed by the scheduler after each batch and after final collection; this was not an interrupted prior attempt, and the journal resumed from the saved state without retriage, recollection, or recoding.
