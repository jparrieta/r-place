## Relations — what set off what

The clearest supported relation is within the Stark-border exchange. The accusation in `dfq1fpm`—“you keep invading our stark sigil” and “full out war”—is answered directly by `dfq1hog`: “We don’t need to fight?” and the claim that the two designs align along a black border. Later, `dfq1xwf` asks what the finished design would look like and says anything inside the sigil ruins it; its direct reply, `dfq23yd`, offers existing black borders and tiles as a compromise. `dfq27vf` then explicitly accepts those borders and trades help on the two pictures. These words support a development from contested overlap to a negotiated boundary. They do not show that reply adjacency itself caused agreement.

The recoloring exchange also contains explicit answers. The post `63267c` calls to “turn this blue.” `dfqr3nq` replies “No,” supplies r/halo’s half-blue alternative, and says not to “diss the chief”; `dfqr6b4` answers “Fair point.” `dfqwrpr` separately states the ordinary rule, “Let’s not mess with other peoples art.” This is a descriptive contrast and a sequence of replies. The corpus does not identify one speaker explaining why the whole community changed its mind, so it does not support a community-level change claim.

The completion post `633xa6` says “Thanks for the help r/Halo.” That is the participant’s own explanation of who helped. It is consistent with the earlier r/halo compromise in `dfqr3nq`, but the cross-thread sequence alone does not establish that the compromise caused the completion. Likewise, the attack warning `634ua9` follows the completion post by 3h 17m 4s, but no passage links the two beyond their shared “red vs blue and halo section.”

The failed “suck it reds” effort and the Trocadero relocation precede the larger Spartan negotiation. No participant says either earlier effort set off the later one, so the record supports a sequence of separate attempts, not a causal chain.

## Posts against comments

The layers differ most where opening posts announce a desired picture and comments expose the constraints.

In `62wp2f`, the post confidently specifies a Trocadero logo, dimensions, a blue background, and coordinates. The comments say that location conflicts with a pink circle and an osu! logo (`dfpmu5n`, `dfpmuof`, `dfpmxpd`), then redirect the project and recommend asking Blue Corner permission (`dfpn9t1`). The post says where the logo should go; the comment layer says the proposed space is already socially occupied.

In `62x1oi`, the post only asks for help with an externally linked image. The comments carry almost the entire visible practice: being overwritten (`dfpu041`), choosing an order of work (`dfpu63m`, `dfpvlm8`), being accused of invasion (`dfpzwrn`, `dfq1fpm`), and settling a shared border with mutual help (`dfq23yd`, `dfq27vf`). The image design lives in the post’s link and is not available as citable body text; the bargaining lives in comments.

In `63267c`, the post calls for turning an area blue, while the comments reject an unrestricted recoloring. They substitute a half-blue compromise (`dfqr3nq`, `dfqr6b4`) and the rule not to mess with other people’s art (`dfqwrpr`). This is an explicit post/comment contradiction, not evidence that the entire community held either view.

The layers align in `633xa6`: the post claims completion and thanks r/Halo, while `dfskva1` calls the result a good compromise. `634ua9` has no comment layer, so the identity of the attacker and any defense exist only as an unsupported warning in the post.

## Counter-reading

My one-sentence reading is: r/redvsblue’s visible r/place conduct consisted of several small efforts to put recognizable words and pictures on a crowded canvas, with sustained bargaining over space, shared borders, reciprocal help, and a half-blue compromise rather than a sustained campaign to conquer neighboring work.

If that reading were wrong, the supplied record should instead contain additional calls by the RvB organizers to raid, invade, overwrite, destroy, or refuse negotiated borders, with compromise language functioning only as cover for that aggressive practice.

The strongest contrary item is `dfq1fpm`: a Stark-sigil defender says “you keep invading,” invokes allies, and threatens “full out war.” It requires the reading to acknowledge actual contested overlap and another builder’s experience of the RvB image as an invasion. It does not overturn the reading because `dfq1hog` answers that they do not need to fight, `dfq23yd` offers a shared-border compromise, and `dfq27vf` accepts it with reciprocal help.

## Saturation

The practice account is not thematically saturated in a strong sense. The retained threads are few, and genuinely different conduct continues late: attempts and relocation are followed by extended border bargaining, then celebration and credit, and the last event thread adds a single defensive warning. The single-code warning therefore remains separate rather than being enlarged to match a target theme size.

The textual evidence is sufficient to establish visible recruiting, failed placement, border negotiation, compromise, celebration, and warning inside r/redvsblue, but not to reconstruct the pictures or the off-venue work. Several event posts are image or link posts; `63267c` has a deleted body, while its title and replies survive. The record points to r/BlueCorner, r/TheBlueCorner, r/osugame, r/Halo, r/place, and a pixel-art design site, but it does not contain those venues’ deliberations. The completion post’s thanks shows help returning as public credit; it does not show how that help was organized. No thread says that organizing moved wholesale elsewhere.
