# Notes

## What was hard

The hardest distinction was between making or refining the picture and defending it. Repair guides do both, but I grouped them with templates when their primary practice was making a mutually legible target; direct calls to save, preserve, erase hostile text, or reclaim ground stayed with defense.

The template discussion did not yield a single collective position. Richer colour, exact palette, maintainability, and ease of defense coexist. I treated that as a supported difference between speakers, not a sequence in which the community changed its mind.

Many posts are images or links whose titles survive without textual descriptions. I coded only the action in the title and did not infer what the image showed. Deleted bodies were handled the same way: a surviving title was citable, but missing content was not reconstructed.

## Data problems

The briefing is a harness-generated empty brief because the supplied pre-week archive has no items. It supports no claim about ordinary vernacular or established roles beyond that limited archive.

There are 29 deleted or removed bodies. Discord is repeatedly named as an organising venue, but its live discussion is absent. The record also begins at d1 04-02 00:45, more than a day after the canvas opened, so the project's founding is visible only through participants' later references.

## Anomalies and leads

The counter-reading found both off-platform authority (“Discord members have spoken”) and continued on-platform usability: one participant unable to join Discord still rebuilt from the posted plot. The combination is more informative than either item alone.

One thread says the Void subreddit changed targets after internal conflict. This is a participant's explanation and may be a lead for a different case, but it is not independently established here.

The closing squid exchange suggests an earlier obligation between StarryKnights and another group. The corpus shows the retrospective accounting, not the original bargain in full.

## Complaints

The archive makes image-centred participation unusually thin: many highly consequential posts offer only a title and external URL. The contract correctly prevents visual inference, but this means the account can establish that participants showed or used a template without establishing its contents.

The pre-week briefing is empty, so the requested comparison with ordinary community life cannot sensibly be made for this case.

## Grouping review

I reviewed the grouping after reading the forest and again after the counter-reading. Repair instructions moved out of direct defense and into settling a workable picture; ally help and border bargaining were joined as reciprocal accommodation; Discord remained separate as venue access and governance. The counter-reading extended the template and Discord themes without requiring a new theme.

The two themes that nearly merged were “settling a workable picture and repair guide” and “rallying to save and rebuild against the Void.” What kept them apart was the practice: agreeing what pixels should be there versus mobilising people to keep or restore them under attack.

## How the reading held up

I did not work from memory of a thread instead of what `read` served. I did not reopen a thread because I could not remember it; expansions were made when the triage window required the full thread, and counter-search hits were opened as required. Earlier threads did not become indistinct relative to later ones: the openings file provided the week-level shape, while the chronological windows and forest preserved the disagreements and exchanges. The run was not interrupted or resumed; the initial cursor was zero.

The most difficult point for holding detail was the dense cluster from `6315ic` through `631wdk`, where attack, trolling, templates, repair and outside help overlap. I used the full-thread readings and forest rather than recollection when separating those practices.
