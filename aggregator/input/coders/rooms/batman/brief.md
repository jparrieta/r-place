## Recurring formats

The clearest weekly ritual is the “Weekly Batman Comics and TV Thread.” It divides talk into designated subthreads and relaxes spoiler tagging inside each book’s own subthread.

> Please limit comments to the designated discussion subthreads. We don't want loose comments cluttering up the thread. (`61tbfd`)

> Spoiler tags are unneccessary within a book's own subthread. (`61tbfd`)

The “Character of the Month” vote is explicitly monthly: nominations run during the final week, in contest mode, for a week-long voting period.

> For the last week of each month we'll have a thread like this one, where people can nominate their favorite character. (`61hmu6`)

Within the opened sample, two common one-off shapes were personal artwork showcases and requests for reading recommendations. They appeared repeatedly, but the posts did not present themselves as scheduled rituals.

## Register

Artwork threads are usually enthusiastic and informal. Praise can be brief and slangy, while criticism can be technically specific; artists may accept the suggestion directly.

> This looks like you're done with it, but I might suggest taking another pass on the lighting to catch Batman's legs (knees) up to Joker's top-lighting. (`dfke4gb`)

> Thanks, that's a good suggestion. I'll go back and give it a tweak. (`dfke8wg`)

A self-described “noob” received direct recommendations and sympathy about the cost of comics rather than ribbing.

> Which one of these would you recommend to read next for a noob? (`61mi64`)

> I got you on the cash part, comics are expensive (`dffstpm`)

Disagreement is ordinary and unevenly tempered. Some users explicitly soften it, some correct themselves, and some turn openly profane when a long film argument sours.
