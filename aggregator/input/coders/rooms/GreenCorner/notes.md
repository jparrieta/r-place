# Notes — r/GreenCorner

## What was hard

The hardest distinction was between Green participants' expansive war language and their repeated willingness to spare named art or honour negotiated borders. I kept both rather than treating the latter as a general pacifist position. The “green master race” and “inferior colors” wording was coded as the speakers' factional defense language, without importing an explanation of intent that the text does not supply.

Attribution was also difficult where hostile Blue posts, deleted authors, and visiting diplomats spoke inside the room. I treated those posts as conduct around this community because they changed what the room was being asked or challenged to answer, while keeping their authorship visible.

The empty briefing provided no supported vernacular or pre-event continuity. It was orientation only and did not support a code or relation.

## Data problems

Twenty-seven items have deleted or removed text, including nineteen post bodies whose titles survive. I coded only surviving titles and replies. Link-post images were not inspected; codes on those posts rest on their titles. Discord and Strawpoll content is not present, so claims about off-platform decisions stop at what returned to Reddit.

The corpus records edit flags but not edit times. Consequently, the poll result appended to `62vokr` cannot be timed as a deliberation interval; the account reports only the 58-minute opening-to-opening sequence from `62vokr` to `62w1f5` and relies on the latter's words “After the poll” for the relation.

## Complaints

The briefing is necessarily unhelpful because the supplied pre-week archive is empty. The instrument otherwise supplied the needed counts, exact citations, forest timings, and complete reply linkage. The distinction between a triage window that happens to contain the whole short thread and an explicitly opened long thread is clear in the reading tool but can look different in aggregate close output; I followed the rule to call `thread` whenever a displayed triage window touched the event.

## Grouping review

I initially grouped defending and expanding Green together, then split them because holding or recovering an existing home is a different practice from allocating pixels to new territory or forms. I also split distrust of Red from alliance-making because those passages use remembered destruction to contest the alliance. After writing the within-case account and counter-reading, I reviewed all ten themes again; no further regrouping was needed. The late code `c0111` extends “making room for neighbours, names, and art” because it reports violations of the memorial bargain while policing those violations.

The two themes that nearly merged were “defending, repairing, and retaking the corner” and “facing loss by retreating, trying again, and remembering”; I kept them apart because the former directs pixels toward holding a home, while the latter deals with a home participants already describe as lost.

## How the reading held up

I did not work from memory of a thread instead of what `read` served. I did not go back because I had forgotten a thread; explicit full-thread calls were made only where the triage window required them, and later `read item` calls followed the counter-search hits. Earlier threads did not become indistinct relative to later ones: the openings overview and chronological batches kept the sequence legible, and the generated forest was read before the account. This run was not interrupted or resumed; the initial cursor was 0.

## Branch and method limits

Branch A was selected by the recorded gate: 243 in-window items against a threshold of 500, with 27,868 characters by people. One reader held the whole room, so there is no inter-coder agreement measure. Reply adjacency in the forest was used only to establish reply structure and measured latency; relations in the account additionally require the speakers' words.
