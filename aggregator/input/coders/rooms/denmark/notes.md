## What was hard

The hardest judgement was separating organised restraint from community conduct that organisers described as rogue. The record contains official plans, neighbour agreements and repeated attempts to stop expansion, but also inconsistent plan images, admitted lack of control, uncompleted rebuilding and a moderator's celebratory ownership of the final attack. I therefore preserve the distinction between formal organisers and uncontrolled placers without using it to settle collective responsibility.

The second difficulty was keeping different forms of pixel work apart. Making and correcting Dannebrog, defending and retaking it, overwriting rivals, and negotiating room for neighbours often occur in the same threads and share vocabulary. They remain separate because they perform different practices: altering a design, answering damage, directing expansion, and making or invoking an accommodation. War language was coded as mobilisation or retelling only where speakers actually used it that way, not as a gloss on every conflict.

The linked canvas images were not readable evidence. I nearly treated participant descriptions of Kaj, Andrea, ships, borders and final images as visual facts, but retained them only as attributed reports. Likewise, the reported Discord army sizes and decisions are speaker accounts, not independent measurements.
