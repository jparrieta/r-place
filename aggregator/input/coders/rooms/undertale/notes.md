## What was hard

The hardest interpretive boundary was between live negotiation and the later story of alliance. The same actors and names recur, especially Ireland and Temmie, but asking for a truce, offering help, failing to communicate an agreement, thanking an ally, and retrospectively calling Temmie a mascot do different things. I kept those practices separate and attributed every explanation to its speaker.

The other difficult boundary was temporal. Batch 01 owns the early sticky as a thread even though its replies run through the event's close, while later batches own threads opened during those replies. I treated slice order as thread-opening order, used item times for chronology, and did not infer causation from the forest or adjacency. Pxls is an explicit successor to r/place rather than another phase of the original canvas.
