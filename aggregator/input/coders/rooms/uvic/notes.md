## What was hard

The hardest distinction was between reciprocal help and rivalry because both occur in the same small set of exchanges. I kept the account descriptive: direct offers, repairs, thanks, and “stick together” support the help practice; r/UofT replacement warnings and later “karma” support rivalry. The participant's own sentence about early conflict and later cooperation is reported as that participant's explanation, not as proof that the community changed its mind.

Image and link posts were another limit. Their surviving titles are citable conduct, but the linked designs are not evidence I can inspect. I coded proposals and celebrations from their words, not the unseen pixels.

## Grouping review

I reviewed the provisional grouping across threads and again against the drafted account. No regrouping was needed. The instrument's `regrouped_from` field records that review on each theme. Shared words such as “help” did not determine grouping: helping neighbouring schools stayed separate from rallying defence because the former enacts reciprocal cross-school assistance while the latter mobilises people around threats to UVic.

The two themes that nearly merged were “Rallying people to defend the UVic logo” and “Helping west coast schools stick together.” What kept them apart was the practice: sounding alarms and locating UVic's mark mobilised defence, while offers, repairs, thanks, and assisting UBC or SFU enacted reciprocity.

The code I would defend hardest is “switching to the majority's medium-blue plan.” The thinnest code I kept is “complaining that UVic does not get pride.”
