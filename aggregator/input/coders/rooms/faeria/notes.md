# Notes — r/faeria

## Gate and fleet limits

The fixed gate selected branch B on 884 in-window items against the threshold of 500, with 186,489 participant-written characters. Two coders each read one slice, so no reader held the whole week and there is no agreement measure. Slice boundaries follow whole threads rather than episodes: batch 01's retained thread continued while batch 02's retained update opened. I carried continuity across the 01|02 boundary only where the later post says it is trying to “bring back the Yak”; other cross-thread ordering is reported as sequence, not cause.

At batch 02 collection, no fact lines were struck. Both new timeline lines report edited flags with item ids, and `RIP Yak` is an unglossed lexicon term with its first-use item id. The collector warned that batch 02 rewrote rather than appended `timeline.md`; review showed all ten batch-01 facts preserved and the two new facts inserted chronologically.

## What was hard

The hardest issue was chronology. The edited opening `62vie8` compresses completion, several losses, relocations, negotiation, and ending into one post, but the record supplies no edit timestamps. I therefore used dated comments and the forest for measured order, and treated the opening's updates as the participant's retrospective sequence. The second difficulty was distinguishing attack from overlap: the posts use “vandalous hordes,” “murdered,” and revenge, while comments say the orange overwrite was another project, request truces, and ask about cooperation. I retained both positions rather than turning them into a claim that the community changed its mind.

The strongest contrary passage, `dft2hwp`, says one builder “just gave up” after suspected bots erased a half-built Yak. I treated the bots only as that participant's attribution. The passage narrows persistence to a collective pattern and does not support a causal claim that bots ended the wider effort.

## Data problems

Deleted bodies were not reconstructed. Linked images show up only as links and participant descriptions; their visual contents were not treated as evidence. Discord is identified as a negotiations venue, but its conversation is unavailable. Edited flags have no edit times. These gaps especially limit claims about the route to “safe pasture” and the precise state of the final remnant.

## Anomalies and leads

The Yak effort repeatedly turned loss into relocation, truce, a new target, or a joke. The post/comment contrast around r/drugs is particularly useful: the post calls for vengeance while comments ask for relocation, possible help, and awake builders. The raw names `/r/avoarmy` and `R/avocado` were left distinct; the record does not establish that they are the same community.

## Grouping review

I reviewed the six-theme grouping again after the counter-reading and did not regroup it. The three step-5 codes extended “making truces and moving the Yak for neighbors” through `emit.theme_add`. “Building, repairing, and rebuilding the Yak together” nearly merged with “keeping pixel work on one picture and coordinates”; they stayed apart because the first recruits labor and performs construction, while the second aligns distributed work to a shared plan. “Fighting, mourning, and joking through lost Yaks” also nearly merged with relocation and truces; it stayed apart because loss responses sometimes remain warning, grief, defense, or memorial rather than becoming negotiation.

## How the reading held up

As case lead in branch B, I did not read raw threads while the fleet ran and did not work from memory of them. At consolidation I read each slice's case beside its codes in order; I did not return to either because I had forgotten it, and batch 01 did not become indistinct while I read batch 02. The scheduler checkpoint after collecting batch 02 was not an interrupted corpus reading: on resumption I reread `out/ledger.md` and `out/_lead_notes.md`, then continued with consolidation. Both slice coders separately reported that they were not interrupted, did not work from memory, and did not find earlier threads becoming indistinct.

## Complaints

Whole-thread slicing makes time overlap across batches even though the slice labels look sequential; without the forest this would invite a false chronology. Edited openings also present a finished narrative without edit times. The scheduler required a preliminary `notes.md` before its checkpoint, while the contract says `case.md` and `notes.md` are written once at the end. I followed the scheduler's explicit checkpoint instruction and replaced that preliminary checkpoint note with this final notes file; this is the only procedural conflict I encountered.
