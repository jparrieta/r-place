## What was hard

The hardest analytical boundary was between practices that shared communist or parrot vocabulary but did different things. Building a slogan, objecting to a political change, explaining its r/place origin, negotiating canvas borders, and enforcing a local rule could not be merged just because they were adjacent or used “comrade.” The final themes separate the five-upvote deal, communist-parrot joke-making, the dispute over joke versus politics, reciprocal alliance work, and enforcement of a specific name-calling rule.

The other hard boundary was event scope. The large front-page thread is the room's own April Fools conduct rather than canvas work, except for a small explicit r/place branch. It remains coded but distinct. The transport-truncated rendering of that thread made the boundary harder because its unseen middle could not be assessed.

The counter-reading required separating descriptive contrast from change. “Unsubbing,” “no politics,” wanting the room “back to normal,” collaborative slogan-building, and a moderator's r/place explanation are simultaneous positions from different speakers. They support a contested local turn, not a community that changed its mind.

## How the reading held up

The case lead did not read raw threads while the fleet ran and therefore did not work from memory of them. Consolidation used `package/codes_by_thread.md`, the two slice cases, the forest, the facts files, and the ledger in prescribed slice order. I did not return to a thread because I could not remember it. Earlier material remained available through the ledger and ordered code file rather than unaided memory; neither slice became noticeably more indistinct than the other during consolidation.

The run began fresh at cursor 0. It was deliberately checkpointed by the scheduler after batch 02 collection and resumed for consolidation, rather than interrupted by a crash or refusal. On resumption I used the saved ledger and `_lead_notes.md` to pick up the reading without recollecting or redispatching anything.

Raw items were first opened only after step 4, for the counter-reading. Every returned match was inspected with `read item`; some were opened more than once because a later, narrower query returned an item already read, not because it had been forgotten. No atlas, retrospective, outside site, linked image, or other community account was consulted.
