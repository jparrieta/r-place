## How the reading held up

This lead run paused twice for scheduler-managed fleet checkpoints and resumed from the on-disk journal, ledger and `_lead_notes.md`; it did not restart or recollect a completed batch. I did not substitute memory of raw threads for served material. Before consolidation I used the ledger and running notes to preserve slice edges, anomalies and facts-file reviews. During step 4 I read pooled codes in slice order with the relevant slice case beside them. Earlier material did not become indistinct relative to later material because claims were returned to these records and the forest rather than reconstructed from memory.

No single reader holds the whole raw week: each slice had one coder, slice boundaries are arbitrary with respect to practice, and no agreement statistic exists. The forest restores sequence but not a shared firsthand reading. Replies spanning the boundary were therefore treated conservatively, and only instrument-measured times or coder-supplied times appear in the account.
