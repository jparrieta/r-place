## Grouping review

After drafting the within-case account I reviewed every theme grouping again. I made no regrouping: the account sharpened qualifications but did not change which practice held any code group together. *Making borders, pacts, and deals with neighbors* and *assigning orange space to displaced groups* nearly merged. ## What was hard

The hardest distinction was between a shared practice and adjacent disagreement. The Yeti thread contains removal scripts, diplomatic objections, evidence, a template, attempted restoration, and later blame; I treated these as a concrete practice of fighting over whether the Yeti stays, not as one consensus and not as a causal chain merely because the comments reply to one another. Likewise, territorial allocation and repair language can sound generous while “uitlenen (niet weggeven!)” retains ownership; the case states both rather than converting them into either pure restitution or pure conquest.

The post/comment contrast also required care. Different speakers praise colonization, defend preservation, or call Place a shared drawing activity. Those are concurrent positions. Nothing establishes that the community changed its collective mind.

The query for “iedereen” or “allemaal” followed by following, listening, keeping to, or working with the plan returned no items. That null applies only to the queried Dutch wording and a partial record; it cannot prove universal noncompliance. The result narrowed the reading to partial, locally successful coordination without overturning the later evidence of erased houses, absent permissions, and incompatible Yeti actions.
