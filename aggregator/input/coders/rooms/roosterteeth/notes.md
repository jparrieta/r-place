## What was hard

The hardest boundary was separating a shared practice from shared vocabulary. Many passages say “help,” “logo,” or “defend,” but recruiting, supplying a plan, placing pixels, repairing damage, negotiating with neighbours, and later recounting the work are different doings. The themes keep those practices separate even when they occur in one exchange.

It was also difficult to distinguish reported outcomes from outcomes visible in the record. Claims that Romania covered a site, the void ate a rooster, alternate accounts attacked, an account was deleted, or a logo survived remain attributed participant reports. Linked images were not treated as observed results.

## Data problems

No reader held the whole week. Eight coders each read one slice, so cross-boundary relations are assembled from cases, explicit links, and the forest and are weaker than a single-reader account. There is no intercoder agreement measure. Several episodes cross arbitrary slice boundaries.

Deleted and removed bodies disproportionately affect short help calls, vandalism reports, and joke proposals. The main event threads were read in full without apparent truncation, but external images, Discord, private messages, and other subreddits’ discussions are not visible. A failed shell heredoc occurred because the environment could not create its temporary file; the same first close was then run successfully with a foreground `python3 -c` call, and no emit operation was duplicated.

The facts files mix H+ and d-marker time formats and append batch sections that are not globally sorted. Final times and intervals therefore came from `package/tree_index.json` or the instrument’s item output, not from visual ordering in `timeline.md`.

The slice cases occasionally describe a post as “setting off” replies where the evidence is only reply position. The final account retained a relation only when answering words, a named earlier event, or the same speaker’s explanation supported it.
