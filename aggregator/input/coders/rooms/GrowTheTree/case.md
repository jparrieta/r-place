## Chronology

The record begins late in the canvas event, at `d1 04-01 21:05`, with people already proposing growth. In the first episode, from `62vhhm` to `62wgvv`, participants offered “rough ideas for expansion,” treated the tree as something that would “adapt and grow,” recruited “arborists,” and changed black pixels to brown or purple so the void would have “no footholds.” This was both design and defense: the tree's flexible form supplied ordinary language for changing direction without abandoning it.

The second episode, from `62wku9` to `62xm2g`, made neighboring projects part of the work. An IUP participant arrived “to make a deal” at `62wku9`; GrowTheTree members answered with mock-ups and accepted a combined sapling and school mark. Participants separately cited permission from an r/ainbow moderator at `62x4q8`. When the American flag cut through the tree, `62x950` called “All hands on deck!” and `62xm2g` announced an agreement to give the flag room in return for repair. The comment layer immediately qualified that announcement: `dfpuji1` said “the treaty failed to pass the Senate.”

The third episode, from `62xsln` to `631du1`, followed the old tree's loss with a new sapling, repeated mock-ups, and arrangements for growing around a butterfly, Rainbow Road, Straya, and nearby art. `62xsnb` compressed the turn: “The American Flag has consumed us. Don't worry! Lets build up that sapling into our new tree.” By `631du1`, the practice had become a cleanup plan covering foliage, roots, neighbors' sky, and later reapplication of apples and the butterfly. This is development in the work, not evidence that every participant adopted one design; `631cyh` and its replies continued to differ over natural foliage versus a defensive outline.

The fourth episode, from `631lwh` to `637fs1`, centered on repeated repair and keeping a recognizable face while flag pixels, random marks, suspected bots, and Pepe designs entered the tree. `635fmd` told participants to “Kill all red pixels” to stop a recognizable Pepe mouth. At `636eq5`, people considered cherry blossoms, autumn colours, solid colours, and outlines, but later comments repeatedly postponed redesign in favor of making it “look like a tree again.” The last pre-close update at `637fs1` prioritized the face, removing pixel noise, friendly contact, and staying inside the black outline.

The final episode, from `6383c4` to `63a962`, begins four minutes after placement ended at `d3 04-03 17:04`. Participants thanked one another, described relief and exhaustion, remembered apples, imagined a more finished tree, and celebrated survival. `dfs12dw` called it “close to a finished tree” and was glad “our little tree” survived; later, `dfsvibk` jokingly claimed one green pixel. These are retrospective accounts of the event, not additional placement.

## Relations — what set off what

The clearest supported response is rebuilding after destruction: `62xsnb` names both ends in one passage—“The American Flag has consumed us” and “Lets build up that sapling into our new tree.” This supports a response relation, not merely a sequence.

A second relation is participants' own explanation for modifying the design after Pepe attacks. At `d2 04-03 12:48`, `dfrnpoh` rejected apples because “they can be used to make the red of the pepe” and proposed lighter green and yellow instead. This supports that speaker's reason for a colour proposal; it does not establish that the community implemented that design. Similarly, `dfrqy10` said they were fixing the face “so more people maybe hestitate before trying to hurt the cute tree.” The later retrospective `dfshwxg` said a face and borders “increased the number of people willing to lay off the tree,” but the record does not independently identify those people or establish the claimed effect, so this remains participant explanation rather than a demonstrated causal relation.

Negotiation and uncontrolled placement repeatedly coexisted. `dfqlmbn` called the border rule the “current official understanding” and labelled contradictory expansion “unsanctioned,” while `dfqn5mh` said the flag “still won't stop trying to expand.” The supported finding is a descriptive contrast between stated agreements and visible reports of continued encroachment. The corpus does not show that an agreement caused the encroachment to stop.

Near the end, `dfsptfg` reported that suspected bot users seemed to give up shortly before a “resurgence of people trying to rebuild the tree.” The passage supports an observed and interpreted sequence, but its “I think” and “seemed” do not support a firm causal claim.

## Posts against comments

The posts often present crisp calls, agreements, and priorities; comments show their contested execution. The post `62xm2g` says “We hereby have an agreement with the American Flag,” while comment `dfpuji1` says “the treaty failed.” The post `6378z8` presents an offer from r/AmericanFlaginPlace to rebuild; comments `dfrv0an` and `dfruzs7` say other members were still pushing through the tree and “mindlessly expand[ing].” These are contradictions between official or post-level formulations and comment-level reports, not proof that either side alone describes every placer.

Design differences also live mainly in comments. The post `631cyh` says “Grow the leaves, grow the forest, not outline,” while `dfr1p5j` argues a border is needed against encroachment and the original poster replies at `dfqjxox`, “I won't block any attempts to outline.” This shows a participant revising their position inside one exchange; it does not establish that the whole community changed its mind.

Conversely, the celebratory ending appears in both layers. The post `6383c4` says “The tree survives,” and comments `dfs0lfc` and `dfs12dw` qualify the success as smaller than its height and only “close to a finished tree.” The comments preserve cost and incompleteness without reversing the post's account of survival.

## Counter-reading

My reading before search was: r/GrowTheTree kept rebuilding a flexible tree by pairing hands-on repair and redesign with repeated deals for room, borders, and help from larger neighbors.

The competing account was that the deals were mostly talk that did not organize what happened. If my reading were wrong, the record should contain people rejecting negotiated coordination, reporting that agreements did not hold, and describing unilateral neighbor expansion as decisive despite formal help.

I searched the complete record for the case's own contrary vocabulary: “treaty failed,” “hivemind,” “cannot control,” “actively trying,” “mindlessly expand,” and “not coordinated.” The search returned 13 items, and I read each returned item through the corpus tool. The strongest contrary item is `dfruzs7`: “while some of them want to help, the rest of them just want to mindlessly expand for no reason.” `dfqyis2` is nearly as consequential because it says a Discord understanding cannot govern a “hivemind” that ignores both Discord and the main page. These findings change the reading by making coordination explicitly partial: agreements organized named contacts and some helpers, but did not control all pixel placers. The central reading survives in narrower form because contrary items such as `dfql1rj`, `dfriuuz`, and `dfrux7m` still describe cooperation, rebuilding help, and seeking help precisely because this small group could not win alone.

## Saturation

The complete in-window record was read in full. It contains 16 deleted or removed bodies, but surviving titles and replies still expose the main practices: growing and redesigning, defending and repairing, arranging space with neighbors, recruiting growers, and making an ending account. The single unrelated property advertisement does not bear on those practices.

The record is thematically repetitive by its later stretch: later threads return to recognizable faces, borders, repair, friendly contact, and survival rather than opening a wholly new practice. The counter-reading added two late codes about the limits of neighbor coordination; they qualified the cooperation theme but did not require a new theme. Off-platform Discord talk is only partial evidence: the record shows that people moved some negotiation there and brought claimed terms back, but not who participated, how decisions were made, or whether unaffiliated placers followed them.

In several cases the surviving replies preserve what people were doing around the gap—for example, `634c3p` retains the question about placing the subreddit name and `dfrcgv8` answers that it would help people find the subreddit.
