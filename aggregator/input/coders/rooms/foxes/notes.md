# Notes

## What was hard

The hardest boundary was between the event’s tools and its collective practice. Scripts and Discord were plainly consequential, but participants also described manual placement, in-thread template correction, negotiation and defence. I kept automation as a means people debated and used, rather than treating it as the author of the fox.

The aftermath screenshot was also difficult. Participants called it legitimate, but comments raised possible sync problems. I preserved that as uncertainty between the post and comment layers rather than deciding whether the image represented the shared final canvas.

The first shell attempt to record `step1_brief` used a here-document and was refused because the environment could not create its temporary file. The briefing and openings were consequently displayed before the successful `emit.stage("step1_brief")` call. No case evidence was coded from that display, and all later stages were announced before their work.

## Data problems

The header reports nine deleted or removed item bodies and one reply whose parent is absent. The deleted opening body of `62uype` leaves only its title. Two Discord invite strings, external templates and a Pastebin script expose only what participants brought back into Reddit. The corpus cannot show the content or membership of those venues.

## Complaints

The append-only theme instrument correctly refused an overlapping code, but a failed consolidation call can leave earlier themes from that same call live. I resumed from the accepted themes and recorded the overlap in `_lead_notes.md`. A transaction or dry-run mode for a whole theme batch would make this easier to audit.

## Theme boundaries and grouping review

“Making a template everyone can follow” and “mixing hand-placed pixels with scripts” nearly merged. They stayed apart because a template specifies what the shared image should be, whereas scripts and hands are competing or complementary means of placing it.

Every initial grouping was reviewed while consolidating. One attempted overlap (`c0011`) was refused and removed from the later group; the early recruitment assignment was retained. The counter-reading produced four codes, all added to existing themes, so it required no further regrouping. Theme labels remain gerund-led and practice-based; shared words such as “fox,” “help” and “Discord” were not sufficient grounds for grouping.

## Relations caution

The forest records reply structure and elapsed time, not causation. Relations in the case account use passages that answer, name or explain the earlier action. Other orderings, including the header following the aftermath, are stated only as sequences.

## How the reading held up

I did not work from memory of a thread in place of what `read` served. I did not return to an item because I had forgotten it; the item re-reads were the required inspection of all 17 counter-search hits. Earlier threads remained distinguishable from later ones because the corpus was small enough to read as one ordered case, and I used the generated forest for sequence checks. The run was not interrupted or resumed.

## Gate and scope

Branch A was fixed by the instrument at 314 in-window items against a threshold of 500, with 29,872 characters by people. The openings file was used only for orientation. Ordinary fox content was treated as context or excluded; the community’s r/place work and its post-event header adoption formed the case.
