## Counter-reading

My reading is that PinkUnion repeatedly tried to keep a distinct pink home while flexibly negotiating allies and replacement territory, and conflict sharpened when cooperation made Pink seem like somebody else's backdrop.

If that reading were wrong, I expected participants to welcome abandoning a distinct Pink identity or being absorbed into an ally's logo as the desired end, rather than treating movement and alliance as ways to keep Pink going.

I searched the complete record for the case's own contrary vocabulary: unify, together, join forces, a sea of pink and purple, background/backdrop, identity, servitude, abandon, give up, and surrender. I opened all eight returned items through the reader. The strongest contrary item is `62raie`: it proposes retreat, joining PurplePixels, and “a sea of pink and purple patterns.” This changes the account by showing that at least one participant welcomed a mixed territorial pattern, not merely a defensive pact. The same item says participants did not have to place purple, while `dfpe63f` accepts unification in order to keep “spreading of pink,” so the core reading survives in qualified form. The strongest opposing passages later insist Pink is “more than just your back drop” (`dfr6du7`) and that defending another group's background can be “servitude” (`dfrc0jd`).
