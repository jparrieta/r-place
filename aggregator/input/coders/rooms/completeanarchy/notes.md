## What was hard

The hardest distinction was between visible Reddit organising and claims about the canvas outcome. Participants say they placed pixels, call for help, and report repairs, but images are not text and some purported changes were local-only or automated. The account therefore treats recruitment, instructions and self-reports as conduct while attributing outcomes to the speakers who claimed them.

“Left unity” also required care. It is vivid participant language and recurs in celebration, but contrasting speakers dispute affiliations and invoke Kronstadt. I retained both without converting contrast into a community-level change of mind. Likewise, a direct reply was never enough for a causal relation; the later text also had to answer, name or explain the earlier passage.

The briefing described ordinary-week language but remained orientation. Its unsupported generalizations were not used as evidence. Openings were read before the gate as required and used only to locate the week's shape.

## Anomalies and leads

The campaign recruits older room language—BASH THE FASH, Spook, bread, Aku and left-unity jokes—without making every use the same practice. The same designs are repeatedly renamed after changes, and victory posts coexist with renewed calls for defence. The post-shutdown record supplies competing accounts of cooperative Free Territory and spectacle rather than one settled retrospective.

The strongest lead is the explicit “psyop” claim: one participant says disclosure was intended to sow distrust of every script. It is corroborated only in the weaker sense that other retrospective comments discuss hostile scripts being made to repair a hammer and sickle; the targets' reactions are not visible.
