## What was hard

The hardest distinction was between building a shared image, changing its intended details, and repairing unwanted changes. The same face or pixel could appear in all three practices, so grouping rested on what the speaker was doing: choosing the design, implementing it, or answering interference. I also kept the subreddit’s fake-release April Fools posts separate from canvas conduct even though they occurred during the same dates.

Attribution was difficult where speakers reported work done in Discord, r/gorillazplace, or by neighbouring groups. Those statements support reports and venue movement, not direct observation of the outside conversation. Image posts likewise support their surviving titles but not an independent description of pixels.

The counter-reading required separating genuine contrary voices from quoted claims that commenters immediately rebutted. “Nobody will protect” appeared inside replies that then reported protection; the strongest contrary item was instead the unqualified claim that a 26,000-person subreddit was not defending.

## Data problems

## How the reading held up

I did not work from memory of a raw thread during fleet coding; as branch-B lead I read no raw threads until the permitted step-5 item checks. I did not return to material because I had forgotten it. Earlier slices became mediated by the ledger as later slices arrived, so I reread `_lead_notes.md`, `ledger.md`, every slice case, `codes_by_thread.md`, and the forest before consolidation and account writing rather than relying on conversational memory.

The case-lead workflow was intentionally checkpointed by the scheduler after each batch and after the fleet completed. It was not an interrupted coder reading: the fleet journals, ledger, facts files and lead notes carried the state forward. No earlier thread became sharper or less distinct in my own raw-thread memory because I never held those slices as their reader; this is precisely the branch-B limitation.
