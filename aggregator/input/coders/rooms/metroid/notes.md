## What was hard

The main difficulty was keeping three objects apart: r/place coordination, the subreddit's own April Fools conduct, and ordinary Metroid talk occurring on the same dates. The fake game announcement and image-prank threads were retained as conduct but every one of their codes states that it is not about the canvas.

The edited opening `62qf3v` compresses several attempts, locations and retrospective updates into one item. I coded its distinct passages separately and used the reply forest to avoid treating the edited text as if it had all been present before the replies.

Images were consequential but not readable evidence. Titles such as `63481q` support “We did it,” while they do not support a description of the completed pixels. I kept such codes narrow.

Reply adjacency was treated only as sequence. Relations in the case account require words that answer, name or explain an earlier act. The forest shows who replied to whom; it does not show causation by itself.

## Complaints

The corpus presents image and link posts whose conduct is visible only through titles and replies. That is methodologically defensible but makes a visual event unusually dependent on participants' descriptions. A rendered snapshot tied to item ids would allow visual claims without external fetching.

The instruction to “account for everything” in retained threads sits awkwardly with automatic bot text and deleted bodies; the bot exception and removal rule resolve those cases, but stating that exception beside the coverage instruction would make the contract easier to execute.
