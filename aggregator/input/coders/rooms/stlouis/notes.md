## What was hard

The hardest interpretive boundary was between repairing and protecting local marks and keeping local and neighbouring pictures alive together. The former acts on the Blue Note, Blues/Cards/Mizzou lettering, Arch, or skyline as local work; the latter depends on a ceasefire, restraint, a relayed order, reciprocal maintenance, or thanks across groups. Shared objects and reply adjacency did not collapse those practices.

Attack-minded turns also required restraint in the account. “I'm making the skyline screw them,” calls to remove the Cubs logo, and reports of Hawks vandalism are real practices, but contrasting speakers and later replies do not establish that the community changed its mind. I therefore describe coexistence and contest rather than a progression from hostility to cooperation.
