# Field check of the room model

I compare model-C3, story-p5, structure-C3 and both handbacks with my sealed field model. I read the inputs in that order. C3 supersedes parts of the earlier story. I retain that difference below. Firm claims concern public statements and exchanges. Proposed mechanisms, reported conduct and effects remain provisional. My selected field episodes do not retest the room frequencies or the shuffled order counts.

## Agreement

1. **Specific instructions make a proposed work actionable.** My design process corresponds to Collective representation, Standardization of output and A11. In `62mg1j`, a contributor offers a small Helix design “that we should be able to accomplish pretty easily.” (`c60185`). The offer specifies a common picture. It gives prospective contributors something concrete to follow. C3 correctly separates this local transition from a general order in which design always precedes templates. The offer is firm. Its effect on contributors remains provisional.

2. **Members negotiate limits without commanding all placers.** My reciprocal buffer process corresponds to Negotiated order and P08. In `62mg1j`, a rainbow participant accepts spatial terms but says, “I don't speak for all rainbownians, but I agree to these terms” (`c60256`). Another speaker disclaims Helix representation. A later reply promises non-encroachment in return for preservation. Members make a local agreement while qualifying their authority. This supports the model's distinction between negotiated terms and control over everyone. Compliance remains unverified.

3. **An objection can redirect a plan.** My space-conflict process corresponds to Territoriality, yielding when asked and negotiating room. In `632pnj`, “How much space do you need?” precedes a stated intention to seek flag space through the megathread. The builder answers, “thanks for being understanding!” (`c2550036`, `c2550040`, `c2550041`). Members exchange requirements and acknowledge accommodation. This sequence supports a conversational change of plan. It does not prove a completed move. It also does not establish A10's particular explanation of moving because too few hands answered.

4. **Preservation gives the work a future audience.** My posterity process corresponds to Collective memory, P12 and entering work in atlases. In `63di76`, an appeal says, “do it for your great great great great (*) grand-children.” A reply supplies “r/placeAtlas”; the requester answers, “Awesome, thanks!” (`c3910036` to `c3910038`). Members connect an appeal to a recording resource. These statements support valuation by these speakers. They do not prove a completed archive or a motive shared by every participant.

## Disagreement

1. **Different things: ending original placement and ending organizing around its products.** Model-C3 says, “Its organizing for this canvas is over.” Original placement has ended in my record too. In `63ef7u`, a late invitation receives “r/place has ended. . Like yesterday” (`c3920007`). Yet at hours 96.06 to 96.98, `63f0qb` offers collaborative cleanup of a copy. Members must “reserve a sector to cleanup by commenting on the quadrant thread” and then upload their files (`c3920185`). P13 already allows remaking and later canvases. The disagreement concerns what its closure sentence covers. It holds for placing on the original canvas. It does not hold for all organizing around that event. Sector allocation and file handoff need explicit treatment as follow-on work. The offered procedure is firm; its execution is not observed.

2. **World: a template does not necessarily settle coordination.** Structure-C3 describes a standard that “replaces argument with reference” and calls it the strongest coordination at scale. The Helix contributor later reports in `6384jy`, “we failed to coordinate enough to actually follow the picture.” The same account reports better organisation through a subreddit and Discord and a later sprite revision (`c3580447`, `c3580448`). The accessible design offered in `62mg1j` did not, by this contributor's account, suffice. Members still needed to align actions and revise the design. This qualifies a strong sufficiency reading of standardization. It does not refute A11's bounded local transition or establish the relative effectiveness of templates across rooms.

3. **World, with a narrower defensible setting: not every room preceded the event.** Under Boundary work, Structure-C3 says, “Every room was a standing community before the canvas opened”. In `62p7xw`, a participant reports, “I started /r/placesnek” because the earlier venue was unsuitable for discussion (`c570391`). This is firm evidence of a reported new organizing venue, not independent proof of its founding date. C3 itself recognizes canvas-born factions, and the structure includes opening a canvas sub. The universal sentence therefore needs narrowing to established host communities. A new venue does not imply that its users lacked earlier affiliations.

4. **Setting: public authority questions extend beyond governing an original territory.** C3 bounds decided order to small factions that govern territory or admit guests. My treaty episode concerns attempts to establish representation between groups. In `62qib1`, an announced treaty receives “You don't speak for me, man.” The organizer answers, “Well I want to talk to your group leader haha.” (`c720105`, `c720109`). After closure, the copy-cleanup proposal receives “who gets to decide what gets cleaned/removed?” in `63f0qb` (`c3920186`). Members question who may commit others or alter their work. These exchanges broaden the relevant settings. They do not overturn the reported concentration of decided order in particular rooms. Seeking authority is also different from establishing it.

## What the room record cannot see or establish on its own

1. **The complete reach of a group's agreement.** The room material already records envoys, visitors and disowned placers. It does not thereby establish a mandate across everyone using a group's name. The two representation disclaimers in `62mg1j` precede “We have an agreement!” (`c60256`, `c60257`, `c60265`). The public field exchange exposes that change in the strength of the claim. Neither record proves ratification, actual restraint or a complete network of agreements. A room count cannot supply those missing links.

2. **A shared event clock with identical phases for every group.** My field sequence places an early Helix offer at hour 2.04 and its retrospective account at 83.76. “Eventually we got better organized” describes an earlier change, not a change at publication hour 83.76 (`6384jy`, `c3580448`). First coded concepts cannot date every practice's onset. Nor does the original closure make every later proposal retrospective. The late Void invitation in `63ef7u` and the copy-work instructions in `63f0qb` concern different possible actions. Both records lack direct clocks for some remembered actions and edits.

3. **One uncontested version of the event worth preserving.** In `63b6md`, “You've got the void all wrong.” elicits “I will be sure to update this ASAP.” (`c3850139`, `c3850142`). In `63e1pb`, a participant objects that cleanup would “sterilise history” (`c3910162`). In `63f0qb`, a restoration request receives a particular decision: “We're keeping the small white triangle above the American flag as a direct request from erasetheplace.” (`c3920193`). Members contest descriptions and retained versions, not merely celebrate and store them. Separating room accounts into memory, transition and authority categories can obscure their connection around a shared object. The field record shows these public connections. It still cannot verify the promised history edit, internal deliberation, final files or all affected communities' preferences.

## The three aggregator claims I trust least

1. **The earlier story's head-count claim.** Story-p5 says joining replies show a room its numbers. C3 explicitly drops that function after its weak test. The acknowledgement “Awesome, thanks!” in `63di76` also illustrates why a brief positive reply cannot itself establish labor or membership. I trust C3's correction; I would remove the older claim from the shared account.

2. **The template as the strongest coordination at scale.** The supplied account does not establish a comparative ranking. The Helix failure report in `6384jy` shows why specifying pixels and securing aligned work require separate evidence. A template can support coordination without guaranteeing it.

3. **The broad closure sentence.** I trust the end of original placement. I do not trust that boundary as the end of organizing around the event. The allocation instructions, authority dispute and retention response in `63f0qb` support continued public coordination over a derivative object. Its execution and eventual outcome remain provisional.
