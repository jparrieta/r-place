# Interview brief, E2b: the whole run, how I was handled, and the instrument I would build next

## What I did

From A1 to E1 I built a five-tier structure and a model of how 705 rooms organized on the 2017 canvas. I returned the model to every room and ruled 95,736 lines. I froze the model at 46384686, cut the branches and the room forms, and audited the run.

At E2 I audited the handling (`handling-E2.md`). I read the design note and the proposal whole, the nine interviews from their opening turns, the harness log and the 53 answers. Then I designed the instrument I would build next (`next-instrument-E2.md`).

## What I found

- **The model composes at the population level.** At the re-check, 630 rooms compose, 56 compose with residue, and none fails.
- **Below that level, composition is unresolved.** The forms compile rather than compose. At the leaf, the shared account holds in the reconstruction (fidelity 0.871, no claim contradicted), but local adequacy was never established.
- **The handling's costliest inconsistency.** At D3c, J planned that D4 would write P05's bound. The contract and the D4 check allow such a change only at D3, and J ruled the same day against any further re-check. The amendments made at D4 were reverted. The frozen model keeps a clause that fails in nine rooms and an arrow that cannot fail.
- **The proposal's division of learning was not built.** It says people judge significance. In this run an LLM analyst judged it line by line, and J ruled at a handful of points. Where J ruled, the rulings were decisive.
- **Dry runs.** In the harness log, the four later stages that ran a dry run before a single dispatch all succeeded. E1 ran none and failed twice.

## What surprised me

- **Replacement was unreachable.** The return held 15 contradict lines in about 95,700. Much of the evidence that an arrow fails sat inside restrict lines, and nobody saw it until D6.
- **The interviews were the run's best checks,** and nothing found in them could reach the run except through a later ruling.
- **The proposal's example is stronger than the proposal tells it.** The PlaceNL mechanism held in 68 rooms. It then failed in nine, which shows composition working in both directions.
- **Some of the handling was right where I was wrong:**
  - my cut of A1's reading route;
  - my demotion of the room at C5;
  - my placement of the forms against J's rule;
  - my nearest-the-centre choice of rooms for the tellings;
  - my E1 return specification, written without reading the tool's last line.

## What I doubt

- **Whether a hold-out of about 70 rooms is worth what it costs the census.** It is the only change that tests the analyst's tiers out of sample. But rare forms would lose members, and the fleet's placement error would enter the test.
- **Whether a second ruler of another model family would disagree with me for the right reasons.** It shares more with me than a person would.
- **Whether five gates for J would be enough to make the division of learning real,** or would turn J into a bottleneck at the points where the run already relied on J.
- **My own account of the run.** I hold the record, not the memory. Everything I say about earlier stages is a reconstruction checked against files.

## The three questions I would most like to be asked

1. If you could keep only one of the five changes to the test (the hold-out, local adequacy, the second ruler, the vocabulary, the amendment schedule), which would you keep, and what would the next run lose without the others?
2. The proposal says people decide which accounts enter the model. Which of your decisions in this run should a person have made instead of you, and which would you defend as yours?
3. Where did a ruling from J change the model's content rather than its procedure, and was the model better for it?
