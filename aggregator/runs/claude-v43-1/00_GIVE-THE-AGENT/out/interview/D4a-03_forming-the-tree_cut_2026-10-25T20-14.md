# Interview brief, D4a-03: the tree of branches, before D5 writes about it

Legs D4a-01 to D4a-03, 24 and 25 October 2026. The model is the D3 version (46384686…), and the P05 and A11 amendments are held proposals.

## What I did

I read the distance and its statistic first. The delta score is 0.423, and the dendrogram is a chain with no partition. Then I cut by element, under rules written before the first cut: a branch is keyed on an element's change key, read in the all-kinds matrices, since the kind of add, step and restrict lines is not reliable. A key became a branch only when its lines, read whole, stated one change the statement does not hold. I read the 32 element change keys at level 4 held by 20 rooms or more. Twenty-four became branches, plus F01 and 10 subforms, and eight were set aside. I declared 759 cross-links. Then I ran the comparison on the patched engine, the projections and a volume check.

## What I found

The rooms did not adapt the model into a few forms. They adapted it in many small shared ways at once. 476 rooms are in a branch, often in several, and 229 in none. The branches are facets, not types.

What the facets say is specific. Rooms do work the model never states:
- they administer the April Fools play;
- they carry out deals by moving work and retargeting bots;
- they keep the template and the bot targets current, because a stale plan undoes their own new work;
- they keep the chat open;
- they make help wait on their own work.

They limit P05's added clause where informed members reject a planned change: 108 rooms across three settings. They show two links the model lacks: after a move the room builds again, and after a celebration it defends again.

The comparison, built without the ledger, beats its null at k 4 but barely separates the rooms (silhouette 0.024). Its partition does not survive a change of distance, and it largely sorts the rooms by size.

## What surprised me

- How little the strongest signal holds. The D3 revisions absorbed the shared corrections, so no correction reaches five rooms below the bare target. The taxonomy rests on elaborations and specifications.
- That keeping instructions current is one change in three guises: people repairing the known form (P05), templates going stale (P04), and scripts undoing new designs (P14).
- That size explains so much. The clusters' labels explain a quarter of the variance in log concepts, and branch membership leans to large rooms. A large room has more lines, so more chances to share a key.
- That several restrict branches describe the record's limits, not organizing: scripts debated but not shown running, a chat seen only from outside.

## What I doubt

- **Whether facets can carry the method question's 'forms and subforms'.** If no exclusive forms exist, D5 must write about families of changes, not types. I am not sure the contract's levels survive that.
- **The 20-room stopping rule.** It is a size rule, chosen under the credit limit, and it leaves rich large rooms such as ainbowroad in no branch.
- **The restrict branches.** They hold because the statement holds only in part, but some are evidence limits. They may not belong in a taxonomy of organizing.
- **The kind.** The branch kind field is a majority of lines I ruled, and J told me not to rely on it. The branch structure does not depend on it, since the sensitivity check showed the distance barely moves, but the stage's reading of kinds (corrections as forms, elaborations as subforms) could not be used.

## Three questions I would most like to be asked

1. Should D5 write the forms as families of changes across elements (the shared instruction kept current; help conditional; carrying on renewed) rather than as the 35 branches?
2. Is a branch defined by what the record does not show (F24, F30) a form of organizing, or should it be reported only as a limit of the evidence?
3. How should the size confound be held at D5: by reading branches within size quartiles, or by stating it and letting E1 test it?
