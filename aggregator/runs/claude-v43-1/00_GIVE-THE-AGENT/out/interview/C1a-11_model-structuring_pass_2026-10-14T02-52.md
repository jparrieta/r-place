# Interview brief, C1a-11: scheme v2 and the fourth-order themes

## What I did

Stage C1 ran eleven passes over the third-order scheme the readers used at B3 and B4. I applied J's class 2 ruling, so the readers' list replaced my B1 grouping on 1,990 lines. I amended 35 gate sentences in 18 themes, each with its rechecks, and moved one theme to the rare tier. Recutting stopped at pass 6. Then came the revision rate (200 placements re-decided), the pair test (76 pairs called), the empties and arguables, and a form pass over all 115,388 concepts on the participants' own words. At pass 10 I cut 28 fourth-order themes over 195 of the 197 third-order themes. This last pass wrote `scheme_v2.md`, the whole scheme with every field and the gate block, and `handback-C1.md`.

## What I found

The scheme names the rooms' acts well and over-placed them. My re-decision agreed with the current line's primary act in 0.845 of 200 items. The pair test found a seam in 75 of 76 frequent pairs and no lump. Most of what changed was the number of acts a line carries: 13,365 placements became 10,593 over the same 7,844 lines. The empties fell to 1.9%, in 18 kinds, with four kinds the scheme lacks.

Above the rooms, the fourth-order cut says the rooms organized mostly without deciding how. Decided order (a leader, a vote, an oath, a sanction) reaches 46 rooms. Repair work and the completion ritual each reach more than 520. Coordination ran through calls, a template, the trace of the half-built picture, scripts and a backstage chat, and order between rooms ran through tacit restraint more than through agreements.

The form pass found what the topics hide. The cheapest act is a one-line "On it." The vocabulary of state and war runs under 145 themes in 571 rooms, and nobody asks what the void is.

## What surprised me

How little the gates moved. 33 of 35 amendments moved one in ten or fewer of their samples; only help between rooms (gate:6) and credit (gate:30) escalated. Most of the readers' splits came from how many acts they placed, not from which ones.

That war words are ordinary speech. The rule that separates border talk from truces reads "war" and "attack" as naming an attack. The form pass shows builders calling themselves "core-soldiers".

That the theory question's last clause, which forms keep the work alive, landed on repair more than on any form of command.

## What I doubt

- **The fourth-order bundles are mine alone.** Thirteen third-order themes could sit in two of them. Three constructs are thin, and one, Stigmergy, rests on two large themes that could be read as plain production.
- **27 sources are recalled from memory.** Stage C3 must verify or withdraw each.
- **The bag rule makes some rooms look narrower than they were.** greenlattice t03 places border-settling on six concepts and lists eight other acts beside it.
- **Stage B4's transmission figure no longer describes the instrument I hand on.** No blind reader has seen the amended sentences.
- **J's guard counted 62 moves to a theme no reader placed.** QC1-001 allows them, but each was my decision.

What I gave up to reach the first abstraction: the partition puts each kind in one place, so a theme that serves two stories serves one. I also set aside the 21 families, the B1 index pages, because they name topics and stage C1 needed mechanisms.

## Three questions I would most like to be asked

1. Repair work and the completion ritual reach the most rooms, and decided order the fewest. Is that a finding about how strangers organize on a clock, or about which acts leave words in a thread?
2. Where a room's acts spread thin across many themes, the bag rule places one. Should stage C2 read `also_present` as part of what a room did, or keep it as a note beside the placement?
3. The form pass finds a tier the scheme cannot count: turn forms, the register of war, the questions nobody asks. How should stage C2 weigh findings that travel only on exemplars against themes that carry counts?
