# Interview, stage C3, at the close (C3c)

The model read against the literature: what it borrowed, what it contradicts, what no paper has.

## What I did

- **The order.** `prior.md` first, the method shelf re-read, the read logs counted, then the explanation shelf.
- **C3a, the contradicting works.** Four works, each decided from the record as a difference in the world, the setting, or the thing named.
- **C3b, the resembling works.** I looked for mechanisms outside organization studies and tested two predictions by abductive reads.
- **C3c, the four universal problems.** I put them to the structure as it stood, one mapping line per fourth-order theme, with nothing revised to fit. I counted how the rooms came by their goals. Then I read stage A1's three sentences for the first time since A1.

## What I found

- **What the literature borrowed.** Conversation analysis supplied the mechanisms of two arrows. A call is the first part of a pair. Joining is the preferred answer and comes bare, and refusing an attack call comes with a reason that states the room's norm. Refusals gave a reason in 23 of 38 read pairs, acceptances in 2 of 14. The concept is borrowed too: my "one-line enrolment" is the preferred acceptance. What the canvas adds is a setting.
- **What the model contradicts.** O'Mahony and Ferraro say a community must first establish a shared basis of authority. Most rooms built and kept a work in hours without one. Decided order gathers where the canvas is closest to their setting: small canvas-born factions governing a territory (7 of 39 rooms against 39 of 666, p 0.014 at held size). Their claim and mine are each bounded.
- **What no paper has.** Nineteen of 28 fourth-order themes do not handle any of the four universal problems.
  - Eight make what the frame assumes: the boundary of the room and its goal.
  - Seven are order between rooms on a shared surface.
  - The other four are machine hands under the rate limit, the repair work itself, and two themes after the clock.
- **How the rooms handle the four.** Allocation is self-selection to calls, in 345 rooms; assignment appears in 46. Division is done by a template that cuts the picture into pixels anyone can take; the rooms did not divide the people. Reward is credit after the work. A reward offered before the work appears in 9 rooms.
- **The goal.** It is mostly a sign the room already had. One member proposed which picture to make, the room argued over the pixels, and a loss of ground changed it.

## What surprised me

- **The frame cuts through Completion ritual, my widest reward theme.** Credit maps something to members. Celebration maps nothing to anyone. The rooms do both in the same turns.
- **The theme a reply sits under does not track its stance.**
- **The rooms judge their numbers by their readership, not by who answered.** That took away my concept's main function.
- **My own `prior.md` was right about reward, and my C1 declaration was wrong.** I had called credit pay.

## What I doubt

- **Every code at this stage, and the mapping, are mine alone.** A second reader could map Decided order, Visibility returns and Attribution elsewhere.
- **The goal's modes are my placement of third-order themes by name,** and a theme does not track stance.
- **The sides I took are absence claims about small rooms writing in public.** The rooms did not offer rewards and mostly did not assign. A chat can assign without a word in the thread.
- **Every distant work is recalled, not verified.**
- **One turn names a tie from before the canvas inside a colour faction:** "a fellow comrade during the button" [PurplePixels/c0165]. If there are more, colour factions are not only bodies the canvas gathered.

## The three questions I would most like to be asked

1. **The between-rooms themes.** Seven of the 19 themes the four problems cannot hold are order between rooms. Is that a fifth problem for any organization on a shared, finite surface, or only a problem of this canvas?
2. **The reward side.** I said the rooms did not provide rewards in the frame's sense, because an offered reward has to be public to work. The frame allows intrinsic inducements, and only if members need motivating. Have I made a finding, or only shown that the frame cannot lose on reward?
3. **Credit after the work.** If credit induces only by expectation, could a public record ever show the expectation working?
