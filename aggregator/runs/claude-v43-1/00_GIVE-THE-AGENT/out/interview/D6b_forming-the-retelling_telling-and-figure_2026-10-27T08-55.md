# Interview brief, D6b: the retelling, the model with its forms inside it

**What I did.** I read the frozen model against the six room forms, one aggregate dimension per leg, and then wrote the telling. For each of the 66 elements and arrows and each form, a rule logged before it ran decided whether the form specifies, restricts, replaces or leaves the unit untouched. The rule used the form's defining rooms and their ledger lines against the 558 defining rooms. I read the deciding lines whole and wrote one sentence per leaning cell. A branch was named only where it leans to the form, holds the cell's rooms and its key matches the cell. I put every new structural claim in `proposals-D6.jsonl`, unvalidated. I read survival by form from the record, not the canvas, and drew the C5 figure again with the forms named in its boxes and arrows.

**What I found.** Each form specifies the part of the model its name says, and none does the model's work another way. Of 396 form lines, 37 specify, 35 restrict, 324 leave the unit untouched, and none replaces it. A works through the terms with neighbours and the plan, C through the design, D through keeping, help and carrying on, F through the spot and the move, B through the chat and the scripts, and E through the host sub's April Fools play. By the rooms' own word, C and D kept the work most often to the freeze (60 and 57 per cent, against 49). B, A and D coordinated most after it. E and F kept least, and F stopped before the close. Across four dimensions one condition recurs: a placer acts on a form or a term only where it reaches them, by telling, a check, a script's target or the allied picture. The forms also carry three returns the model lacks: the call returns, the move returns to building, and the marking returns to keeping.

**What surprised me.** Two dimensions, collective identity and contention, are untouched by every form. No form changes the quarrel brought in, the faith in a colour, the attack calls or the taunts. The forms differ there only in how often their rooms hold those elements, and B holds them most. What the room most threatening the account stands for, a faith that names its rivals, is changed by no form; B's rooms only hold it more often. I also did not expect the arrows to be restricted nearly everywhere and specified almost nowhere: 16 of 20 restricted somewhere, one specified. The marking, the record and carrying on often begin while the work is still being built.

**What I doubt.**
- **The survival reading.** It rests on the `work_remained` field, which was right in 25 of 34 rooms at prior.md, and the differences between forms are modest. Silence and the end hour partly measure how long a form's records are: F's are the shortest, E's the longest.
- **E's restrictions.** Ten of its 13 changes are restrictions, many where the record cannot show an order. They may track how thinly its records hold the canvas work rather than how its rooms organized.
- **B.** It is a grouping, not a form, so its seven changes may describe three different sets of rooms.
- **The rule's licences.** Some branch placements rest on a widened key-match rule, or on a branch's own words where its key sits elsewhere (F08). Those placements are my reading.
- **The proposals' rooms.** The eleven proposals' room lists are my reading of the lines, and none has an order test.

**The three questions I would most like to be asked.**
1. If the forms partly sort records by how much of the canvas work they hold, which of the 72 changed cells would survive a test that holds record size fixed, and which would vanish?
2. Should the play run as work (PR-D6-10) enter the model, given that it widens the scope the D3 ruling set, and that the one room the model could not compose at the first return is a room whose record is only that play?
3. The forms carry three returns the model lacks. Is there a room whose dated record runs one of them as a whole loop, and would that be enough to ground the model's first cycle?
