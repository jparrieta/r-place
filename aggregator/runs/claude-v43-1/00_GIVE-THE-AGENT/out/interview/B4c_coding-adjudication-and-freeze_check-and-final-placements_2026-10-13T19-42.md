# Interview brief, B4c: the blind sample's check, and the placements final

## What I did

I scored two arms of fresh readers against the settled line. The arms were DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, the fleet's model, and OpenAI gpt-6-astra under Codex's built-in instruction. Each read 48 packets: 250 uniform items, 250 adjudicated items and all 185 unresolved items. I declared the scoring before any figure was computed. Then I assembled 2,739 readings keyed on packet and position, refused one line whose typed room was wrong, and wrote `reassign.jsonl`, its OpenAI sibling and the R readings. I read all 79 items that the fleet had agreed on and a fresh reader placed elsewhere, and gave each a paragraph and a verdict. The settled line, `assign_final.jsonl`, stands unchanged as the placements final.

## What I found

- **Fresh readers agree with each other more than with my line.** DeepSeek readers agree with each other in the uniform stratum at 0.781, exactly as the fleet did on the same items. They reach the settled line at 0.657.
- **Where the line came from explains much of the gap.** Where the fleet agreed and my B1 grouping matched, fresh readers reach the line nine times in ten. Where the class 2 rule kept my whole B1 grouping, exact agreement falls to one in ten.
- **The family the scheme decides worst is G14, space and help between rooms.** Its siblings turn on who asked whom for what, and one exchange holds several of those acts at once.
- **Fleet unanimity is weak evidence.** In 25 of the 79 items the fleet's two readers agreed on a placement that a field of the scheme contradicts. They counted concepts under a theme whose own `must_not_show` named the sibling. Two readers sharing a habit looks like the scheme deciding.

## What surprised me

- **Fresh readers often took my side against the fleet.** In class 2 items, they placed my B1 theme that the fleet had left out: the homeland flag in AmericanFlaginPlace t03 and FaroeIslands t01, the joint front in puzzleanddragons t04, the plans in twitchplayspokemon t03. I had expected class 2 to show only my B1 themes failing.
- **They also placed themes neither of us had,** such as T3-065 in wales t02, T3-113 in slovenia t02 and T3-041 in tampabaylightning t13.
- **The OpenAI arm agrees with itself far more than DeepSeek does** (0.872 against 0.781), but not more with my line. On the 18 items read twice, its two replications agree within a packet at 0.86 to 0.89 and across packets at 0.65. They are the same model given identical messages, not two independent readers.

## What I doubt

- **The 79 verdicts are my readings, made after I saw the fresh placements.** I fixed the test in writing, but I wrote the scheme and settled the line.
- **The class 2 rule kept B1 themes nobody placed** in 22 of 53 sampled items. I do not know how many of the corpus's 1,990 class 2 items carry such themes.
- **The adjudicated stratum was drawn from items the fleet split on.** So the rise in agreement there is partly regression to the mean.
- **The task differed from B3's** in three ways the stage required. A difference between the B4 and B3 figures is not the scheme's alone.

## Three questions I would most like to be asked

1. Should the class 2 settled line keep B1 themes no reader placed, or should stage C1 inherit those items as the fleet placed them?
2. Is fleet unanimity on an item enough to leave it unadjudicated, given that 25 unanimous items in this sample rest on a placement a field contradicts?
3. G14 decided worst for strangers but was middling for the fleet. Is the fault in G14's sentences or in the readers' habit, and how would stage C1 tell the two apart?
