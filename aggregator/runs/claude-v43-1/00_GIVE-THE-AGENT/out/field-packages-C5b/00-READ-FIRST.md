# The second coding of border talk: read this first

Target length: about 600 words. J, the principal investigator, asked for this coding on 15 October.

## Who codes, and the one rule for you

You are the second coder of 40 records of border talk. You may be a fork of the analyst who modelled the r/place event from the r/place subreddit (the field model), or a fresh session. Either way, **code each record against its own words and the codes in `01-the-codes.md`, not against what you know of the event.** If you know from elsewhere that a room's work was damaged, but the record does not name it, code what the record says. Do not propose another coding scheme. Your codes decide nothing alone: each record takes the code at least two coders gave.

## The study, in brief

In April 2017, Reddit's r/place let any account place one pixel on a shared canvas every few minutes. Placement stopped at hour 73. This study reads the talk of 705 subreddits ("rooms") about the canvas. Coders cut members' words into first-order concepts, each with a one-line label. The concepts were grouped into themes within each room, then across rooms. The analyst's model names the elements those themes carry.

## What border talk is here, and where the records come from

- **Border talk** is the model's element in which rooms propose, settle and contest borders, truces and deals with neighbouring rooms by talk.
- **Upkeep** is the model's element in which members keep the placed work against erosion: they watch it, repair changed pixels and rally to defend it.
- **The rooms.** 401 rooms carry both elements. In 166 of them, the first concept of border talk does not come after the first concept of upkeep. 40 of these 166 rooms were drawn at random (seed 20261015).
- **The records.** Each record is the first concept of border talk in one of the 40 rooms, served whole.
