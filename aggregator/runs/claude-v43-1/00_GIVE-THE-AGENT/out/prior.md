## 1. What I expect the scheme to hold

Dated 2026-10-07.

The scheme will hold hundreds of third-order themes, not tens. I expect between 250 and 450 at the end of B1, with a long rare tier. The reason is the reading: 705 rooms did the same few things in many distinguishable ways, and the kinds I logged three times or more in A1 already number about twenty-five before any of them is split by how it was done.

Kinds I expect, from the notes, in the material's terms. Each is a family of kinds, not one theme.

- **Calling and gathering.** Single calls to put the room on the canvas, often with no visible reply; calls repeated by many members; rallies, recruiting posts, "help us" threads; calls to other subs and to a streamer's viewers.
- **Finding the work already there.** Members discovering their mark already placed, joining work another group began, adopting it, being credited with it, or mourning work the canvas kept.
- **Choosing and fitting a spot.** Looking for empty space, asking a host for room, moving the drawing, shrinking it, fitting it into gaps on a full canvas, trading pixels for room.
- **Instructions for placing.** Coordinates, templates, grids, pixel maps, overlays, outline-before-fill orders, colour guides.
- **Moving the talk off the thread.** Discord, IRC, Telegram, Skype, a second sub, a private sub, a stream chat; plans brought back to the thread; members outside the venue missing the plan or refusing the venue.
- **Scripts, bots and extra accounts.** Writing, sharing and running them; arguments over whether they are cheating; scripts fighting the room's own plan; scripts repairing a rival; hand defence against bots.
- **Holding the work.** Night watches, shifts across time zones, repair after attack, borders and outlines as protection, maintaining instead of expanding.
- **Deals between rooms.** Truces, alliances, non-aggression pacts, borders, protectorates, mutual defence, help traded for help, permits from a host such as Green Lattice or Rainbow Road; leaders' deals that members do not keep; a deal kept only on the stronger side's terms.
- **Fighting.** Raids, griefing, retaliation, revenge, wars declared in jest or in earnest, sabotage, covering a neighbour, accusations and blame for an attack.
- **Restraint.** Restraint named as the room's rule; refusing to cover others' art; relocating when asked; restraint that came only after a neighbour objected; restraint beside conquest talk.
- **National and political work** (J's words; study material like any other, named plainly). Building the nation's flag; hijacking, covering or recolouring another nation's flag; border fights between national rooms; unions, hearts and shared flags across nations; diaspora and expatriate members; regional and historical flags inside a national room; national symbols chosen over art; rival flags drawn by rival factions of one nation; refusing or covering political symbols (swastikas, Nazi flags, communist or nationalist imagery); left-wing and right-wing canvas factions; a moderator removing political replies; national rivalries carried in from outside the canvas (India and Pakistan, Israel and Palestine, Serbia and Croatia, Russia and Ukraine, Turkey and Greece, Scotland and England, Quebec and Canada).
- **Canvas-born rooms that govern.** Colour factions and subs made for the canvas (the Blue Corner, the Void, Green Lattice, Rainbow Road, Purple factions, the taskbar) claiming territory, issuing permits, policing purity, migrating, splitting.
- **Moderators.** Sticking, gathering posts into megathreads, banning canvas posts, leading the work, refusing to take a stance, running their own April Fools switch beside the canvas.
- **Who does the work.** One or two members carrying it; a self-appointed leader; a vote; a chief elected; members unable to bind the crowd; a representative or diplomat for the room.
- **Talk about the work.** Arguing over the design, over what belongs on the flag, over whether the canvas belongs in the sub; calling the work pointless or tiring; credit and blame after the close; accounts that disagree.
- **What is left after.** Thanks, pride, mourning, timelapses, an atlas entry, successor canvases, a new sub, recruits who stayed.

The two or three kinds the material will refuse:
1. **Deliberation off the record.** Many rooms decided in Discord, IRC or private messages. The record holds only what came back. I would want a kind for how those venues decided; the material will give only "a decision returned from a venue".
2. **Reward and motive.** The rooms rarely say why a member placed a pixel, beyond pride, fun, a joke or loyalty to a team or nation. A kind for what the work paid its members will be thin; thanks and credit are talk about the work, not reward.
3. **The canvas as outcome.** No second-order theme can show what the canvas kept; the record shows what members said became of their work. A kind like "keeping the work" will rest on the room's words, never on the atlas.

How I will read to meet J's second request. The order is random and not mine, so I cannot pick rooms for variety. I can keep variety in the scheme: mint generously at the front, keep two themes when in doubt (the asymmetry), keep the national and conflict kinds as distinct as their doings, and log every idea before it is minted.

exemplar rule: Among quotes that all pass a theme's gate on the quote alone, choose the one that meets the gate most directly, in the participant's own words, without needing the surrounding thread to be understood. Among equally direct quotes, prefer, in turn: a quote from a room unlike the trigger room (in size, in side of the onset contrast, or in form), so that the three exemplars come from three different rooms where the theme has them; then the quote whose hour lies inside the placement bracket (hours 0 to 73) over one from the aftermath; then the earlier hour. A quote that names another room or a national group is not passed over for that reason. Every passing quote not chosen is named in the exemplar's `rivals` by its code id.

## 2. Expectations about the model

- Continuity at the top, drops at the bottom, a model all the way down; the lower levels will describe decline and continuity. (The operator's expectation, recorded here so stage E1 can say whether it held.)
- The analyst's own, from the reading:
  - **Becoming able to act** will rest less on persuasion than on a find. Many rooms became able to act when a member showed a mark already on the canvas, a template, or a host's permission. A call alone often drew nothing.
  - **Settling what to act on** will often be settled by the canvas before the room spoke (the linked work placed from the first hours), by a host's terms, or by one member's start. Votes and polls will be rare and late.
  - **What sends a group back to the beginning** will be loss of the spot: a bigger neighbour, the Void, bots, a flag war, a moderator's move. After it, rooms either relocate, shrink, join a host, or stop.
  - **What follows organizing** will be talk about it: credit, blame, mourning, and calls to rebuild on a successor canvas. The organizing end will cluster near hour 73 but not all at it (146 rooms call to rebuild after it; 79 rooms end before hour 60 without those calls).
  - **The forms.** I expect a handful of forms that differ in where the work was coordinated and on whose terms the room held its space: single-call rooms with little follow; template-and-defend rooms run through a Discord; canvas-born factions that govern territory; national rooms whose organizing is diplomacy among nations; late rooms that live on a host's permission. The national rooms will not be one form: some fought, some federated, some lived under a stronger neighbour's terms.
  - **At the top** I expect the population to compile more than compose: different rooms contributing different configurations (Kozlowski and Klein). I expect the global model to need limited branching for deals and venues, and case particulars for the rooms whose work was carried by one member.

## 3. Composition criteria, fixed before the scheme (contract §1)

- **Local adequacy.** Each analysed room receives a model plus validated adaptations accounting for its evidenced organizing; unresolved claims and insufficient evidence are reported separately. A room's organizing is accounted for when every one of its second-order themes that holds a coordinating turn (under §5) is reached by an element of the model or by a validated adaptation, or is listed as unresolved with its reason. A room with no coordinating turn (nine rooms under the A1 rule) is accounted for by saying so; it is not a failure. Denominators: the 705 rooms of the analysed set; the 696 rooms with at least one coordinating turn; the rooms' second-order themes holding a coordinating turn.
- **Shared adequacy.** Clusters must explain their members jointly through a stated condition or mechanism.
  - **A distinct shared adaptation** is one change to one element or arrow of the global model, of one kind (the same op on the same element), met in at least two rooms unlike each other (in size, side or form, as the B1 floor reads it). Two ledger lines that D3 adjudicates as the same change on the same element are one shared adaptation. A change met in one room only is a particular.
  - **Limited branching** looks like this: the number of distinct shared changes grows slowly as rooms are added in the seeded order, and flattens over the last third of the rooms; most rooms carry few particulars beyond their branch's shared changes; the branches' conditions can be stated in terms the record shows.
  - **Unbounded revision** would look like this: distinct shared changes keep coming at about the same rate to the end of the order, with no flattening; or most rooms need particulars that touch the global model's top elements (the two achievements, the reset, what follows), not only its lower branches; or two branches' conditions cannot be told apart from the record.
  - These are the grounds. No numerical cutoff is imposed; the curve shape and the share of rooms carrying top-level particulars are reported with their denominators.
- Report the fixed-global verdicts (`does-not-compose` included), particulars, and corrections met twice as diagnostics. Repeated corrections are candidates for clusters, never failures. Count ledger lines and distinct shared changes separately. Report both pre- and post-adaptation fit. Do not call exact local fit evidence of global composition by itself.
- **What each level's verdict will rest on** (two kinds of claim):
  - **The population pattern** rests primarily on the shape of the corpus: counts with their denominators, the order of events on the clock against the brackets of §4, the onset contrast, and the organizing end under §5. Supported by a pattern that holds in counts over the analysed set and at held size; counted against by a pattern that vanishes at held size (the confound in §6) or that rests on code mass. The words of the record are secondary: exemplars that show the pattern is what rooms did, not an artefact of the coding.
  - **The forms and subforms** rest on both, with the shape of the corpus primary for membership (rooms sharing the same adaptation keys, D4) and the words of the record primary for what the form is (exemplars and the gates of the third-order themes its members carry). Supported by members whose records show the form's condition in their own words; counted against by members that share keys but whose words show a different doing, or by a form whose condition cannot be stated from the record.
  - **The particular histories** rest primarily on the words of the record: the room's concepts, forest and exemplars, dated. Supported by a dated sequence of the room's own turns that the model's elements reach; counted against by turns the model cannot reach, or by a sequence the forest contradicts. The shape of the corpus is secondary: where the room sits in the counts.
  - **The method question's verdict** reads composition in Machlup's sense: whether one can rely on the composite of the room-by-room accounts. A population that compiles (different rooms contributing different configurations, each evidenced and explaining its members jointly) answers the method question positively and is recorded as compilation, not as composition in Kozlowski and Klein's narrow sense. The verdict says which of the two it found, at each level. Neither word is a success grade.
- Composition fails where adequately observed cases require only unrelated particulars, shared branches do not explain their members, or global claims remain incompatible with validated local evidence without a defensible scope decision. An insufficient record yields uncertainty, not demonstrated failure. Here that means: a room whose canvas talk was in a thread the coder returned uncoded (contract §5), or whose decisions were all taken off the record, is a room of insufficient evidence, not a room that failed to compose.

## 5. The rule for a coordinating turn (contract §1, `organizing_end_hour`)

Copied from `coordinating-rule-A1.md` (written at A1b-19, measured error added at A1b-20). It changes only by a logged departure, and every figure computed under it is then reported twice.

A coordinating turn is a coded turn that moves the shared work on the canvas. The coder's first-order label opens with the act the turn performs. The rule reads that act.

A turn counts when it does one of these:

1. It calls people to the work: calling for, calling on, calling everyone, urging, rallying, recruiting or inviting.
2. It assigns or plans the work: coordinates, a template, a grid image, a script, a Discord, a site, a design, a choice to drop expansion and hold what the room has.
3. It reports placement before hour 73: work done, started, under attack, overrun.
4. It defends the work, or asks others to.
5. It makes, keeps or breaks an alliance, a truce, a border or a deal.
6. It corrects another's placement: asks a neighbour to move, stop or respect a boundary, or repairs what was overrun.

In the labels: the label opens with a directive or building verb (proposing, starting, placing, fixing, moving, negotiating, accepting, refusing, dropping and others); or it opens with "calling" followed by for, to, on or a group sent to act; or it names a directive object (help, coordinates, permission, a truce, an alliance, a deal, a border, a move, a defence, a repair, a template, a script, a Discord, an attack).

A turn does not count when it looks back or away from the work: recounting, remembering or explaining what happened; joking, mocking, teasing, or anything about April Fools, a prank, a meme, the sidebar or the stylesheet; mourning or lamenting; thanking, praising, crediting, celebrating; apologizing, blaming, accusing, wondering, hoping; a moderator removing, locking or stickying a post, or sending discussion to a megathread; an argument about a choice, which defends a position and not the work; a plan for next year or next time.

After hour 73 a turn counts only when it calls people to canvas work or plans it: a call, a proposal, an invitation or an organizing turn that names the canvas, a logo, a flag, a design or a rebuild. Recounting the week does not count, even when the label names an alliance.

`work_remained` is the room's own last word on what became of its work (kept, lost, silent), from the forest and never from the canvas.

The rule's measured error (seed 20261007, 100 concepts): agreement 79 of 100; precision 0.92; recall 0.66. The rule undercounts coordinating turns and its end hours lean early. On 34 rooms with a kept or lost verdict, work_remained is right in 25. The end is reported with the calls after hour 73 (`organizing_end-A1.jsonl`, median h72.02) and without them (`organizing_end-to73-A1.jsonl`, median h71.69). QA1-002 (J): organizing is counted before the freeze at hour 73.
