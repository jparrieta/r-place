## The rules

Both rules were logged in the diary before they ran (`../_analysis/d6a17_select_r2.py`; result in `../_analysis/d6a17_select_r2.json`).

**Round 2, the rooms that show the form's parts.**
- A defining room shows one of the form's changed cells where one of its selected change lines has the cell's unit and one of its deciding ops, as in round 1.
- Where the form has parts, the three rooms are one per part, three different rooms. B's parts are its three facets: a room holds the chat, the scripts or the raids where it has a selected line at that fourth-order theme, and it may hold more than one. D's parts are its three kept leaves, D.1, D.2.1 and D.2.2. A, C, E and F keep no parts.
- The three rooms chosen are the triple that shows the most of the form's cells jointly. Ties are broken by the sum of cells each room shows, then by selected changes, then by coded concepts.
- Distance to the form's centre is reported, not used.

**The good old room.** This is one room per form that shows the form as a whole, the plain typical case.
- The candidates hold every column of the form's change signature in the allocation's own columns: for B the three facets together, for D keeping, help and carrying on together. They also lie nearer the form's centre than any other form's.
- They are ranked by the form's cells shown, then by distance to the centre, nearest first, then by selected changes.
- Every form had candidates, so no fallback was needed. A good-old room may be one already told; then this README points to it.

## What the figures show that the first round did not

All six form figures and the 22 room figures of this round were drawn by `../_analysis/d6a17_figures_r2.py`. Each is a `.dot` source with `.png` and `.svg` renderings. The script imports round 1's drawing (`../_analysis/d6a11_form_telling.py`) and adds two things.

1. **A hollow border for a form cell the room does not show.**
   - In a room figure, every changed cell of the room's form is drawn, whether or not the room shows it.
   - A cell the room shows keeps round 1's solid, thick blue border.
   - A cell the room does not show has a hollow border, a double blue outline. Its fill still says what the room does with that unit: carried as stated, absent, no line, or changed by another op than the form's.
   - So each room figure shows at once which of its form's changes the room makes and which it lacks. Round 1 drew only the cells the room showed.
2. **Restricts on arrows split by what the restrict line says.** The split uses one reader's sort of every restrict line on an arrow held by a form's defining rooms, made at D5 (`../_analysis/d5a36_restrict_sorted.json` to `d5a41_restrict_sorted.json`, one file per form).
   - In a room figure, each restrict line on an arrow is drawn by its kind:
     - "the arrow fails here": dark red, bold;
     - "the record cannot show it": violet, dotted;
     - "a condition or an overlap": red, dashed.
   - A line sorted as both is drawn as both. Other changes on an arrow (add-step, add-condition, link) are orange and dashed.
   - In a form figure, a restricted arrow of the form is drawn as two parallel arrows. One counts the form's restrict lines that say the arrow fails; the other counts those that say the record cannot show it. A line sorted as both counts in both, and the lines that state a condition or an overlap are counted in the label. For example, form C's arrow from the design to the template (A11) fails in 41 of its 58 lines, and the record cannot show it in 2.

Two smaller changes came with these. A change whose element is a dimension, which the stage C5 drawing has no box for, is drawn as a box beside the model; only r/4chan has one. The legends are marked "round 2". The marks of round 1 ("robust", marginal, size-dependent), the colours of round 1's nodes and the stage C5 layout are unchanged.

**What the split does not cover.** Restricts on elements, not arrows, are not split; the D5 sort read only arrows. The sort is one reader's, mine, with no second coder (J: no readers for the rest of the run).
