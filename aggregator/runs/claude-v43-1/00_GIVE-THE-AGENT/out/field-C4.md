## Three claims a view of the whole event could most easily overturn

Section 2 asks me to restate these before opening `field/`. I write them now, at C4a, without having seen any field material. They are not a reading of the field. If the field arrives at a later leg, they stand here as written before it.

1. **Relations between rooms grow out of keeping the work.** Keeping a mark against erosion brings the room up against the neighbours whose pixels erode it, and the room turns to talk with them (A04). A room under strain turns to other rooms for help (A13), and rooms whose works face the same threat join to defend them (A19). A view of the whole event could show alliances and coalitions formed in r/place itself, before the works they defend, and brought into the rooms ready made. Then the order the rooms show would be the order in which they heard of the alliance, not the order in which it was made.
2. **Most attack calls are refused inside the room, with a reason.** A call can turn hands against a rival's work (A15). Most such calls are refused, and the refusal's reason states the room's norm or the attack's cost (A16; F-014). The rooms' threads show the refusals. A view of the whole event could show that the attacks happened anyway, organized in the square or in chats, by members whose refusals the thread never shows, or by members of no room. Then the refusal would be what a room says in its own thread, not what its members did.
3. **Decided order is rare, and gathers in the small factions the canvas grew** (C01; F-006, bounded). A view of the whole event could show decided order made between rooms in the square: councils, treaties signed for many rooms at once, a few coordinators directing placement across rooms. Then decided order would be rare inside rooms because it lived between them, and the claim would hold only for what a room decides for itself.
