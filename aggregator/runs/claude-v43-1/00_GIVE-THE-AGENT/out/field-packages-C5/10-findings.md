# The findings the declarations cite

Each finding as the study's ledger holds it now: its statement and its bound.

## F-005 (standing)

Machine hands arrive under strain: in rooms carrying both, upkeep comes before the first script or spare account in 160 of 203 (0.788, null mean 0.603), and the reverse order holds in 41 of 203 (0.202, null mean 0.375).

Bound: Where a room scripted the building or an attack from the start, machine hands come first (canucks, empiredidnothingwrong).

## F-006 (bounded)

Decided order is rare: a decided element of order (a leader, a vote, an oath, a sanction, a division of the work; Decided order) is placed in 46 of 705 rooms, against 526 for repair work and 522 for the completion ritual.

Bound: Counts placed themes; votes and divisions of work also appear in rooms not placed on it (sweden, ripcity, edmontonoilers, empiredidnothingwrong in the pass 1 story rooms). Bounded at C3a by an abductive read prompted by O'Mahony and Ferraro 2007, who hold that a production community establishes a shared basis of authority before it can govern itself (p. 1081). Decided order gathers where a room governs a territory for a colour or rules on guests' requests to build: 7 of the 39 rooms placed on a colour-faction theme (Neo-tribes) or on vetting others' requests carry it (0.179), against 39 of the 666 other rooms (0.059). Held to room size, 7 against a null mean of 2.7 (95th percentile 5, p 0.0140; decided-order rooms shuffled within items_in_window quartiles, 2000 shuffles, seed 20261014). The gap lies in the two smaller quartiles (5 of 14 against 13 of 339); in the two larger it vanishes (2 of 25 against 26 of 327). None of the 56 rooms whose members asked a host for space carries it. The factions appoint overseers and lay down rules (PurplePixels c0057, c0137), name a temporary leader (TheGreatGreenBox c0051) and call for bans (orderofthebluecorner c0009).

## F-008 (standing)

Short of hands, rooms turn to other rooms: in 171 of 240 rooms carrying both, the first upkeep comes before the first help given, asked for or traded with another room (0.713 against a null mean of 0.596), and in 87 of 121 before the first coalition, counted on coalitions without hosting (0.719 against 0.614, p95 0.678).

Bound: Late rooms often enter the canvas through other rooms, with help or a coalition first (the coalition order holds in 0 of 4 late rooms). naruto, cited before, joined a coalition before any upkeep, so it no longer stands as the coalition instance.

## F-009 (standing)

The play frame does not delay the canvas work across rooms: 59 frame-filled rooms at held size start their canvas work at a median h21.80, against h20.25 for 275 others (one-sided permutation p 0.43, 2,000 permutations).

Bound: A first run that counted only cited frame concepts put 8 rooms in the group and found a 15.5-hour delay; the rule undercounted the frame and was corrected before the result was used. ultrahardcore and totallynotrobots started late inside the frame; they are not its rule.

## F-010 (bounded)

Calls to the canvas bring the host sub's line around canvas talk: in 132 of 155 rooms carrying both, the first call comes before the first megathread, removal by rule, changed rule, canvas sub, objection to the flood or dismissal of the canvas (0.852 against a null mean of 0.538, p95 0.594); early side 48 of 57, late side 2 of 2. While the canvas runs (before hour 73), the calls come before the host sub's rulings in 102 of 118 rooms (0.864, p95 0.644) and before members' objections in 57 of 64 (0.891, p95 0.688).

Bound: Partly constitutive: a removal or an objection needs canvas posts. 23 rooms draw the line first: rules remove the first call as it is posted (9), a canvas sub or megathread is set up before the host carries a call (7), members dismiss the canvas or object to other rooms' canvas posts before the room calls (7). A member's judgment of the canvas's worth after the close answers no flood: in transformers (sampled) the order holds and the first line, at h80.40, names no flood, so the mechanism does not operate there (the reviewer's error 5, C2c).

## F-011 (standing)

Membership carried in does not come before the attack: on the nation claimed beyond the mark, colour faiths and frames (the candidate narrowed at pass 4), the first such concept comes before the first attack in 52 of 126 rooms carrying both (0.413), below the null mean of 0.532 (p95 0.603). The reverse, attack first and membership told after, shows in the count; it was not declared and is not tested.

Bound: The quarrel theme alone failed at pass 2 (2 of 9 rooms). The story rooms show membership framing a fight after it (a fight told as national history); that order was not tested.

## F-013 (standing)

In some rooms a tie the members had before the canvas names the rival in the same turn that calls for or carries out the attack. Read in the story rooms of passes 1 to 4, 10 turns in 7 rooms pass three checks (an attack called or carried out, the rival named, the tie named or rested on in the words): CKCrusaders, anarcho_capitalism, cardinals, completeanarchy, lakers, rpi, unitedkingdom. Examples: "For two world wars the French and british have stood together against the germans ... covering the flag of Germany" (unitedkingdom/c0146); "You mean WPI? I'm down for a lil interpolytechnic griefing." (rpi/c0022); "Proposal: CRUSADE against the infidel. Recapture Constantinople ... The flag of Turkey is bottom left from us, let's conquer it." (CKCrusaders/c0085).

Bound: Untested for order (QC2-002): the tie and the attack are one turn, so no order statement can count it, and no share of rooms is claimed. The coders placed these turns under the attack and taunt themes; in lakers, rpi and cardinals no concept sits under the membership candidate's themes. Colour factions the canvas gathered name rivals the same way (5 turns in 3 rooms) and are reported as a boundary, not as this finding, since their faith is not a tie from before the canvas.

## F-014 (standing)

Refusals of a call carry a reason; acceptances come bare. Of 80 seeded reply pairs, 57 had a first part that calls for or proposes an attack. Read for stance, refusals gave a reason in 23 of 38 and acceptances in 2 of 14 (one-sided Fisher p 0.0032). Refusals were not longer (median 9.5 words against 11.5). In a refusal the reason states the room's norm or the attack's cost: 'Why? That'll just cause them to want to attack us' (uoft/c0115).

Bound: Coded by the analyst alone under rules written before reading. First parts are attack calls only; calls to place were not tested. The theme a reply sits under does not track its stance, so the pairs were drawn by theme and coded by reading. Reply intervals were not read as conversation analysis's delay, since posting latency is not turn-internal delay.

## F-015 (standing)

The rooms do not read their numbers off the replies to a call (weak: an absence claim). Of 40 seeded first turns on the doubt-about-numbers theme, 11 judge the room's own hands, and 0 of them rest on who answered or is present in the thread. 3 rest on the size of the sub, fan base or game, 2 on the canvas, 1 on both, and 5 on nothing: 'Sadly I don't think we have enough people to make anything :(' (sabres/c0008).

Bound: Coded by the analyst alone; one theme, one sample of 40 rooms. The readership ground was not foreseen when the rules were written and is coded apart. A thread ground does appear outside the sample: 'I mean I want to but nobody's here' (torontoraptors/c0060), in reply to an attack call. The claim is about small rooms writing in public and is marked weak.

## F-016 (standing)

Nine of the 28 fourth-order themes are ways of handling one of the four universal problems, and 19 are not. Information provision: 4 themes (Sensemaking, Stigmergy, Backstage decision-making, Attribution), 555 of 705 rooms. Reward provision: 2 (Completion ritual, by my reading of its credit acts since C5a-01, by construction before; Visibility returns), 543 rooms, nearly all of it credit and celebration after the work; a reward offered before the work appears in 9 rooms (prizes 7, a tile as payment 2). Task allocation: 2 (Mobilization, by construction; Decided order), 394 rooms; by self-selection to calls in 361 (calling members 345, recruiting kindred subs 44, timing a mass placement 2), by decision in 46. Task division: 1 (Standardization of output), 237 rooms, by coordinates and templates (207); division among teams in 5. The remainder of 19 holds 7 themes between rooms (a change of level), 6 that make the boundary and membership the frame takes as given, 3 different mechanisms (making the goal, sizing it, multiplying hands under the rate limit), the work itself (repair), and 2 after the clock.

Bound: The mapping is the analyst's reading of the declarations as they stood, one reader. The counts are rooms whose coded talk sits under a theme, not rooms that solved a problem: the evidence is talk in public and there is no denominator of groups (contract section 5). Two mappings are by construction (Mobilization, Completion ritual) and rest on the declarations, not on the rooms showing that a call allocated or that credit induced anyone to place. Every claim that a problem is absent or thin is about small rooms writing in public and is marked weak.

## F-017 (standing)

The rooms' goals were made on the clock, mostly from a sign the room already had, proposed by one member and argued in its design rather than chosen between; a vote was rare; loss changed the goal. A member proposes the room's mark in 166 of 705 rooms (median first hour 12.7). The design is argued in 281 rooms and put to a vote in 18 (median first vote at hour 42.4; 7 of 18 before hour 24). The goal is inherited in 152 rooms: the homeland's flag 96, national emblems 39, the room's mark found already placed 43, a sign others began adopted 6. It is contested in 152, mostly over whether the canvas belongs in the host sub at all (arguing the canvas's worth 76, objecting to the canvas flood 72). It is changed in 190: relocating 116, upkeep chosen over expansion 43, fewer pieces 29, quitting 20, weighing fight against move 17. The structure places the making of the goal in Collective representation (T4-006) and the model in the room settling its mark (P02) and the starter (R01), outside the four problems, which take the goal as given. The goal is a picture, so one artifact can state it and divide it (A11, theorised); at room level an argued design goes with shared coordinates only slightly more often (94 of 281 rooms, against 113 of the other 424; not held to size).

Bound: The four modes are the analyst's placement of third-order themes by their names; the theme a record sits under does not track its stance, so the counts are of rooms whose coded talk sits under a theme, not of rooms read for how they chose. The modes overlap (a room can inherit a flag and argue its design). Counts are not held to size. A goal set in a chat or before the canvas is invisible here, and the claim that goals were rarely chosen between rivals is an absence claim, marked weak.
