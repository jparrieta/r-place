# Family G18  politics, history and hate symbols

The full tests of every theme in this family. A placement rests on the gate; the discriminators say what separates a theme from its nearest neighbours.

## T3-080  arguing over political symbols

gloss: Members argue over a political or religious symbol on the canvas: what it means, whether it belongs, whether to take it down.
must_show: A symbol argued over for its politics: the hammer and sickle as a sign of occupation, Deus Vult as an alt-right sign, a religious symbol judged too divisive, MH17 drawn on a flag, partisan marks on a shared flag.
must_not_show: National emblems added with no argument (T3-113); covering hate symbols (T3-146); ideology argued in general from the canvas (T3-179).
decide_when_both: A symbol argued over and then covered: T3-080 when the argument leads, T3-146 when it is a hate symbol and the covering leads.
varies: From Czechs calling the hammer and sickle a symbol of occupation to a Mexican room keeping religion off its mural.
is_not: Painting over a swastika (T3-146).
vs T3-113: Is a national emblem added (T3-113), or a political symbol's meaning argued (T3-080)?
vs T3-146: Is a hate symbol covered (T3-146), or a symbol's politics argued (T3-080)?
vs T3-179: Is one symbol on the canvas argued over (T3-080), or an ideology argued from the canvas as a whole (T3-179)?
vs T3-130: Is one symbol's meaning argued (T3-080), or national politics read into the canvas at large (T3-130)?
vs T3-177: Do members call for or draw swastikas and Nazi imagery (T3-177), or argue over what a political symbol means and whether it belongs (T3-080)?

## T3-081  casting fights as national history

gloss: Members cast canvas fights as their nations' historic wars: invasions repeated, old battles and songs replayed, occupation recalled.
must_show: A canvas fight told in the terms of a nation's real history of war or occupation: 'Russia is invading us again', a war song turned on black pixels, a takeover called a blitzkrieg reenactment, Kosovo again.
must_not_show: War language for fights between game fandoms or teams with no national history (T3-050, T3-005); national politics read into the canvas outside a fight (T3-130).
decide_when_both: A fight fought and told as history: T3-005 or T3-006 for the fighting, T3-081 added when the historical framing has concepts of its own.
varies: From one line about an invasion again to running war narration with operations and battle names; Kosovo, Stalingrad, the Piave, blitzkrieg.
is_not: A streamer's fans calling a raid on a rival logo a war: a game-fandom war has no national history.
vs T3-005: Is the defence told as a fight (T3-005), or cast as a nation's historic war (T3-081)?
vs T3-050: Are taunts traded as sport (T3-050), or the fight cast in a nation's war history (T3-081)?
vs T3-130: Is the fight told as historic war (T3-081), or national politics read into the canvas (T3-130)?
vs T3-135: Is the fight cast as a nation's history (T3-081), or as the fandom's story (T3-135)?
vs T3-166: Is a canvas fight cast as historic war (T3-081), or the nation's whole record argued (T3-166)?

## T3-086  carrying in the sub's quarrel

gloss: A community's own quarrel or rivalry is carried onto the canvas: one faction's slogan pushed over the other's, the rivalry fought in pixels.
must_show: A dispute or rivalry from the community's own life fought or argued on the canvas: a faction's mark pushed over another's, the rivalry declared to have reached r/place, members arguing whether outsiders will care.
must_not_show: Taunts traded with a rival community (T3-050); ideology argued (T3-179).
decide_when_both: A rivalry carried in and taunted: T3-050 when the taunts lead, T3-086 when the quarrel's marks do.
varies: From the Bitcoin block-size split to old-school and current RuneScape players and college rivalries.
is_not: Two sports subs trading taunts (T3-050).
vs T3-050: Are taunts traded (T3-050), or the community's own quarrel fought in marks (T3-086)?
vs T3-179: Is a general ideology argued (T3-179), or the community's own dispute carried onto the canvas (T3-086)?
vs T3-006: Is a rival's work defaced (T3-006), or the community's own quarrel carried into the marks (T3-086)?

## T3-130  reading national politics in pixels

must_show: National politics brought to the canvas work: the pixel fight felt as a referendum campaign, a government disowned, elections and regimes joked about, a national choice of targets.

decide_when_both: A target chosen for national political reasons and converted, as when a national room puts its own flag over another nation's: T3-068 for the converting, T3-130 for the national reason when it has concepts of its own.

## T3-142  making ethnic jokes about rivals

gloss: Members mock rival nations or peoples with ethnic jokes and slurs during the canvas fights.
must_show: Jokes, insults or slurs aimed at a rival people or nation: refugees, 'elf scum', a whole people cursed, national stereotypes used as abuse.
must_not_show: Taunts aimed at a rival community with no ethnic content (T3-050); arguing whether a taunt goes too far (T3-143).
decide_when_both: Taunts with some ethnic jokes: T3-050 when the taunting leads, T3-142 added when the ethnic jokes have concepts of their own.
varies: From mock fantasy-race insults to visitors called refugees with an ableist slur; a whole people cursed for ruining everything.
is_not: A sports sub taunting a rival team's fans (T3-050).
vs T3-050: Is the rival a community taunted as such (T3-050), or a people or nation mocked (T3-142)?
vs T3-143: Is the joke made (T3-142), or argued as going too far (T3-143)?
vs T3-182: Is bigotry voiced (T3-142), or shut down (T3-182)?

## T3-146  covering swastikas and hate symbols

gloss: Members cover swastikas, Nazi flags and other hate symbols or slurs placed on or near their work, or call others to.
must_show: Hate symbols or slurs on the canvas covered or reported: swastikas, Nazi flags, Hitler drawings, slur banners; calls for help against them; promises to cover them for others.
must_not_show: Calls to draw swastikas (T3-177); ordinary repair of stray pixels (T3-021).
decide_when_both: Hate symbols and slurs covered are T3-146; obscene drawings and ordinary insults covered are repair (T3-021). A theme that covers swastikas and refuses requests to draw them keeps T3-146 primary, with T3-177 added when the calls have concepts of their own.
varies: From one member removing a swastika beside a flag to a leftist room promising to cover swastikas on another nation's flag.
is_not: Members turning a rival's logo into a swastika (T3-177).
vs T3-177: Are swastikas called for (T3-177), or covered (T3-146)?
vs T3-021: Is a hate symbol or slur covered (T3-146), or the work repaired of other damage, obscene drawings included (T3-021)?
vs T3-080: Is a hate symbol covered (T3-146), or a political symbol's meaning argued (T3-080)?
vs T3-182: Is a hate symbol on the canvas covered (T3-146), or bigoted talk shut down (T3-182)?
