The expectations the return (D1 to D3) should test. Which forms keep the work alive, answered from the record. The model is the one that stands after pass 10: 23 standing elements, 20 drawn arrows and nine propositions (`account.md`, `figure-C5.md`, `propositions.jsonl`).

## What the model implies for the theory question

The question asks what forms organizing takes when strangers organize on a clock they did not set, with no way to compel anyone and no goal given to them; how the forms differ in their functioning; and which of them keep the work alive.

- **One structure, run in different ways.** Rooms run one order: a start [R01], calls for hands [P01], building and reporting [P03], and keeping the work [P05]. Each step follows the one before in most rooms carrying both (A02, 64 of 89; A01, 81 of 126; A03, 148 of 190). The forms differ in what the room meets after it builds:
  - neighbours, with whom it talks borders, trades help and joins coalitions [P08, P16, P17];
  - a rival, against whom a call turns, and whom the room's own members often restrain [P09, P07, P18];
  - a loss, after which the room judges its hands too few and moves [K02, P10];
  - or nothing, as in a late room that builds in one thread and stops at the close.

  These are paths through one structure, not separate kinds of organization.
- **Alignment without decision.** Members try to align their placing without deciding how they are organized. They answer a call in one bare line [P15], argue a design and point to a template [P02, P04], and name a leader, vote or swear an oath in only 46 of 705 rooms (C01, F-006). Decided order is coded more often where a room governs a territory for a colour or vets others' requests to build.
- **Agreements no one can enforce.** Rooms make agreements with neighbours, and no one can make every placer keep them. Members say so themselves, and a second analyst found the same in r/place.
- **The clock closes making, not the room.** Placement stopped at hour 73 for every room. After it, the room records and curates the work and decides what to keep [P12, P13], and it can no longer place.
- **The condition that separates the rooms the model tells from those it does not** is what decides whom a room fights and whom it admits. Where the work decides, through neighbours and erosion, the model tells the room. Where a faith in a colour or a host's rule decides, the model does not (thebluecorner, catalunya).

## A counterfactual at a leaf

- **day9 borrows from ubc.** day9 offered a neighbour terms before any damage, lost the agreement at hour 48, judged its spot lost and moved [K02, P10]. ubc, a neighbouring leaf, kept a border by agreement [ubc/c0076]. A05 is the mechanism to borrow: a settled border gives members a line by which they spare the neighbour's work. With A14, it also opens a channel for help. Had day9's agreement held, the model expects the erosion that led to the loss to have been smaller, and the move not to have come at hour 49. The model cannot say whether the agreement would have held, since no room could make every placer keep a deal. The counterfactual borrows a mechanism; it does not predict an outcome.
- **ultrahardcore borrows from the spine.** ultrahardcore built late, in one thread, with no upkeep and no border talk. Had its mark stood earlier, A03 and A04 say the room would have turned to keeping it and met the neighbours whose pixels eroded it. It would then have carried the talk with neighbours that its record lacks.

## Transferability, and where the model should fail

**The conditions, from the evidence.**
- **Voluntary participation.** Every placement was voluntary. No one held authority over another's placing (a setting fact), and a call could ask for a member's time but not order it.
- **Existing authority.** No one held authority over placing. Moderators held authority over their own threads, and they drew a line around canvas talk in 274 rooms [P20]. Decided order is coded in 46 of 705 rooms.
- **Resource access.** Each account could place one pixel per interval. Members in 236 rooms used scripts or spare accounts to place more [P14].
- **Commitments.** The record holds informal agreements between rooms that no one could enforce [P08]. It holds no legal commitment.
- **The external clock.** Placement stopped at hour 73 for every room. The clock was set by the site, not by any room.
- **Whether failure dissolves a group.** It did not. 627 of 705 rooms had posts in the week before the canvas, so most were communities before it, and losing a mark did not end a room. For 78 rooms with no posts that week, the record does not show when they began.

**Where the conditions plausibly hold, and what the model predicts there.**
- **Shared surfaces open to all,** with a rate per person and a fixed end: later pixel canvases, editing sprints with a deadline, hackathons with no managers. The model predicts the same order from a start to keeping, calls answered in one line, unenforced agreements with neighbours, and curation after the end.

**Where they do not hold, and what the model predicts there.**
- **Organizations with authority to assign work:** a decided order replaces the bare answer and the call.
- **Paid crowds with task allocation:** allocation replaces self-selection to calls.
- **Open-source projects with maintainers** who lay out the tasks: the trace on the work is read through a structure someone keeps, and Stigmergy, as cut here, would not be the right name.
- **Settings with no fixed end:** curation and carrying on are not tied to a terminus.
- **Settings where failure dissolves the group:** a loss ends organizing rather than leading to a move.

**What this record cannot check.**
- Talk in chats, which appears only where a member reports it in a thread.
- Placers who never wrote in the room.
- What survived on the canvas, which is not evidence here (rule 7).

**The frame.** 705 rooms are coded in this snapshot of 4 October 2026. 706 is the target census excluding r/place, and r/the_donald is not coded. Stage A1 dropped no room (`dropped.jsonl` is written and empty). Two leads outside the frame are noted in `leads-outside-frame-A1.jsonl`. The frame is built from artworks, so it cannot warrant claims about all communities that organized.

**Where the model should hold and break, in its own terms.**
- **It should hold** where the work decides whom a room meets: neighbours met through erosion, rivals met through a call.
- **It should break** where a faith in a colour picks the rivals and rules on guests (Neo-tribes, which no element uses); where a host rules on guests on its mark (P19, a candidate); where late rooms enter the canvas through another room (A04's and A19's bounds); and where the coders coded few of a room's replies.

## Expectations for the return (D1 to D3)

The return should find the following if the model composes. Stage D1 writes `EXPECTATIONS.jsonl` from these lines. Each names the elements, the kinds of room where they should appear, and where they should be absent.

1. **The spine.** Expect R01 or P01 before P03, and P03 before P05, in most rooms with a mark of their own. Expect it absent where a member placed alone before any call (A01's bound) or found the mark already placed (A03's bound).
2. **Neighbours after upkeep.** Expect P08 after P05 in rooms that kept a mark among neighbours. Expect border talk first in late rooms that came to a full canvas and asked for space, mostly as terms offered before any damage (A04's bound).
3. **The contest answered inside the room.** Expect P07 after P09, with refusals that give a reason, in rooms that carry attack calls. Expect a tie from before the canvas named in the attack turn in some rooms with a league, school or national rivalry (C02, untested for order).
4. **Loss and the move.** Expect K02 before P10 only in rooms that lost a spot. Expect it absent in rooms that moved early by choice or by a coalition's plan (A10's bound and N08's boundary).
5. **Decided order.** Expect C01 absent in most rooms. Expect it present more often in rooms that govern a territory for a colour or vet others' requests to build.
6. **The host's line.** Expect P20 after P01 in standing subs. Expect it weaker or absent in subs opened for the canvas.
7. **Machine hands.** Expect P14 after P05 where upkeep is under strain. Expect scripts before upkeep where a room scripted the building from the start (A06's bound).
8. **The close.** Expect P11 after P03, P12 after P11, and P13 after P12. Expect no placement after hour 73, and expect curation of the record near the close and after it.
9. **The April Fools frame.** Expect K01 beside the work in rooms that played April Fools, with no delay of the work, and with members asking whether a post is meant.
10. **Where the model should not compose.** Expect rooms of colour factions and rooms whose host rules on guests to compose with a residue: the faith or the host's rule picks rivals and guests, and no element does.

## Which forms keep the work alive, answered from the record

This answers from the record, never from the canvas (`organizing_end-A1.jsonl`; `c5d_kept.py`).
- **The rooms' own reports.** Of 705 rooms, the record says the work was kept in 347 (0.492), lost in 218 (0.309), and falls silent in 140 (0.199). Silence leaves the outcome unknown.
- **The comparison by element.** A room that writes more carries more elements (contract section 5), so the comparison is also made within the middle half of rooms by size. There, 0.554 of rooms say their work was kept. The share rises somewhat in rooms carrying:
  - upkeep [P05]: 0.597;
  - border talk [P08]: 0.590;
  - attack calls [P09]: 0.622;
  - the move [P10]: 0.618;
  - the contest in talk [P18]: 0.632;
  - decided order: 0.645, over 31 rooms.

  It falls in rooms that voice doubt about their hands [K02]: 0.510.
- **The clearest difference is silence.** Rooms carrying these elements fall silent about the outcome less often (0.06 to 0.13, against 0.148). A room that organizes more also says more about what became of its work.
- **The timing of the last coordinating turn.** The median `organizing_end_hour` is 72.02 (696 rooms). The aftermath share of coordinating turns after hour 73 has a median of 0.000 and a mean of 0.020. Most rooms' last coordinating turn falls near the close. The last coordinating turn is a last observation, not a demonstrated end.
- **Whose work this is about.** This compares what a room's members say about their own work in its threads. It does not say what survived on the room's patch of the canvas. Much of a room's artwork was placed by people who never wrote in the room. It is a comparison, not a prediction, and stage D5 reads it against the canvas by branch.
- **What the model implies for which forms keep the work alive.** The forms that meet neighbours and rivals and keep the work report it kept a little more often than others. No form in this record sorts kept from lost by much. The model explains how rooms organized better than whether their work survived.
