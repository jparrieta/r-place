## 12. The form test

**The constructs.** K01, the April Fools frame, at T3-100 (the joke kept), under T4-001 (Keying).

**The relations the form would need.** Members like a change the play made. They ask whoever made it to keep it, or they make it real themselves. The keeper answers, and the change lasts or ends. Across members, each shows one route: an outside maker asked in 4, the sub's own people in 2, members doing it themselves in 2. The relation from request to keeping is not shown across the branch.

**The four organizing problems.** The mapping is empty. The requests ask someone else to do work, but they allocate nothing the members can enforce; a company owes the members nothing. A remainder is supported in all 8 lines: what the play leaves depends on who made it. Members asked the maker, or they took the thing over and made it real themselves.

**What varies among members.** The addressee (a company or site, the sub, or no one) and whether anything came of it. No subform candidate stands on keys; no route has more than 4 members. r/spacex's app and r/4chan's poll campaign are particulars of one history each.

**Result: a grouping, not a form.** The constructs can be named and the routes listed, but the members do not show the relations, and the branch binds no construct of canvas organizing. The coders' own lines say so three times: the requests are "rather than continuing a canvas collective" or "rather than a canvas-derived legacy".

## 13. What would make you doubt this is a form at all

I already report F06 as a grouping. Three things make me question whether it tracks more than which products' makers joked.

First, the makers. Four of the eight members are subs about a game or a site whose makers ran an April Fools change. A sub could ask to keep a joke only where its product's maker had made one. The branch may sort subs by their makers' habits, not by how their members organized.

Second, size. The median member has 308 coded concepts against 101 over all rooms, the largest of F01's subforms, and the canvas median area sits at the 95th percentile, which follows size.

Third, the ending. F06's median organizing end is hour 71.05 against 72.02. Work is kept in 3 of 8 members, lost in 3 and silent in 2. The mean share of coordinating turns after the close, 0.0019, is the lowest of F01's subforms. P13, carrying on, is absent in 5 of 8. These rooms asked to keep a joke and did little to carry the canvas work on.

What would make me read it as a form: in most members, a request to keep a joke followed by the keeper's answer in the record, and the same rooms asking to keep their canvas work in the same way.
