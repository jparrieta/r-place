## 12. The form test

**The constructs.** P11 (marking the work) at T3-043 (credit for the work), under T4-026 (Completion ritual).

**The relations the form would need.** A member or a room claims credit. Others deny it or check the claimant. The claim is settled, accepted, corrected, or left open. In one room, the settled credit decided what the room would spare (r/Okami). Across members these are shown in subsets: a dispute over who made the work in 8, a claimant checked in 3, a list of helpers corrected in 3; 5 members credit without a dispute. No member shows a claim, its denial and its settlement and then a next act shaped by it, except r/Okami, and the room model notes that dependence as one "the standing arrows do not state".

**The four organizing problems.** The branch binds reward, with F-016's limit. Credit is the reward these rooms argued over: who may be named for the work. Whether credit moved anyone to place is not tested. It leaves division, allocation and information free. A remainder is supported: the collective that made a work had no roll of members, so credit claims drew its edge. An account an hour old, a member of another nationality, a political sub, a specialist group beside the host sub: each was asked whether it belonged to the "we" that made the work.

**What varies among members.** Who the dispute was between (members, rooms, a host and its specialists) and what was checked (age, nationality, activity). These are particulars of single histories. No subform candidate stands on keys.

**Result: a grouping, not a form.** The constructs can be named, but the members do not show the claim, the contest and its settlement together, and the key is a step inside one element at one theme. Unlike F09, the branch states no relation between two constructs in every member's line.

## 13. What would make you doubt this is a form at all

I already report F19 as a grouping. Three things make me question even what it tracks.

First, the coders. The third-order theme holds both contests over credit and plain thanks, lists and corrections. Five members credit without a dispute, and the name speaks only of the dispute. The branch may sort rooms by how a coder read a credit exchange, as a dispute or as a thanks.

Second, size. With 14 members, one room is 7 per cent of the branch. The median member has 89 coded concepts, below the population's 101. The canvas median area sits at the 45.4th percentile; I read that only afterwards, and it grounds nothing.

Third, the ending, which is ordinary. F19's median organizing end is hour 72.8 against 72.02. The mean aftermath share is 0.0202 against 0.0200 over all rooms. Work is kept in 6 of 14 members, lost in 6, and silent in 2 (14 per cent, against 20 per cent over all rooms). Arguing over credit does not show in how the work ended.

What would make me read it as a form: in most members, a credit contest settled and the settlement shown to decide a next act, as r/Okami's credit decided what it spared.
