## 12. The form test

**The constructs.** P13 (deciding what to keep) at T4-028 (Transition), restricted.

**The relations the form would need.** A member proposes carrying the work on, or starts it. Others join or refuse. The work goes on or stops. Across members these come in part. One member's start or a call with uptake not shown is in 17 members; in the cited passages six rooms' members report placing elsewhere, mostly alone, and none shows a room moving together. Carrying on began during the work in 8, a proposal to keep the mark at home is in 4, and refusal in 2 (with more in members' words: "place is over bro..", "Just let it die man").

**The four organizing problems.** The mapping is empty. Carrying on after the close does not divide, allocate, reward or inform the canvas work, which had ended. A remainder is supported, the same as in F23: what keeps a group once its task is gone. Here it was answered mostly by one member's call or start, and others answered with silence or refusal, or said the room had "no real use" left (r/AcePlace).

**What varies among members.** Where the work went on (another canvas, a flair, a banner, an emoji, a crossover with another fandom), who carried it, and when it began. Most of this is particular to single histories. One subform candidate stands on keys, as in F23. **Result: a grouping, not a form.** The construct can be named, but the relations the form would need, uptake and an outcome, are shown in no member's cited passages, and the key is a restriction of one element at one theme. ## 13. What would make you doubt this is a form at all

I already report F25 as a grouping. Three things make me question even what it tracks.

First, the coders and the op. F25's commonest reading, one member's start or a call with uptake open, is also F23's commonest. The same kind of passage went to F23 when a coder wrote it as a condition on P13, and to F25 when a coder wrote it as a restriction. F25's members' lines restrict more, against rooms of their size, than those of any branch accounted so far: 12.9 restrict lines a room against 7.9, P05 in 21 of 27 against 101 of 175. F23's sit at their pool's rate. The branch may sort rooms by the coder's habit of restriction more than by what the rooms did.

Second, size. These are large rooms: the median member has 196 coded concepts, nearly twice the population's 101. r/thebluecorner holds 17 per cent of the branch's coded concepts, and three of the four theme excesses rest on it. The canvas median area sits at the 99.1th percentile; I read that only afterwards, and it grounds nothing.

Third, the ending, which separates F25 from its cousin. F25's median organizing end is hour 72.91 against 72.02, and its mean aftermath share is 0.0271 against 0.0200. Work is kept in 14 of 27 members, lost in 11, and silent in 2 (7 per cent, against 20 per cent over all rooms). F23's members organized well past the close (median end 80.7, aftermath share 0.0702); F25's hardly did. This difference does not come from the coders' ops, since the survival figures come from the hours of the records. If it holds, the "in talk" of F25's name marks a real difference from F23: here carrying on stayed mostly in talk.

What would make me read it as a form: in most members, a call taken up by others and the work shown going on, with the room's decision visible.
