## 12. The form test

**The constructs.** P19 (hosting) restricted at T3-010 (settling borders with neighbours), under T4-016 (Negotiated order), with P16 (help) and R02 (envoys) in all 22 members, P08 (borders and deals) in 21, and K02 (too few hands or too little room) in 22.

**The relations the form would need.** A guest room, short of room or hands (K02), asks a host room for space through a member who carries the request (P19, R02). The host grants it, refuses it or sets terms (P08). The grant holds or is withdrawn, and placers on both sides honour it or not. Across members these come in parts. By my reading of the coders' lines, a request with no grant shown is in 8 members, a grant for one arrangement in 8, and a disputed or provisional grant in 5. In the members' words the requests, grants, refusals and disputes are all present, but the host's side of each arrangement is mostly in other rooms' records.

**The four organizing problems.** The mapping is empty. The four problems concern how one organization divides, allocates, rewards and informs its members' work; hosting is an arrangement between two rooms. A remainder is supported: rights to space among rooms with no common authority, granted one request at a time. Members granted and withdrew leave, disputed who could approve, and in one thread denied that anyone owns space at all.

**What varies among members.** Whether the member was guest or host; who carried the request; whether the grant held. No subform candidate stands on keys: all 22 lines lift through T3-010. For stage D6, the hosts' own records (a lattice that held small works, a road of colours, a project of hearts) would show the rule that these guests' records lack; r/greenlattice, one such host, was read at length in F12's account. And A07's restriction now runs through three branches (section 7). Both go to `notes-D5.md`.

**Result: a grouping, not a form.** The constructs and their relations can be named, and members' words show them in parts. But the key is a restriction of one element at one theme, the host's side is mostly outside the branch, and no relation between two constructs is in every member's line. Unlike F09, the branch states no such relation. The branch bounds no claim.

## 13. What would make you doubt this is a form at all

I already report F31 as a grouping. Three things make me question even what it tracks.

First, the coders. These rooms' coders restricted the model far more than coders of rooms of their size: 12.7 restrict lines a room against 8.1, second only to F30 among the branches accounted. They restricted P05 in 19 of 22 members, against 133 of 219. The worry logged at D5a-21 applies: the branch may sort rooms by the coder's habit of restriction.

Second, whose record it is. The key restricts what a guest's record shows of a host. The hosts named in these threads had applications, requests and refusals, and their rules lie in their own records. A branch cut on what one side of an arrangement fails to show may track the side, not the arrangement.

Third, the ending. F31's median organizing end is hour 72.22, against 72.02 over all rooms and 72.16 in F24. Its mean aftermath share is 0.0094, against 0.0200 over all rooms and 0.0182 in F24: these rooms did little after the close. Work is kept in 13 of 22 members and lost in 9 (41 per cent, against 31 over all rooms), and no member's record is silent on the work's fate (against 20 per cent over all rooms). The median member has 161 coded concepts against the population's 101. The canvas median area sits at the 67th percentile; I read that only afterwards, and it grounds nothing.

What would make me read it as a form: in most members, the request, the host's rule or answer, and what became of the grant, all shown.
