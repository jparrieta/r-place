## 12. The form test

**The constructs.** P23 (the chat, a candidate) restricted at T3-014 (moving planning into a chat), under T4-010 (Backstage decision-making), with P04 (templates and coordinates) in 23 members and C01 (a decided element of order) restricted in 10.

**The relations the form would need.** The room moves its planning into a chat (P23). The chat decides. The decisions return to the thread, and placers act on them (P04). The key restricts that run, and the members' lines restrict two different parts of it. In 11 members no move into a chat is shown: the chat was asked for, offered, or found. In 10 a chat worked, and only its outside is in the record. In no member are the chat's decisions and their effect on the placing both shown.

**The four organizing problems.** The mapping is empty. Half the lines record a chat that is not shown at work, and half record a limit of the record; neither binds division, allocation, reward or information by itself. A remainder is supported: making a venue is work that comes before a venue can carry information. Members asked for a chat, offered to make one if others came, said they lacked the time or skill to set one up, and in one room promised it for the next day, thirteen hours before placement ended. The four problems take a channel among the members as given.

**What varies among members.** Whether a chat worked at all; who reported it; whether its decisions came back. No subform candidate stands on keys: all 24 lines lift through T3-014. For stage D6, the key lumps two things the members' lines separate, a chat never shown at work and a chat whose talk is out of sight. **Result: a grouping, not a form.** The construct can be named, but the relations a form would need are shown in no member, and the key restricts two different things. Unlike F09, the branch states no relation between two constructs. The branch bounds no claim.
