## The method question, in one sentence

Can independently reconstructed case histories be composed into a shared dynamic account that supports explanations at successive levels of specificity, from population-wide patterns, through forms and subforms, to particular organizational histories, while keeping access to the evidence from which those explanations were developed?

The theory question is unchanged: when strangers organize on a clock they did not set, with no way to compel anyone and no goal given to them, what forms does organizing take, how do they differ in their functioning, and which of them keep the work alive?

## How the B1 walk goes (the stage file, kept here for after a compaction)

- **Legs.** One stretch per leg, about 1 MB of codes and second-order themes (`b1_stretch_sizes.py <first> <last>`; `mem.py room <sub> --size` for a draw room), declared in the diary when the leg opens; in the draw, the rooms `mem.py next` names, up to about 1 MB. Then B1c, B1d and B1e.
- **The next room is always `mem.py next --stage B1`**, at every leg start and after every compaction. It follows the order, the stopping rule and the draw.
- **Startup packet each leg:** this head, the stage file, the last five diary entries, `mem.py scheme --brief` (paged at 80 lines: `--offset 80`, 160), `validate.py --open B1`.
- **Per room.** Read the A1 note (`mem.py note`) and every theme's note and concept labels; the room on its own first, then against read rooms tied to it. An episode needs both records to tell it, by one thread or one artwork in the same hours; a member writing in both rooms is strong evidence (`b1_dump_rooms.py $TMPDIR d_ <rooms>`; `b1_shared_counts.py`; `b1_shared_detail.py`). One member carrying the same call into several rooms counts (E249, E1021, E1022). Mark "same": "thread" only when both rooms' codes sit in that thread. A member's name only from `cite.py`. A member who only says they are from a room is not "a member who writes in" it; check the shared list. A room is done when every second-order theme is placed or listed in `unplaced-B1.jsonl` (Edit tool), only for a bare title, link or reply.
- **The tie search.** `mem.py neighbours` (paged at 80 lines), `saturation.py order` for read status, then the partners' dumps searched in the hours the room names them; read every match in full. Grep the episode file for the partners and the room's own name, and join an existing line where it tells the same event. An unread room's record is a lead, never an episode.
- **Episodes.** One line per event, listing every room that tells it; a joining room is added through `b1_episode_join.py <spec.json> --leg <leg> [--write]` (Read the file's tail again before the next Edit). It applies only an episode's last entry per run; several joins to one line go in separate runs. Describe members without naming them. List only kinds a listed room holds as placements, not bags. Check the parse with `jq -c .episode out/episodes-B1.jsonl | wc -l` in a turn of its own. Before closing, run `b1_episode_read_check.py <read_rooms_file>` and `b1_episode_theme_check.py <first> <last>`. Members take they and them.
- **Order of work in a room:** read every theme; log ideas and mint the room's new kinds first; then place the rest with `python3 out/_analysis/b1_place.py <sub> t01=T3-002 t02=T3-004,T3-103 ...` (timeout 600,000 ms; a comma places the theme in several kinds, the first the primary). Before choosing kinds for an unfamiliar theme, `b1_label_grep.py "<regex>"` and `mem.py scheme --brief | grep` show how earlier themes were placed.
- **Rule 0, single placement.** One third-order theme per second-order theme, the one whose gate it meets best. A second or third only where a concept of its own supports it; a one-code theme takes one kind. Write the leg's table as a `.psv` with the Edit tool (header `sub|theme_id|primary|additional|codes|why_not_one`, one line per added kind, new lines before the `END|t00|` placeholder; cite only codes under that theme), place every kind with `b1_place.py` first, then convert the whole table with `grep -v "^END|" ... | tr '|' '\t'` and run `b1_multiplace_write4.py <tsv> --leg <leg> --write`. Beyond three kinds: place once and record a bag through `b1_bags_from_file2.py <tsv> <leg> <bagfile> [--write]` after the rule 0 write (bag lines `sub:tNN:T3-kept:T3-a,T3-b:why`, exactly four colons, no colon in the why), one bag file per room (`b1bNN_bags_<room>.txt`). Check with `b1_precheck_bagfile.py <bagfile>` after the placement, then `b1_check_bags.py <leg> <sub>`. Bag when the extra kinds have three or more concepts each and are named in the label or note; keep three when a fourth sits in one or two concepts, or is not named, and write the unnamed kind into the room note for B1d.
- **April Fools themes (from B1b-20).** Staging the room's own fake, with its readers falling for it, spotting it or testing its premise, is T3-001. Add T3-095 only where the theme tests a claim it did not stage, or where members work out the moderators' change. The moderators' change of the sub, and members playing inside it, is T3-023 (also a neighbour's joke threads and a moderators' multi-day game); objecting to the jokes in three or more concepts is T3-032; a company's or organisation's own April Fools is T3-048; switching off the prank styling is T3-097; keeping the joke's styling or flair after the day is T3-100.
- **Reading a room (the handler, 7 October):** read the whole print before placing; grep does not replace reading. `b1_room.py <sub> > $TMPDIR/x.txt`, printed in parts under 30 KB; check the line count.
- **Verbs, never by hand.** `mem.py idea "<label>" --trigger <sub>:<tNN>`; `mem.py mint --label "..." --trigger <sub>:<tNN>`; `mem.py place <sub>:<tNN> --theme T3-NNN`; `mem.py unplace <sub>:<tNN> --theme T3-NNN`. A merge or split of a theme with more than twenty members: `log --kind split --ids`, `python3 tools/snap.py B1 move-<move>-<n>`, then `log --kind move`. A mint places its trigger as first member.
