## The method question, in one sentence

Can independently reconstructed case histories be composed into a shared dynamic account that supports explanations at successive levels of specificity, from population-wide patterns, through forms and subforms, to particular organizational histories, while keeping access to the evidence from which those explanations were developed?

The theory question is unchanged: when strangers organize on a clock they did not set, with no way to compel anyone and no goal given to them, what forms does organizing take, how do they differ in their functioning, and which of them keep the work alive?

## How the B1 walk goes (the stage file, kept here for after a compaction)

- **Legs.** B1b-01, B1b-02 ...: one stretch of the order per leg, about 1 MB of second-order themes and concepts (`mem.py room <sub> --size`: bytes of codes plus themes, not the forest; `out/_analysis/b1_stretch_sizes.py <first> <last>` sums them). Declare the stretch (first and last order) in the diary when the leg opens. Then B1c (the saturation test), B1d (the second pass), B1e (the scheme standing, handback, interview cut).
- **The next room is always `mem.py next --stage B1`**, at every leg start and after every compaction. It follows the order and the stopping rule. Never a room from memory.
- **Startup packet each leg:** this head, the stage file, the last five diary entries, `mem.py scheme --brief` (paged at 80 lines: `--offset 80`, 160), `validate.py --open B1`.
- **Per room.** Read the A1 note (`mem.py note`) and every theme's note and concept labels. Read the room on its own first, then against rooms already read that it is tied to (`mem.py neighbours`, any `leads-A1.jsonl` line naming it, and the store's concepts naming it or its artwork). An episode needs both records to tell it, by one thread or one artwork in the same hours; a member writing in both rooms is strong evidence (`out/_analysis/b1_shared_authors.py` on two dumps from `b1_room_codes.py`). For a room whose themes I cannot place, or whose A1 note marks it unsure, read the forest and case too, logged with `mem.py log --kind opened --ids <sub>` and why. A room is done when every second-order theme is placed or listed in `unplaced-B1.jsonl`; unplaced is only for a theme with no doing to read (a bare title, a test post). A theme that stands alone goes on the rare tier, not out.
- **The tie search.** Every `mem.py` listing stops at 80 lines, the `--by-room` listing too. List tied rooms with `mem.py codes --grep "<re>" --by-room`, paging with `--offset 80` until the last room; intersect with the read list (`saturation.py order --stage B1 | grep -w read | cut -f2`, then `grep -wFf`); page each match in full. The store's grep is case-blind, so a common word matches widely; `\b` bounds a short name.
- **Episodes.** One line per event, listing every room that tells it; a room joining an existing event is added to its line, with the edit noted. The close-check joins those rooms only for the themes the line lists: list the doings in the shared event.
- **Order of work in a room:** read every theme; log ideas and mint the room's new kinds first, while the room still has unplaced themes; then place the rest with `python3 out/_analysis/b1_place.py <sub> t01=T3-002 t02=T3-004,T3-103 ...`. A mint after the room's last placement is counted at the next room. Mint a kind on a trigger whose own placements stay at three or fewer; a bag's kept placement may be the new kind only when the new kind is its best gate.
- **Rule 0, single placement.** One third-order theme per second-order theme, the one whose gate it meets best. A second or third only where a concept of its own supports it and the primary cannot carry it. Write the leg's table as a `.psv` with the Edit tool (header `sub|theme_id|primary|additional|codes|why_not_one`, one line per additional kind), convert with `tr '|' '\t'`, and run `python3 out/_analysis/b1_multiplace_write3.py <tsv> --leg <leg> [--bag "sub:tNN:T3-kept:T3-a,T3-b:why"] [--write]`; check and write in one call with a timeout of 500,000 ms; several `--bag` flags may go in one call; no colon inside a bag's why; a bag written twice is a duplicate. Beyond three: place once and record item-is-a-bag. `validate.py` warns once per multiple placement whatever the log says.
- **Reading a room (the handler, 7 October, 18:55):** read the room's whole print, every theme with every concept label, before placing any of its themes; grep finds things and does not replace reading. `python3 out/_analysis/b1_room.py <sub>` prints it through `mem.py`; a large room goes to `$TMPDIR` and is printed in parts under 30 KB with `sed -n`; check the file's line count (`grep -c ""`) against the printed range. The Read tool cannot open `$TMPDIR`. Each stretch-end line says how the rooms were read.
- **Verbs, never by hand.** `mem.py idea "<label>" --trigger <sub>:<tNN>`; `mem.py mint --label "..." --trigger <sub>:<tNN>`; `mem.py place`; `mem.py unplace`. There is no relabel verb. A move of members out of an existing theme: place, then unplace, then `b1_tier_line.py` (one destination) or `b1_split_line.py` (several), and a diary line (`--kind split` when a theme is split; it counts as a new kind for the rule). A merge or split of a theme with more than twenty members: `log --kind move` and `python3 tools/snap.py B1 move-<move>-<n>`.
- **Grouping.** Gioia's first-order consolidation: stay near the material, let the number explode, get lost before getting found. Kinds, not sizes: one third-order theme is one doing; never split by degree. When in doubt keep two. No ceiling; hundreds expected (prior §1: 250 to 450). A theme holding more than one in ten second-order themes is read again for kinds inside it. Names 2 to 5 words, gerund-led, near the material, never a construct's name and never one of the four problems.
- **The floor:** a kind seen in two rooms unlike each other (size: at least twice the items; side of the contrast; or form) enters the main scheme; one room only, the rare tier. Rooms telling one episode count as one for the themes the episode lists: `episodes-B1.jsonl`.
- **The stopping rule** (`saturation.py status --stage B1`, `--json` for the rounds; never count by hand): rounds begin after 10 average rooms and 10 rooms without a new kind; markers 1 and 2 after three empty rounds each; then the draw (marker 3); then the report and `done.sh B1 --pause` for the PI's decision.
