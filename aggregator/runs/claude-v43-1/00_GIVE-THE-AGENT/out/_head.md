## The method question, in one sentence

Can independently reconstructed case histories be composed into a shared dynamic account that supports explanations at successive levels of specificity, from population-wide patterns, through forms and subforms, to particular organizational histories, while keeping access to the evidence from which those explanations were developed?

The theory question is unchanged: when strangers organize on a clock they did not set, with no way to compel anyone and no goal given to them, what forms does organizing take, how do they differ in their functioning, and which of them keep the work alive?

## How the B1 walk goes (the stage file, kept here for after a compaction)

- **Legs.** One stretch of the order per leg, about 1 MB (`out/_analysis/b1_stretch_sizes.py <first> <last>`), declared in the diary when the leg opens. Then B1c (the saturation test), B1d (the second pass), B1e (the scheme standing, handback, interview cut).
- **The next room is always `mem.py next --stage B1`**, at every leg start and after every compaction. It follows the order and the stopping rule. Never a room from memory.
- **Startup packet each leg:** this head, the stage file, the last five diary entries, `mem.py scheme --brief` (paged at 80 lines: `--offset 80`, 160), `validate.py --open B1`.
- **Per room.** Read the A1 note (`mem.py note`) and every theme's note and concept labels; the room on its own first, then against read rooms tied to it. An episode needs both records to tell it, by one thread or one artwork in the same hours; a member writing in both rooms is strong evidence (`b1_shared_many.py` on dumps from `b1_room_codes.py` and `b1_dump_rooms.py`; `b1_grep_rooms.py`; `b1_show_codes.py <sub> c0001,c0002`; `b1_room_placements.py`). Mark "same": "thread" only when both rooms' codes sit in that thread; otherwise "artwork". A member's name only from `cite.py`. A room is done when every second-order theme is placed or listed in `unplaced-B1.jsonl` (Edit tool, `{"sub", "second_order", "theme_id", "label", "leg", "why"}`), only for a bare title or link. A theme that stands alone goes on the rare tier, not out.
- **The tie search.** Listings stop at 80 lines. Count with `b1_listing_pages.py <read_rooms_file> "label=regex"`, list with `mem.py codes --grep "<re>" --by-room`, intersect with the read rooms (refreshed from `saturation.py order`), read every match in full. An unread room's record is a lead, never an episode. `\b` bounds a short name.
- **Episodes.** One line per event, listing every room that tells it; a joining room is added with "Added at B1b-NN" at the start of the evidence and the end of the why. List only kinds a listed room holds. Before closing, run `b1_episode_read_check.py <read_rooms_file>` and `b1_episode_theme_check.py <first> <last>`. Quote a member only through `cite.py`; members take they and them.
- **Order of work in a room:** read every theme; log ideas and mint the room's new kinds first; then place the rest with `python3 out/_analysis/b1_place.py <sub> t01=T3-002 t02=T3-004,T3-103 ...` (timeout 600,000 ms). Before choosing kinds for an unfamiliar theme, `out/_analysis/b1_label_grep.py "<regex>"` shows how earlier rooms' themes with similar labels were placed.
- **Rule 0, single placement.** One third-order theme per second-order theme, the one whose gate it meets best. A second or third only where a concept of its own supports it; a one-code theme takes one kind. Write the leg's table as a `.psv` with the Edit tool (header `sub|theme_id|primary|additional|codes|why_not_one`, new lines before the `END|t00|` placeholder; cite only codes under that theme), convert the whole table with `grep -v "^END|" ... | tr '|' '\t'`, dry-run `b1_multiplace_write3.py <tsv> --leg <leg>` then add `--write` (timeout 500,000 ms). The tool records; it does not place. Beyond three kinds: place once and record a bag through `b1_bags_from_file.py <tsv> <leg> <bagfile> [--write]` (bag lines `sub:tNN:T3-kept:T3-a,T3-b:why`, exactly four colons), which also writes the table's new lines.
