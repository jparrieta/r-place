# Open distinction: does a move send a room back to recruiting?

Opened at C2a-01 (stage C2, pass 1). Two readings of finding F-002 and of the story's relocation sentence stay open across pass 1.

## Reading 1, written at pass 1

A room that moves after a loss goes back to choosing a spot and cutting its design. It does not go back to recruiting. The count behind this: under the statement "some call comes after the first move", P10 to P01 holds in 0.722 of 79 rooms. A shuffle of each room's own labels gives 0.962. P10 to P03 (building after a move) holds in 0.754 of 65 rooms against 0.939. The pass 1 story says: "The move does not send it back to recruiting."

## Reading 2, from the story rooms

In 4 of the 20 story rooms, the room called members in again after a loss or a move:
- day9: "This is the new plan! Everyone do this!", h51.3, after the move at h50.7.
- texas: "We need to ask other subs for help", h51.8, and calls to university groups after losing to Norway.
- scp: a new proposal and a new call after each loss.
- worldoftanks: four attempts, each with a renewed call.

The shuffle may also set a high bar. Rooms with many calls spread across their record pass the "after" statement almost always, so a null of 0.962 may say more about how dense calls are than about what a move does.

## What would decide it

Compare, for each room with a move, the rate of calls in a fixed window after the first move (for example six hours) with the rate in a window of the same length before it. Use the room's own hours, and take the corpus base rate at those hours as the control. Reading 1 holds if calls fall after the move against both. Reading 2 holds if they do not, and then the story's sentence becomes: a move sends a room back to choosing a spot, and often to a fresh call.
