## D01 — Critical mass

The visible opening is already collective: at H3.79 a member says “Join us,” and at H3.96 another calls “Cover the board in lattice!” [P01][c0001][c0003] These are recruitment into ongoing work, not evidence identifying its original starter; [absent R01] is unobserved, and [absent A02] is not-testable from this opening [c0001]. Recruitment continues under strain: “We can only survive if we recruit!” makes survival the participant's reason for seeking hands, not a measured recruitment effect [P01][c0361].

Capacity is debated through competing uses of pixels: one participant explains that a growing perimeter slows expansion, another prefers maintaining existing territory, while another orders “DONT CLEAN. SPREAD!” [K02][P05][c0046][c0031][c0045] These contrasting instructions do not establish a collective change of mind [P05][c0031][c0045]. Collapse talk leads to proposals to frame what remains, close borders, and “retreat and relocate”; it does not demonstrate completed evacuation [K02][P10][c0316][c0347][c0349]. Consequently [A10] reaches deliberation here, not its stronger claim that insufficient hands make the room stop fighting and move [c0349][c0350][c0366].

Calls are not the only entry route into work: “I've been fixing random pixels ... and thought I should join you guys!” explicitly puts repair before declared membership [P01][P03][c0674]. [A01] therefore describes a possible recruitment route, not a necessary opening order or a demonstrated call-to-builder chain in that person's case [c0001][c0674].

## D02 — Coordination without command

Members make a repeating pattern publicly findable by signing it: “I couldn't find this subreddit” motivates a sign proposal, “Welcome ... sign readers!” addresses its intended audience, and another participant says the text is needed “so others can join us and help!” [P02][P01][c0094][c0114][c0205] This adds a visibility-return route from the mark to recruitment; the passages support its proposed function, not a count of arrivals caused by the sign [P01][c0094][c0205]. A dissenting member says a sign “ruins the green lattice,” so signing is itself disputed [P02][c0119].

Coordinates, renders and letter instructions make construction reproducible: the new sign directive locates it immediately above the old one, orders the old sign filled, and says “FOLLOW THIS PATTERN”; a subsequent post specifies shortened Es [P04][P03][c0429][c0446]. “The new sign is constructed!” reports completion [P03][c0439]. [A11] is restricted to circulating designs and instructions: these separate posts do not show a template settling a preceding design argument within one exchange [c0119][c0429][c0446].

The room also organizes deliberately: members propose assigned white and green jobs and call votes over the background; a moderator announces “WE BLACK NOW” [C01][P02][c0138][c0324][c0385][c0366]. This places it on the decided-order side of [C01], without refuting that claim's cross-room prevalence [c0138][c0385]. “Without command” cannot mean no commands were issued; it means these instructions did not compel all placers, as approved art continued to be erased [C01][P19][c0366][c0789][c1104].

Discord supports work beyond pixel placement: a participant recalls making recruiting posters during cooldowns, while an official links post provides an art application form and approved-project document [P01][P19][c1149][c0760][c0761]. The approval diagram is not just a picture template: it distinguishes allowed art from a no-build zone [P04][P19][c0654]. Its territorial function belongs inside hosting, not solely standardization [P19][c0654][c0799].

Machine placement is unestablished: “script to protect sign” is a request, and writing a reinforcement script is offered in exchange for help, not reported as deployed [absent P14][c0542][c0989]. [A06] reaches solicitation, not proven machine use; the alternate-account call concerns upvoting, not placement [c0542][c0989][c0079]. These are unobserved execution, not observed nonuse [P14][c0542][c0989].

## D03 — Maintenance

Maintenance and growth are recurrent allocations, not cleanly separated phases: “growth first,” “I'm still tidying,” and “clean efficiently so we can devote time to expansion” articulate different priorities and a return from cleanup to spreading [P05][c0006][c0050][c0234]. [A03] applies to keeping built work but needs this recurrence rather than a once-only handoff after completion [c0439][c0518][c0938]. Overnight defense is explicitly requested, and making a larger sign is proposed to reduce defacement [P05][c0461][c0362].

Recovery can change what counts as correct maintenance: after collapse and fallback proposals, the black-background announcement is followed by praise for darkness eliminating stray pixels, while another member defends white and green as the “true nature” [P02][P05][c0350][c0366][c0395][c0381]. That is a redesign sequence with continuing disagreement, not proof that one proposal caused the decision or that everyone accepted it [P02][c0357][c0366][c0381].

At H58.61 the core-defense call names the Void; at H59.70 a post explicitly says “Now that the void has been repelled” and redirects effort to unwanted areas [P05][c0912][c0934]. The latter supplies the participant's link from reported repulsion to new priorities; neither passage identifies who achieved the repulsion [P05][c0912][c0934].

Attribution matters to repair: a participant admits fighting Warframe art as “random griefing” until realizing the mistake [P06][c0698]. Another apologizes for latticing over little men because they had not seen the thread or the pictures [P06][P07][c0456]. These accounts support mistaken recognition as one explanation of damage, not an explanation of all damage, given explicit calls to purge artwork [P06][c0456][c0836].

## D04 — Order without law

Coexistence is sought early: “We'll need other groups on board” and “I believe that we can coexist” precede later pressure to recruit for survival [P08][c0011][c0016][c0361]. [A04] cannot make defensive erosion the sole route into diplomacy; anticipated coexistence also motivates outreach [c0011][c0016]. Envoys seek leaders for wider negotiations and requests are carried into Discord [R02][P08][c0066][c0280][c1092]. Joining the “united Placetions” instantiates coalition affiliation, but not, by itself, the shared-threat mechanism of [A19] [P17][c0099]. The refused anti-Void alliance further limits that arrow: threatened work does not automatically yield coalition membership [A19][c0222][c0912].

Hosting is central, although [P19] is only a candidate in the standing model: applicants request land, hosts deny new projects, publish an application route, distinguish approved zones, and order unapproved art covered [P19][c0106][c0251][c0760][c0654][c0799]. Protection is selective rather than an unconditional all-art policy: “Rule #1: Protect art” coexists with orders to overwrite art outside the approval zone and claims that more art would end the lattice [P19][P07][c0415][c0799][c1083]. These positions require territorial admission and limits, not a new construct beyond [P19] [c0654][c0799].

Approval has to reach and persuade placers: “Approved art is being erased!” exposes implementation failure, and “approved already ... isn't popular with our placers” distinguishes moderator permission from popular acceptance [P19][P21][c0789][c1104]. [A05] operates as reminders and repair obligations, not guaranteed compliance: members say “follow the agreement,” while another promises to fix treaty mistakes [c0407][c0295]. Learning approval can matter locally: the forest places “Oops. Ok I'll stop” after an approval correction, but this does not establish document-wide effectiveness [P07][P06][c0753].

The tentative [P21] is instantiated by the explicit distinction between official agreement and placer acceptance [c1104]. [A21] is supported narrowly: approval is invoked and its practical authority disputed, without establishing that every later erasure was principled refusal [c1104][c0698]. In another exchange one speaker experiences an apparent sudden attack, and the next says the move had been discussed in Skyrim's Discord; the reply answers the information gap but does not demonstrate that all participants had received notice [P08][P21][c1145][c1146].

Help ranges from barter to gifts and refusals: “if we help lattice that Russian Flag, they'll give us that space for Boba” receives “Works for me. I'll start latticing them” 3.2 minutes later in the forest [P16][A14][c0635][c0636]. This is an accepted exchange of destructive labor for protected space, not simply benevolent maintenance [P16][P09][c0635][c0636]. A different applicant promises defense “No matter what,” while a request for help is refused because no soldiers can be spared [P16][c0855][c0225]. [absent A13] is not-testable as its complete shortage-to-solicitation-to-help-received mechanism: defensive strain and offers of maintenance are visible, but not that causal chain [c0912][c0604][c0755]. [A14] explains some, not all, assistance [c0855][c0635][c0636].

An agreement with Yugioh explicitly links protection to defense against white spread; a later call asks for pixels against interference with the protected work [P08][P16][c0933][c0951]. The stated defense rationale supports a participant's explanation, not independently observed reciprocal pixel flows [A14][c0933][c0951]. In the Slime exchange, a rebuilding call invokes approved status and Rule #1; the applicant answers “Thank you :)” 1.3 minutes later [P07][P16][P15][c1140][c1141]. [A12] is restricted here to this bare thanks after a repair proposal, not a demonstrated bare acceptance of initial recruitment [c1140][c1141][c0041].

Retreat remains at [P10]'s smallest range, asking whether to move; changing the background and rebuilding do not establish completed relocation [c0349][c0366][c0439]. The Skyrim representative agrees to move the name into the dragon, and a reply welcomes compromise 6.4 minutes later; subsequent pleas to stop attacking the relocated logo prevent treating agreement as a settled outcome [P08][P07][c1143][c1144][c1122].

## D05 — Collective identity

Faith in a color is visible in “we preach our gene,” conversion from blue, and helping because one supports green [K04][c0027][c0201][c0730]. A moderator later calls the community “completely new and thrown together from scratch”: that is a participant's account of emergence, not an independently established origin or a pre-event collective baseline [K04][c1148].

Identity does not dictate one policy: “Our lattice is art” and a call to purge all artwork defend a pure core, while another account defines the lattice as protecting others' artwork [K04][P19][c0572][c0836][c0241]. Color-faith therefore conditions disputes about hosting, not an inferred unanimous program or a causal explanation for every exclusion [K04][P19][c0836][c0799][c0830]. [absent K03] and [absent C02] are not-testable as inherited rivalries: the supplied pre-week brief is empty, and event-era anti-blue or anti-Void talk cannot establish a pre-canvas quarrel [c0227][c0913][c1148].

## D08 — Contention

Attack calls coexist with restraint: “OVERTAKE THE BLUECORNER SIGN” is a call, while a reply to a separate anti-blue proposal says “we do not seek to destroy anyone” [P09][P07][c0397][c0227]. [A15] is not-testable as recruitment causing attackers to turn: these concepts do not trace recruits into raids [c0001][c0397]. [A16] is instantiated narrowly by the forest's anti-blue proposal answered with the peaceful-nation refusal; the reason is visible, not a room-wide acceptance rate [c0227]. [A17] is exemplified by a moderator invoking the peace treaty to stop action in disputed territory, not by assuming every attack produces a deal [c1138].

The Void posting wave is contention entering this room: threats of consumption, repeated allegiance, and a mocking variation accompany defense alarms [P18][c0904][c0858][c0906][c0912]. [A18] links named threat and contest talk locally, but does not establish one organizer, identical intentions, or that host members authored the hostile flood [c0904][c0906][c0913]. Nor does a hostile offer dictate alliance: the Void's offer of reclaimed land for attacking Skyrim receives “No deals with the chaos of the void!” 14.0 minutes later [P16][P07][c1147][c1124].

The strongest competing account is deliberate purification at guests' expense: the explicit demand for an “immediate purging” of artwork and official orders to overwrite unapproved art make exclusion substantive, not merely accidental [P19][P09][c0836][c0799]. But rebuilding under Rule #1, gratitude for protection, and refusal of the opportunistic Void deal rule out reducing all hosting to a sham [P19][P07][P16][c1140][c1142][c1124]. The adapted explanation is attempted, selective hosting under contested authority; it does not claim reliably peaceful outcomes or a community-wide conversion from peace to aggression [P19][P21][c0789][c1104][c1140].
