# r/toolband — D2 local model

## D01 Critical mass

The project began as an invitation to secure a Tool section, paired with a proposed location, rather than an already organized community undertaking [R01][P01][c0007][c0008]. Replies reported keeping a logo going and giving a hand, supporting recruitment into construction in this exchange [A01][c0009][c0010]. Starter and call occur together in the opening; this supports their connection, not a separate elapsed stage [A02][c0007][c0008]. Later calls reached related music audiences, including a reported appeal to progmetal's Tool fans [P01][c0104].

Capacity was an explicit problem: an organizer counted “like 10 active people” who could barely hold the basic logo [K02][c0092]. This was a participant's estimate, not a census [K02][c0092]. One small practice falls outside recruitment: pointing out the “screaming carrots” at particular coordinates invited attention to another picture without asking for work [new: t08 noticing another drawing][c0011].

## D02 Coordination without command

Contributors proposed a wordmark, debated spacing and eye size, and discarded an ambitious design to see “something rather than nothing” [P02][c0010][c0124][c0142][c0106]. They reported finished letters and named precise repairs, including the L's tail and the T's crossbar [P03][c0070][c0047]. Links, coordinates and an exact pupil position supplied common references [P04][c0012][c0108][c0147]. This was not simply spontaneous coordination: upvote agreement, an eye-size vote, a contributor comparing their work to leading groups in games, and insistence on one plan place this room in the decided-order exception to C01, without challenging its cross-room frequency claim [C01][c0052][c0142][c0111][c0112].

The references also needed translation into usable participation: mobile users asked how to edit and received app instructions [P01][c0042][c0043][c0050]. A builder unable to distinguish some colors received palette positions instead of color names, a finer accessibility step within standardizing output [P04][c0148][c0149]. A reference alone did not guarantee convergence: people deleted another builder's correction, while a spacing argument ended in an explicit concession [P04][P06][c0136][c0124][c0125]. The record cannot establish that posting a pixel template itself settled these disputes; surviving references and instructions do not supply that causal test [absent A11][c0046][c0124][c0125][c0147].

Bare answers occur: “I'll defend the bend” and “Already done!” commit or report completion without demanding an answer [P15][c0129][c0138]. The defense commitment follows a negotiated proposal, but it does not establish A12's stronger claim that every reader owes a next answer, nor its proposed acceptance/refusal contrast [A12][c0127][c0129]. Machine placement by this contingent is unobserved, not disproved: suspicious numbered accounts are attributed to interference, not to the Tool builders [absent P14][c0114].

## D03 Maintenance

Construction and upkeep overlapped: contributors wanted to finish and hold the current design before expanding because dots were already appearing [P05][A03][c0076]. They sought overnight guards, put the original logo before the eye, and made the eye minimal enough to defend both [P05][c0081][c0150][c0146]. Keeping the work also consumed the keepers: staying awake near completion became dreams, exhaustion and the admission that it “took up way too much of my life” [P05][c0155][c0162][c0226][c0227]. This warrants a finer account of maintenance's experienced cost, not a claim that fatigue caused a later decision [P05][c0222][c0226].

Attribution was not uniformly hostile: contributors admitted a wrong gray pixel and later confessed accidental last-second damage that could not be undone [P06][c0123][c0214]. Deliberate repairs were also being erased by other contributors [P06][c0136]. Maintenance pressure is visible, but a turn from that pressure to Tool-run scripts or spare accounts remains unobserved; the record does not test A06's proposed mechanism [absent A06][c0092][c0114][c0226].

## D04 Order without law

Neighbor talk did practical work. Encroachment prompted “We must reach peace” and a reported appeal on PlaceStart's megathread [P08][A04][c0181][c0182]. The Bender bargain exchanged speech-bubble space for defense; the reply “I'll defend the bend” explicitly accepted its obligation [P08][P16][A14][c0127][c0129]. At the model's minimum scale, this is a two-group defensive alliance, not evidence of a council or integrated command [P17][c0127][c0129]. The account that “we ... formed alliances” supports participants' retrospective understanding, but cannot establish that a shared attack caused coalition formation [absent A19][c0230][c0127].

The same bargain shows hosted space and an envoy: one contributor brought back permission to use Bender's speech bubble, and later contributors sought a PlaceStart moderator's blessing [P19][R02][c0127][c0156]. Those are reported permissions and requests, not direct observation of the host's deliberations [P19][c0127][c0156]. Neither guests nor hosts had a single uncontested account of territory: “You're encroaching on what I worked so hard to create” answered Tool's own encroachment complaint [P08][c0158][c0159].

Restraint did not require an already settled boundary. “We don't need to invade anyone” invoked unused space and punishment for greed; another contributor preferred a truncated eye or a future truce to war [P07][A05][c0132][c0152]. Pokémon's request to spare Vaporeon's feet received an offer of a separating border and reciprocal help; another branch already offered “a few pixels” [P07][P16][A14][c0174][c0175][c0176]. Under PlaceStart pressure, a help request was relayed to Pokémon's Discord with an explicit warning about limited capacity: solicitation and relay are observed, delivery from that appeal is not [P16][A13][c0178][c0179]. A later participant reports direct messages yielding “we'll stop”; this is reported success, not access to the messages [P07][c0237][c0238].

The tentative mandate-dispute entry is unresolved, not instantiated by proximity. “One person said we can move” and “people actively resisting my moves” establish reported permission and internal interference, but neither says that an agreement cannot bind placers or that its speaker lacks authority [absent P21][c0186][c0187]. They therefore cannot establish that negotiating produced a dispute about who may commit the group [absent A21][c0186][c0187]. The private exchanges needed to decide that reading are not supplied by those reports [absent A21][c0237][c0238].

Loss did repeatedly lead to relocation: a wave takeover prompted a new-coordinate motion, while a consumed corner was followed by a redirection to another build [P10][c0057][c0059][c0060]. “The Radiohead idea isn't working out” explicitly justified another location proposal [P10][c0083]. Relocation reconnects to building locally: the redirection's nested reply reports trying pixels, and the closing account recalls successive logo forms [new: t04 returning from relocation to building][c0060][c0061][c0234]. But low manpower did not invariably make the group abandon a spot: it also justified a minimal eye that could be defended beside the logo [A10][P02][c0092][c0143][c0146]. The adaptation adds resizing in place as an alternative, rather than treating every smaller design as a move [A10][c0143][c0146].

## D08 Contention

The participating contingent was not simply peaceful. “Black out ABOVE our current logo” ended with “May the Pokemon rest in piece,” showing an expansion call that acknowledged a neighboring work [P09][A15][c0145]. “Besting RadioHead” and a later account blaming the Red Wings sub carry contest into words, but they concern distinct episodes, not one continuously inherited rivalry [P18][c0087][c0221]. Calls to spare neighbors and seek truces coexist with encroachment; the coded passages do not show that a particular attack call directly caused a refusal, negotiation or taunt [absent A16][absent A17][absent A18][c0145][c0132][c0174][c0087].

Against an exclusively hostile reading, a reply reports that war was cancelled, thanks Tool for not retaliating, and receives an offer to keep the former opponent's square neat [P07][P16][c0115][c0116][c0117]. That supports an exchange and reported outcome, not the claim that one offer alone cancelled war [P16][c0115][c0116]. Yet a separate contributor claimed to be trying to infiltrate the voiders as a moderator to divide and redirect them; no result is demonstrated [new: t09 attempting deceptive redirection of opponents][c0133]. This is neither an agreed border nor observed script sabotage [new: t09 attempting deceptive redirection of opponents][c0133].

## D07 Imprinting

Completion produced celebration before shutdown as well as at the end: “We've Done It!” invited expansion, and the closing post celebrated that Tool and the Flaming Eye survived [P11][A07][c0134][c0215][c0216]. Named thanks and reflection made cooperative labor explicit [P11][c0229][c0230]. Within the celebration thread, an atlas entry and request for carefully worded accounts followed the opening; this supports celebration becoming record-making, not a claim about every participant's motive [P12][A08][c0219][c0220][c0215].

Survival was imperfect. One builder was shut out before repairing last-second changes; others called the missing pieces “battle scars” and “a part of the story” [P12][c0208][c0211][c0212]. These comments instantiate P22's damage-as-history end of its stated range, without establishing a dispute over editing a shared clean copy [P22][c0211][c0212]. Requesting a fuller atlas story is record work, not evidence that a false account was actually corrected [P12][c0220].

The mark brought someone back into the room: “Didn't know this subreddit was a thing” received “So it worked!” [new: t13 bringing canvas visitors into the subreddit][c0164][c0165]. That observed arrival is narrower than hoped-for subscribers or retrospective claims of recruitment; it does not measure growth [new: t13 bringing canvas visitors into the subreddit][c0128][c0198][c0230].

The local ending was not only deciding what canvas effort to retain. The celebration itself said “get back to ... normal,” and later replies reclaimed speculation over “pixel crap” [new: t14 resuming ordinary room talk][c0218][c0240][c0241]. Some builders both missed placing and welcomed its end; that ambivalence must not become a community-wide reversal [new: t14 resuming ordinary room talk][c0222][c0228]. Keeping a later project, banner or durable alliance is unobserved, not demonstrated absent by a return-to-normal remark [absent P13][c0218][c0230]. Atlas recording alongside return talk does not establish record-making causing either carryover or withdrawal [absent A09][c0219][c0218].

## D06 Host framing

April Fools conduct had its own objects: a fake double-album announcement caught a reader, and a mock subreddit move reversed memes and constructive content under “totally a moderator” [K01][c0006][c0002][c0030][c0031][c0032]. It was not a canvas relocation [K01][c0030][c0048]. Other readers needed explanations of the pixel timer and collective work; orientation precedes evaluation in one exchange where a skeptic explicitly changed their mind after reading an explanation [new: t06 explaining the canvas to room members][c0097][c0078][c0079][c0080].

Repeated calls also met objections to a front page filled with pixel posts and demands for one thread [P20][A20][c0190][c0194][c0202]. The boundary was not simply builders against outsiders: a contributor valued the large logo while wanting discussion contained [P20][c0193]. The strongest limit on a whole-room-unity reading is the explicit complaint about “25+ separate threads” and participation requiring “infinite free time” [P20][c0202][c0203]. One speaker's changed mind and other speakers' enduring objections are different findings, not stages of collective conversion [P20][c0080][c0189][c0202]. Requests for containment do not themselves demonstrate moderator enforcement [P20][c0193][c0194].
