# r/canada: adapted room model

## D01 Critical mass

Calls recruited people to make and enlarge Canadian symbols [P01] [c0001] [c0006] [c0230]. They did not necessarily initiate the work: the call to put differences aside received directions to a flag already being made, followed by the caller acknowledging that it had missed that effort [A01] [c0005] [c0007]. Recruitment therefore recurred around existing work rather than establishing a single start–build sequence [A01] [c0006].

A call could receive a bare, joking commitment, “red fox standing by” [P15] [A12] [c0230] [c0233]. This supports a written acceptance, not an inference that every reader answered or placed a pixel [A12] [c0233]. Capacity limits also supported staying small: “It will take too much man power/organization to maintain a larger one” and “successfully maintaining one” opposed expansion [K02] [c0207] [c0240]. Shortage did not necessarily induce relocation [A10] [c0206] [c0207].

## D02 Coordination without command

Participants sought a recognizable flag through proposed designs, proportions, coordinates and grids [P02] [P04] [c0065] [c0070] [c0077] [c0235]. Proposing or beginning work supplied a point around which others could gather, but the visible record does not identify one starter governing the whole undertaking [R01] [A02] [c0230] [c0233] [c0235]. Reports that a flag was finished could immediately call for a larger outline, making building iterative [P03] [c0006].

Posting a design did not itself settle it: approval coexisted with a question about its height, and another participant explicitly required consensus and a changed strategy before adopting a better-looking flag [A11] [c0075] [c0077] [c0299]. Changing plans could waste contributors' pixels [P04] [c0389]. Instructions could receive a direct completion report, “Done. Working on it now,” without establishing canvas-wide compliance [P15] [c0282] [c0283].

Script use was proposed for defence, while other participants disputed whether bots or coordinated users explained the work [P14] [c0451] [c0353] [c0354]. These passages do not verify actual script deployment for this flag or a transition from manual upkeep to machines [absent A06] [c0451] [c0353]. The observation is not-testable, not observed absence of automation [P14] [c0353] [c0354].

The aggregate prevalence claim about decided organization cannot be tested from this room, and neither a strategy venue nor a request for consensus proves an appointed leader or formal vote [absent C01] [c0299] [c0311]. The observation is not-testable; invisible decisions cannot be replaced by an assertion that the room lacked organization [C01] [c0299].

## D03 Maintenance

“Good, now we must maintain our ground” explicitly makes achieved work a reason for upkeep [A03] [P05] [c0139]. Yet completion and maintenance overlapped: a “perfect” flag post received an attack report, and a participant expressed pride while doing maintenance [P05] [c0324] [c0333] [c0355]. Celebration was not a terminal stage preceding all further work [A07] [c0355].

Attribution remained disputable: one speaker denied scripts and pointed to multiple users and spreadsheets, while another described the canvas as mostly script-driven [P06] [c0353] [c0354]. Neither statement independently identifies the people or mechanisms responsible for every change [P06] [c0353] [c0354]. Protection and neighbour deals were explicitly named as purposes of the canvas subreddit, supporting their practical connection but not proving that erosion caused each negotiation [A04] [c0311].

## D04 Order without law

Participants sought coexistence through restraint, borders, truces and volunteered negotiation [P07] [P08] [R02] [c0146] [c0184] [c0303]. “We are currently at war with 1G” received a proposal to negotiate with Summit rather than continue bloodshed, a direct local alternative to fighting [A17] [c0183] [c0184]. A reported Quebec peace decision supported an appeal for reciprocal restraint; it did not establish that all placers complied [A05] [c0146].

A visitor asked that expansion spare a relocated logo, and the answer proposed a border while admitting, “I can't control everyone on the Canada flag” [P08] [P21] [A21] [c0302] [c0303]. The tentative authority mechanism applies at this limited scale: a request for coexistence elicited a qualification about binding placers [A21] [c0302] [c0303]. It does not show members repudiating a concluded treaty or disputing a formally authorized representative [P21] [c0303]. Nor does being next to the flag establish a guest living under Canada's jurisdiction [absent P19] [c0302] [c0304]; that host–guest arrangement is unobserved.

Help was requested and offered, but it competed with existing commitments: one person declined because of work for Gorillaz; another offered a future free pixel while defending a different picture [P16] [c0101] [c0306]. The model's shortage-to-help pathway must therefore distinguish requests, conditional individual offers and delivered collective assistance [A13] [c0204] [c0306]. A truce-and-aid request alone does not show that an agreement subsequently produced help [absent A14] [c0205]; that causal sequence is not-testable here.

"Think we need a new spot" followed resistance and accompanied new coordinates, supporting a proposed restart, not a verified relocation after erasure [P10] [c0234] [c0235]. Similarly, invitations to join FlagAlliance or form an alliance cannot by themselves establish a functioning mutual-defence coalition or its formation under upkeep pressure [absent P17] [absent A19] [c0055] [c0222]; these outcomes are unobserved and their causal pathway not-testable.

## D05 Collective identity

The flag was a vehicle for competing accounts of Canadian and Quebec belonging, not a single shared national position [K03] [K04] [c0286] [c0295] [c0520] [c0523]. One participant called the conflict a “literal political war”; another explicitly connected hostility toward Quebec's flag to support for independence [K04] [c0286] [c0295]. This adds feedback: canvas conduct could become a participant's evidence for a pre-existing political stance, rather than identity only arriving from outside [K04] [c0295].

The Hans Island call named Denmark and territorial rivalry in the very turn that called for invasion, closely instantiating the model's same-turn link between prior ties and a target [C02] [c0448]. This is evidence about that call, not proof that the entire subreddit collectively chose its rival [C02] [c0448].

## D06 Host framing

Separate rail and bridge bait was keyed as April Fools play, but replies could leave the joke for sincere inquiry about access and limited passes [K01] [c0123] [c0124] [c0254] [c0255]. A finer step is the explicit “All jokes aside” or “Fools aside” switch into ordinary practical discussion; the prank did not keep every subsequent claim in a joking frame [K01] [c0123] [c0254] [c0261] [c0268].

Moderators directly redirected a protection-and-expansion call to r/placecanada [P20] [A20] [c0482] [c0484]. Removals were not merely a terminal response to too much activity: participants explicitly cited removals as their reason for seeking another subreddit or Discord venue [P20] [c0168] [c0273]. Boundary work could redirect coordination rather than end it; this does not demonstrate how much work subsequently occurred elsewhere [A20] [c0168] [c0273].

Ordinary corrective exchanges about possessives and time zones also continued inside canvas threads [new: carrying ordinary corrective side-talk through event participation] [c0008] [c0012] [c0029] [c0031]. ## D07 Imprinting

A museum-posting request and the final contribution post show preservation and retrospective reporting [P12] [c0339] [c0499]. They do not establish that celebration caused preservation rather than accompanying it [absent A08] [c0324] [c0339]; that causal claim is not-testable.

The “Final Planned Image ... Fixed” title establishes an offered corrected version, while another account says the actual frozen flag is “forever stuck at 158” [P12] [P22] [c0511] [c0543]. The tentative P22 must be restricted: intended and frozen versions coexist in the record, but no observed exchange decides which shared copy should be kept or disputes a cleanup as historical revision [P22] [c0511] [c0543]. A call to make a flag on a new canvas instantiates continuation as a proposal, not demonstrated uptake [P13] [c0529]. No cited passage connects that call causally to the preserved final image [absent A09] [c0499] [c0529]; that pathway is not-testable.

## D08 Contention

Recruitment could itself be an attack call: converting the US flag into Canada's and invading Denmark were framed as collective tasks [P09] [A15] [c0044] [c0448]. This supports mobilizing for attack, not a verified diversion of people previously recruited for construction [A15] [c0044]. An EVE attack proposal received a dismissive response questioning the activity's worth, not an observed successful restraint of attackers [A16] [c0470] [c0471].

The opposite response direction also occurred: conserving the fleurdelisé received the direct answer “I'm doing everything I can to destroy it” [P07] [P09] [c0288] [c0289]. Restraint could thus become the occasion for a contrary destructive declaration, rather than resolving it [new: answering preservation with avowed destruction] [c0288] [c0289]. The declaration is not independent verification of pixel destruction [P09] [c0289].

A question about declaring war while wanting coexistence received “retaliation,” supporting an explicit participant explanation for escalation [P18] [A18] [c0296] [c0297]. Post-event “Canadian medicine” sarcasm and an appeal to live together preserved contrasting political readings, not evidence that the community changed its mind in a common direction [P18] [K04] [c0520] [c0523].
