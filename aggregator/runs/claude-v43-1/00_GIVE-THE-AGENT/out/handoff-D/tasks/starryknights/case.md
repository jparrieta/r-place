## Chronology

The supplied room begins only at d1 04-02 00:45, so it does not show the project's first day. Its first episode, bounded by `62wr6n` and `6301sz` (d1 04-02 00:45 to d1 04-02 14:56), is making the picture visible as a shared achievement. The opening vow says, “I shall rise again … and I will go on with my drawing” (`62wr6n`); people then publish a guide (`62xag9`), the “offical discord” (`62xo35`), a timelapse (`62xopv`), near-finished and finished views (`62xz0x`, `62y390`), congratulations (`62xycp`), and finally “We got featured!” (`6301sz`). These are not merely reports about an image: the comments claim small contributions, thank participants, and continue placing pixels.

A second, overlapping episode opens with “Recommend we use this grid to better-reproduce the texture” (`62y1v1`) and continues through proposed sky and horizon edits (`634ix9`). Participants work out what picture can be both recognisable and maintainable. One side says the original has “very rich textures” (`dfpx0c7`); another says changing the settled template would attract griefers and take more work (`dfpx3jt`); a third says the exact 16 colours prevent “useless fighting” over interpretations (`dfq2m4d`). This is a descriptive difference in priorities—fidelity, stability, and coordination—not evidence that the whole community changed its mind. Later corrections to a missing bottom row (“Whoops, will fix that,” `dfqzwqt`) show the template being maintained in public, while the Discord-selected bottom-left design (`6308wh`) shows that some choices were made elsewhere.

The third episode, bounded by `6315ic` and `633ehh` (d2 04-02 18:05 to d2 04-03 00:31), is attack, defense, and repair. “The Starry Night painting is being slowly attacked by the void” (`6315ic`) is followed by alarms, taunts, repair guides and calls to help. Participants say “We have to save it!” while preserving the moon (`dfqjx3y`), hand out a guide (`dfqluuv`), and define the border as the basis for rebuilding (`dfqo186`). Outside participants arrive from named groups; one says, “I left the void to help you guys repair” (`dfqn82j`). A post reports “Slowly we are pushing back Void” (`6320uc`) 2 hours 21 minutes after the alarm, and `633ehh` claims the Void moved on 6 hours 26 minutes after it. Those timings establish a sequence. The participant's own explanation in `633ehh` attributes the easing attack to internal conflict in the Void subreddit; the record does not independently establish that causal account.

The final episode begins six minutes after placement was disabled, with “Thanks for the help everyone!” at d3 04-03 17:06 (`6383p2`), and extends through the post-event accounting in `dft6dv4` at d3 04-04 10:48. Participants accept the ending (`6385ba`), claim “We did it!” (`638hvc`), offer a farewell centred on “hard work, respect, cooperation and perseverance” (`638i0l`), and imagine that “Van Gogh would be proud” (`63bwgi`). Imperfections are reinterpreted as “reminders of the difficulties we had” (`dfs31t6`). The last dispute is not a community reversal: an outside poster jokingly accuses the Knights of breaking a bargain (`63cy4y`), and commenters answer that they did help remake and maintain the squids (`dft6dv4`).

## Relations — what set off what

Several reply pairs support relations because the later words explicitly answer the earlier ones. When `dfqo2c6` calls the overlay “cheating,” `dfqox4n` answers 19 minutes later that “The barrier to helping for a defense against the void needs to be low. Otherwise the battle is already lost.” The criticism sets off a justification of the overlay as necessary for defense.

When MonaLisaClan offers help in `6328l7`, `dfqrorc` replies 42 minutes later, “If they threaten you again, we'll be there to help!” The offered assistance sets off an explicit promise of reciprocity. Likewise, TampaBayLightning asks to share the border in `634abd`, and `dfr8kv2` answers 8 minutes later by asking the proposer to join Discord “so we could talk about this more”; the request sets off movement of the negotiation to the off-platform venue. In the carrot-farm exchange, sympathy and fear for the farm in `631mor` receive “Then help us” in `dfqpdds` 1 hour 32 minutes later. The later turn directly converts sympathy into a request, while the next reply explains that the small community is already trying but is stretched across other problems (`dfqpiiv`).

Other developments are only sequences. The attack alarm in `6315ic` is followed 2 hours 21 minutes later by the pushing-back report in `6320uc`, then by the moved-on claim in `633ehh`; no passage shows that the alarm itself caused the recovery. Similarly, the repair offer in `dfqn56h` and the thanks in `dfqn65w` are adjacent and responsive, but they evidence acknowledgement of help, not that the thanks caused further repair. The corpus does not show a single decision that settled every template disagreement: later uncertainty in `633yds` persists after several template posts.

## Posts against comments

The posts often present a clear public line; comments show its qualifications.

The posts say that Discord members selected a “new bottom left” (`6308wh`); the comment layer says some participants cannot join Discord yet will still “protect and rebuild this area according to this plot” (`dfqxvme`). This is a descriptive contrast between where one choice was made and where instructions remained usable.

The post layer presents an official Tampermonkey template (`631wdk`); the comment layer contains both “Feels like cheating” (`dfqo2c6`) and the reply that low barriers are needed for defense (`dfqox4n`). The disagreement is explicit, but it does not show a community-wide resolution.

The posts repeatedly advertise finished or near-finished work (`62xycp`, `62y390`, `633dqs`); the comments repeatedly turn celebration back into continuing labour, including expanding the right side (`dfq5x28`), fixing a missing row (`dfqzwqt`), and defending after missing the original build (`dfr0duj`). The layers therefore tell the same broad story of shared work but differ in register: posts announce milestones and recruit attention, while comments expose correction, doubt, access problems, and the small exchanges that make repair possible.

The claim that the Void moved on lives mainly in the post layer (`633ehh`). Its comment layer reports that gaining ground felt easier and celebrates defense (`dfr0duj`), but does not independently verify the post's explanation about the attackers' internal conflict.

## Counter-reading

My reading is that the subreddit coordinated a shared artwork through common templates and reciprocal help; the Void attack shifted the room into defense and repair, after which participants remembered the result with pride.

If this were wrong, the record would instead repeatedly show that operative choices were made in Discord, that incompatible templates made subreddit participants undo one another's pixels, or that people unable to enter Discord could not participate in coordinated repair.

The strongest contrary item is `633yds`: “people keep adding and removing the same pixels,” the writer sees browns absent from their template, and asks whether it is correct. This directly limits any claim that the room achieved one settled visual plan. `6308wh` is also contrary evidence because it attributes a design choice to “the Discord members,” beyond the visible record.

The search tested the case's own terms for Discord-made decisions, the “same template,” adding and removing, and inability to join. It returned six items, all of which were read through the instrument. `dfraazo` adds that the commenter did not know whether anything had been decided; `dfqxvme`, however, says they cannot join Discord but will still rebuild “according to this plot.” The competing account therefore changes the reading from complete subreddit coordination to partial, distributed coordination: some decisions and live discussion were off-platform, and template mismatch caused rework, but the subreddit still carried actionable plots, repair instructions, help requests, corrections, and reciprocal commitments. The search did not find repeated evidence that exclusion from Discord prevented participation; within this small and partial record, that null result cannot establish that access was generally open.

## Saturation

Practice saturation: the latest distinct practice in this account appears with the post-event inspiration and remembrance material at `6341s6`; later threads extend saying goodbye, pride, migration, and retrospective accounting rather than opening another practice. The six themes therefore repeat across the end of the record, although this is saturation within the supplied window rather than a claim about the wider project.

Evidence saturation: the subreddit record is internally coherent enough to support the making–attack–repair–remembrance sequence, but it is not a complete record of coordination. Discord is explicitly where members “have spoken” (`6308wh`), where negotiations are redirected (`dfr8kv2`), and where bans are handled (`63376w`); those discussions are unseen. Twenty-nine bodies are deleted or removed, and several image or link posts retain only titles. These gaps limit details of decisions and visual outcomes, not the visible recurrence of templates, defense, help, and remembrance.

## Selection

I read the empty pre-week briefing and the complete openings file before triage, as the case-lead instrument requires. The openings showed that the supplied room begins well after the canvas opened and is almost entirely devoted to the Starry Night project. I then read every thread in corpus order. All 70 touched the event and were retained as coded; threads whose windows did not contain the whole discussion were opened in full. No thread was excluded as ordinary subreddit business or as a separate April Fools joke.

Titles on image and link posts were treated as participants' surviving published words, not as observations of the linked image. Automatic bot output was not coded as community conduct. Human actions around tools—sharing an overlay, asking for links, correcting coordinates, or summoning help—were retained.

## Absence

The archive contains 29 deleted or removed bodies. In some event posts the title survives and supports only the narrow action stated there: “We're being vandalised” (`6319vi`), “Fuck The Void” (`632o7h`), or the warning not to cover intended black (`6337km`). I did not reconstruct what their missing bodies may have said. Surviving replies sometimes preserve the shape of an exchange, but not the deleted speaker's reasons.

The pre-week briefing contains no archived items, so it cannot establish whether this room had prior vernacular, roles, or rituals. The record also cannot show the images themselves, off-platform Discord deliberation, or how named outside communities understood the relationship. Accordingly, visual fidelity, alliance symmetry, and who actually placed particular pixels remain outside the supported account.
