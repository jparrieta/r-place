## Chronology

The record opens with PCMR absorbed in its own April Fools makeover. At H+0.02, `62m2aw` calls it “the cruelest april fools joke ever”; members alternately hate, explain, extend, and work around Comic Sans, the “Inferior” relabelling, fake sponsors, and the moderators’ “technical difficulties” cover. That local episode remains visible through `dfq1faz` at H+41.08, where a member asks to “switch the font back.” It is not the canvas campaign: it is the room’s concurrent joke.

The canvas becomes visible in the room at H+1.79, when `62mrpa` announces “We’ve reached /r/place.” From there the slice accounts show repeated cycles rather than one settled project: members announce marks, summon people to coordinates, correct letters and colours, enlarge or relocate designs, and defend them after damage. The episode from the urgent “help needed” post `62qvq6` at H+13.49 through the later “on board” reply `dfomadq` at H+13.83 makes the public recruitment form especially clear. Later work diversifies into a relocated PCMR figure, Windows and hardware logos, an FPS counter, scripts and reference images, Discord rooms, and boundary talks. Long stretches also contain ordinary PC advice and April Fools posts; batches 30, 34, 37, 41–44, 46, 50, 52, 54, 67, 70, 72, 75–76, 78, 80–81, and 83 add no live event code, so the campaign was recurrent within a busy general forum, not the whole life of the room.

At H+73.38, after placement had stopped, `63882i` says “It’s over! We did our best.” What follows is not further placement in this record but interpretation and reuse: people dispute blame, salvage the FPS joke, share a fixed image and timelapse, upscale the canvas, plan posters and case designs, and imagine missing art. A replacement-canvas attempt runs from `6390j0` at H+75.46 (“New PCMR Space Is Being Drawn”) to `dfsah77` at H+76.55, where a commenter doubts it “will be as popular as place was.” The last event item, `63epg4` at H+95.24, shares a “full 72h” timelapse. The final slice then returns to visible hardware, gaming, support, giveaways, and routine moderation; that bounded edge does not prove all later event talk ceased.

In `62qvq6`, `dfolxps` explains that the banner “isn’t doing so well at maintaining the pattern”; 51 seconds later its direct reply `dfolyj1` says “I understand” and “I’ll see if I can help.” Other comments in the same episode report “fixed the S” at `dfom3e4` and “on board” at `dfomadq`. The teaching exchange is similarly explicit. `dfq4ba0` says “I want to participate” but does not know how to place a tile; `dfq4d44` and `dfq4m59` explain navigation and colour placement, and `dfq5gdt` closes the 52-minute sequence with “figured it out. LETS DEFEND OUR SPOT.” Here instruction supports reported participation. It does not establish what pixel was placed.

Neighbour negotiation sometimes progresses beyond adjacency. At H+64.07, `635eth` asks “Can we talk?” and proposes that Snek wrap around the PCMR neck. After 2.19 hours, `dfrjm3g` replies “We are all for co-op!” Later `dfrxw2b` offers “if you defend our logo. we shall defend yours,” and its direct reply `dfrxyxf`, 100 seconds later, answers “Trying our best!” This supports a reciprocal local accommodation and mutual-defence relation. By contrast, `dfou5e9` merely reports “I am in negotiation with the blue corner”; it is the participant’s explanation, not independently observed agreement.

Requests also change what becomes available after the event. `dfsz5q8` asks for the PNG to make a poster; 6.79 hours later, in the same reply branch, `dft8ftx` says “I edited in the link due to community request.” That wording supports the stated response. In contrast, the closure at `63882i` followed by fixed-image sharing at `dfsg6vh` and a timelapse at `dfsgr4w` is reported as an observed sequence: the record does not show that the closing post caused preservation.

No relation used here crosses a slice boundary. Relations elsewhere in the account are assembled from slice reports and the forest; because no single fleet reader held the whole week, cross-slice continuity remains weaker than these within-thread exchanges.

## Counter-reading

The competing account stated before search was that bots, scripts, macros, and overlays chiefly produced the apparent collective maintenance, leaving public calls as commentary rather than participation. If it were stronger, the record should contain repeated firsthand automation claims, failed public uptake, and outcome explanations crediting automation over responders and neighbours.

The strongest contrary item is `dfrtsy4`: “Users input a script into their browsers console that will automatically place a pixel in the correct spot. That is how most of the complex pieces are made.” `dfrpna5` reinforces it: “Most complex / pixel perfect stuff is made by bots that use lots of accounts.” The targeted search over r/place/canvas/pixel near bot, script, macro, overlay, or automated language also returned `62vvo2`, a public request for a script to rebuild and enlarge the PCMR sign, and `dfsb568`, which says “Only bots and large groups of people could really take advantage.” These items require a real qualification: automation was part of how participants understood complex work, and script requests and overlays belonged to the organising repertoire.

The alternative does not erase the observed human practices. `dfskc8e` explicitly says StarryKnights used an image overlay “but no bots,” while `dfolyj1`, `dfom3e4`, `dfomadq`, and `dfq5gdt` report helping, fixing, joining, and defending after public exchanges. The strongest conclusion is therefore mixed: the record supports automation claims and infrastructure, but not the causal claim that bots chiefly maintained PCMR’s marks or that public recruitment was merely after-the-fact talk. The broad automation query was truncated after 200 early ordinary-computing hits, so the focused searches tested event-adjacent language; absence outside that scope establishes nothing.

## Saturation

The first live code for playing along with, resisting, and working around the makeover, and for trying April Fools pranks, occurs in batch 02. Continuing ordinary PC talk appears in batch 08. Celebrating the canvas and designing PC-themed art first appear in batch 09; recruiting and teaching pixel work also first appears there after the append-only correction of one misgrouped makeover code. Repairing/defending and negotiating with neighbours begin in batch 12; moderating repeated r/place posts in batch 14; turning the canvas into PC jokes in batch 15; arguing over targets and restraint in batch 17; and tool/Discord coordination in batch 19.

Later slices do add new practices rather than merely repeat the opening repertoire. Accounting for loss and the eventual finish first appears in batch 20; preserving/reusing a canvas image in batch 38; imagining missing art in batch 60; and trying a replacement canvas in batch 68. Batches 66–82 deepen aftermath work through mourning, blame, files, posters, case designs, missed logos, jokes, and timelapses. The last coded batch is 82; batch 83 adds no event code. This is saturation for the record’s recurring practices, not proof of completeness: images and removed bodies are often unavailable, off-platform Discord/PM work is not observed, a single coder read each arbitrary slice, and long out-of-scope threads were triaged rather than exhaustively searched for stray mentions.

## Selection

This branch-B case was selected by a complete census and a threshold gate, then divided into 83 whole-thread slices in corpus order. The lead read the openings digest/titles before dispatch; one coder read and disposed each slice, and the lead consolidated the pooled codes, slice cases, facts files, ledger, and forest. Event-touching threads were opened in full by their slice coder; ordinary long threads could be triaged. The forest preserves reply structure and code coverage, but it does not make one person a reader of the whole week and reply adjacency alone was never treated as cause.

## Absence

There is no evidence here of a single stable PCMR command centre, a settled final design throughout the event, or a community-wide decision to abandon or embrace expansion. Discord servers, PMs, linked plans, images, and scripts are mentioned, but their off-platform exchanges and visual results are not present. Different speakers’ success, failure, blame, and restraint claims remain contrasting positions unless an exchange supports a relation. The final ordinary slice and several coded lulls bound the visible recurrence of r/place talk; they cannot establish that every member stopped discussing it or that no deeper item in a triaged thread mentioned it.
