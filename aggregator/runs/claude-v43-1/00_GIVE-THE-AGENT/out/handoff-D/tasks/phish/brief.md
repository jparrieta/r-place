## Vernacular

Members call a fan a “phan,” often without stopping to explain the spelling.

> Finding out someone is a phan, getting called out, etc. (`61v7fd`)

The “lot” is the social and merchandise scene around a show. To be “miracled” here means unexpectedly getting into a show, while a “lot shirt” is gear evaluated and bought in that setting.

> My friend and I both got miracled into the show (easy to make fakes back in the day). (`623bd6`)

> What do you guys look for in gear on lot? (`61t6e8`)

“1.0,” “2.0,” and “3.0” are period labels for the band, but this week uses them as shared shorthand rather than defining their boundaries.

> I was 16 at the time but really wish i got to see 1.0 or 2.0 Phish (`dfjeuzh`)

> No, it's from 3.0. (`dfegy50`)

Recordings are routinely labeled “AUD” and “SBD,” and “couch tour” is set alongside going to shows in person.

> Anyone Know Where G.R.A.B. AUD recordings can be found? (`62ew1s`)

> Been looking all over and only found the Bonnaroo set and the SBD that was released. (`62ew1s`)

> So you couch tour/go to shows but listen to the studio albums instead of the AUDs? (`dfh1sty`)

“PTBM” is ticket-lottery shorthand whose practical meaning is assumed: users discuss requests, cards, names, and duplicate wins without expanding the acronym.

> Address doesn't matter, My husband and I both put in for lottery every tour. (`dfkc141`)

## Register

The dominant register is casual, enthusiastic, and specific: dates and song names can stand as complete answers, while longer replies narrate concerts in emotional detail.

> 12/31/93 Hood. I get goosebumps just thinking about that peak (`dfic625`)

> Phish will definitely do that to you, no doubt :) (`dfjgg4v`)

Profanity is ordinary emphasis rather than exceptional language.

> Sick stash, Reba and an amazing Mike's> Kung> Mike's. Fucking fire (`dfffzdk`)

Disagreement is direct but often remains easygoing. A lot-shirt exchange moves from emphatic dislike to a short acknowledgment.
