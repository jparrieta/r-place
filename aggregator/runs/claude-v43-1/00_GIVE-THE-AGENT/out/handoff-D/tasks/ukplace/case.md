## Chronology

The supplied room does not show the canvas opening; its first visible episode begins at `632d0m` on d2 04-02 21:25 and runs through `dfriyuw` on d2 04-03 09:42. People were already coordinating a design through scripts: they debated a heart, negotiated around a Calgary Flames “C,” warned that AFK scripts made changes difficult, and then a moderator told people, “please do NOT use these scripts anymore” because they conflicted with a new one. The shared practice was not simply placing pixels but concentrating many placements behind a changing target whose control could be exercised through an external image (`dfqwdrs`).

From `635omh` on d2 04-03 09:25 through `dfryepa` on d3 04-03 16:39, the room combined a stickied master-script instruction with decisions about what the flag should contain and where its edges should stop. Members proposed tea, county flags, Big Ben and other additions, while visitors and participants disputed northward expansion. One participant admitted, “We were expanding north” (`dfrk2sw`); a Mexican visitor asked them to “please stop expanding your flag” (`636ted`); and another participant called reported cooperation against Mexico “shame to our flag” (`dfryepa`). These are contrasting positions in the same period, not evidence that the whole room changed its mind.

Placement ended inside the next episode, from `6386dg` at d3 04-03 17:16 through `6387dy` at d3 04-03 17:20, 19.85 hours after the first visible thread. Posts announced the end and displayed final UK/Britaly images. Comments inventoried what survived—“United Kingdom” remained legible and Devon and Somerset remained mostly intact (`dfs4jg0`)—while another participant supplied their own explanation for damage: the void plus “us trying to overwrite Mexico,” with the “brash and aggressive stance” of UK Discord and scripters costing the pristine text (`dfs9nbw`). This is a participant's causal claim, not an independently established causal relation.

A post-event reckoning followed. `638zb4` opened at d3 04-03 19:21, 2.08 hours after the end announcement, with “GO FUCK YOURSELVES” on behalf of people hit by scripts. The exchange ran to `dft3sh2` at d3 04-04 08:35, 13.23 hours later. Participants alternately defended the overwrite as a memorial, rejected that justification, invoked unowned space, and called for accountability. The main interlocutors eventually described the argument as “fun” and closed with tea, biscuits and a possible future defence pact (`dft3pgd`; `dft3sh2`). Moderator posts then addressed “all those aggrieved” (`63coxf`; `63cpxv`) and thanked Italian and Commonwealth allies (`dft3f72`). Finally, `63czfx` at d3 04-04 08:59—23.55 minutes after `dft3sh2`—half-rallied the room to a new space, while `63d37p` at d3 04-04 09:30 continued arguing over scripting after the original canvas had closed.

## Relations — what set off what

The clearest early relation is an explicit negotiation. A visitor reported moving a “C” but asked whether the UK text could be shrunk “to make peace” (`dfqs0fr`); the direct answer offered shrinkage if the visitors helped maintain the text (`dfqs6j5`). The reply content, not reply adjacency alone, establishes the proposed exchange.

Script conflict prompted a moderator instruction within the same thread: “please do NOT use these scripts anymore as they are conflicting with the new one” (`dfriyuw`). The words name both the problem and the requested response. Elsewhere, the sticky's call to move everyone to one new script (`635omh`) follows earlier AFK-script difficulties, but the corpus does not say that the earlier thread caused the sticky, so this is sequence rather than causation.

The Mexican appeal produced two different direct responses. “Please stop expanding your flag” (`636ted`) was answered first with “our flag hasn't expanded” (`dfrqunl`) and then with a suggestion to use Discord to work out an agreement (`dfrr8he`). The text supports denial and redirection; it does not establish that an agreement was reached off-platform.

The Tennessee complaint triggered competing justifications. The opening spoke for “Anyone you ran script on” (`638zb4`); a reply reframed the overwritten work as less important than a war memorial (`dfsa0t2`), and the complainant answered that the memorial was being used to hide “shit actions” (`dfsaefs`). A separate speaker initially said bots won unowned space (`dfsaq6b`) but later wrote, “the more I thought about it, you're right” and called the method “really shitty” (`dfsga5a`). That is a supported development in one participant's position, not a community-wide change.

The closing exchange also states its own relation. The complainant said the back-and-forth had been fun but was “growing old” and that they were leaving (`dft3pgd`); 4.4 minutes later the moderator agreed it had been fun and proposed a future pact (`dft3sh2`). Their words support mutual de-escalation. By contrast, later posts addressing aggrieved groups (`63coxf`; `63cpxv`) follow this exchange, but no passage says the exchange caused those posts.

## Posts against comments

The post layer often presents a single actionable object; the comment layer exposes disagreement about its reach.

- The Babymetal post reports a defence-only claim—people said they were “only running scripts to defend the UK Flag” (`635va6`)—while a comment says, “We were expanding north” (`dfrk2sw`). This is a descriptive contradiction between claim and admission.

- The moderator sticky presents the new script as “the absolute best way” to contribute (`635omh`). Comments beneath it warn that it may overwrite Fred Flintstone and make enemies (`dfrs2ng`) and repeatedly report that the Discord invitation is down (`dfruwpx`; `dfry5xd`; `dfs1pjd`). The post supplies a coordinated route; the comments show its social and access costs.

- The final-image post records a finished object (`6386w6`). Its comments supply two incompatible readings: survival and pride (`dfs4jg0`) versus the participant explanation that aggressive UK scripting cost the room its pristine text (`dfs9nbw`).

- The Tennessee post is unambiguously accusatory (`638zb4`). Its comments do not converge: some defend memorialisation or superior manpower (`dfsa0t2`; `dftmgqq`), while others distinguish respect for the memorial from criticism of displacement (`dfsokgl`). Only the comments contain the later friendly close (`dft3pgd`; `dft3sh2`).

## Counter-reading

My reading is that r/ukplace concentrated placement through a changing shared script and Discord, which helped build and maintain a national design but also allowed expansion to outrun neighbour-by-neighbour agreement; the resulting erasures were contested by outsiders and some participants, then partly converted into banter, thanks and retrospective justification.

If that reading were wrong, the record should instead contain sustained accounts of scripts only repairing an existing UK design, together with neighbours crediting that bounded defence or participants consistently treating expansion as fair play rather than stopping scripts, expressing shame, or blaming aggression for retaliation.

The strongest contrary item is `dft6xva`. It sympathises with lost art but calls the event “a pixel eat pixel world,” distinguishes UK “scripting” from botnets, and defends programmers because Reddit supplied an API. Searches across the supplied record for defence/repair/maintenance, expansion/overwriting/destruction, and fair-play language also returned a direct denial that the flag expanded (`dfrqunl`), a claim that r/place “allowed” displacement (`dft6mpw`), manual repair of county flags (`dfrygv9`), and a bounded instruction that the flag script must not overwrite the heart (`dfqrwwp`). Those findings strengthen the fair-play and repair side of the account and prevent treating criticism as consensus.

They do not overturn the reading. The same returned set contains an admission of northward expansion (`dfrk2sw`), a warning about overwriting Fred Flintstone (`dfrs2ng`), a call to stop incursions into Mexico (`637546`), and the participant explanation that aggressive UK scripting invited damage (`dfs9nbw`). No returned neighbour credits the room with consistently bounded defence. That null result applies only to these vocabulary-led searches of this 172-item window; the late-starting, partially deleted record and off-platform Discord prevent a broader absence claim.

## Saturation

The supplied Reddit record was read in full, but it is not a whole history. It starts on d2, the pre-week briefing contains no archived items, 27 bodies are deleted or removed, and a moderator sticky routes contributors to Discord (`635omh`). Reddit retains scripts, requests, disputes and reports of what came back, but not the off-platform decisions that changed targets or settled agreements. The record is therefore strong for visible Reddit conduct and weak for the full decision process.

Practice did not simply repeat in the late threads. The script-running, flag-design and boundary-negotiation themes were already present before placement ended, but the end introduced posting final images and inventorying survival, and the post-event period introduced addressing aggrieved groups, arguing about accountability, turning a row into banter, and tentatively rallying to another canvas. Conceptual saturation was not complete inside the window: genuinely new post-event practice still appeared near its end.

The grouping was reviewed after the chronology, relation analysis and counter-reading. No regrouping was needed: “arguing that scripts are fair play” remains distinct from “running one changing script from Discord” because the former justifies legitimacy while the latter operates the shared machinery; “calling out what the scripts wiped out” remains distinct from “negotiating who gets which pixels” because retrospective blame is not the same practice as proposing terms before or during overlap.

## Absence

Removed and deleted bodies are frequent enough to narrow claims but do not erase the main visible practices. Several deleted submissions retain informative titles—“Master UK Script - only use this one” (`632n5g`), “You all are a bunch of assholes” (`636h59`), and the end announcement (`6386dg`)—and surviving replies still show negotiation, scripting, disagreement and aftermath.
