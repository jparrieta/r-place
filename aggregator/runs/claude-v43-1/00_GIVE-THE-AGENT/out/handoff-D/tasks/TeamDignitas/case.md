## Counter-reading

Reading: TeamDignitas turned a small Yellow man into shared work by finding it, agreeing its design, maintaining it, and celebrating its survival, while containing most rivalry in jokes and trash talk.

What would unseat it: if attacking rivals were the more consequential shared practice, the record should contain people directing or reporting attacks, destruction, raids, trashing, or conversions as accomplished collective work—not only proposals that others contest.

The strongest contrary item is `dfofrpu`: “turning it into a dig logo rn.” It is action against TSM's work, not merely imagined trash talk. A search across the supplied record for the case's own attack vocabulary—attack, destroy, trash, vandal, raid, turning, ruin, betray, and bamboozle—returned ten items, each read individually. Most instead name threatened or completed damage to DIG (`dfohjmb`, `dfrew8k`, `dfrk2b6`, `636rxc`), reject vandalism (`dfofpa7`, `dfofxvs`), or sustain the Bakery repair joke (`631ygp`, `dfqxigo`). The search returned no item using “raid”; within a record of 66 in-window items, that null result establishes only that this word and an explicit raid report are absent from this small textual record.

The counter-reading changes the account by preserving one reported conversion and the initial pro-attack replies as real conduct. It does not overturn the larger pattern: the same exchange contains an explicit defense of others' hard work, later threads repeatedly organize repair and defense of DIG's own mark, and no searched item reports a collective raid or completed destruction by this room.
