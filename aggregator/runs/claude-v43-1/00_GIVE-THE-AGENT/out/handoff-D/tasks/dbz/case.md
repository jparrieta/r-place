## Chronology

When the window opened, r/dbz was mostly discussing Dragon Ball episodes, characters, art, games, and canon. Its first event-adjacent conduct was local April Fools play rather than canvas work: a commenter congratulated the admins because the “episode thing on the side” got them (`dfosq7a`), and later threads staged fake Dokkan and Dragon Ball news, played along, challenged whether contributions were jokes, and admitted being “rused” (`62qylr`–`dfq47rt`). These separate joke threads share a practice, but the record does not say that one caused another.

The first canvas episode was a small recruitment post at H+4.52. The author asked for help in the upper-left corner (`62nwg3`) and, 52 seconds later, corrected the coordinates and supplied a direct link (`dfnzhj5`). No other participant or outcome appears, so this episode ends at `dfnzhj5` unresolved.

The larger organising episode begins at H+29.28 with “Lets put dbz on r/place” (`62vk51`). Participants proposed Goku and Vegeta, started a foot, recruited helpers, protected the shoe, voted on Goku’s form, and tried to keep people from making solitary changes. They also bargained and fought with the Club Penguin builders and worked around Samus. A moderator later told people to consolidate place talk and not antagonise other communities (`dfq7qs4`). Separate posts nevertheless kept asking for aura, seven Dragon Balls, defense, repair, Vegeta, Dat Boi, and other targets (`62yufg`–`632hpd`); moderator replies repeatedly sent those proposals back to the ongoing thread (`dfq2fb0`, `dfq321h`, `dfq7os7`, `dfq7se0`, `dfqgqoq`). These calls demonstrate continuing parallel ideas, not successful parallel builds.

After “We lost it” at H+52.00 (`dfqmytz`), participants argued for another Goku, retaliation, or a smaller four-star Dragon Ball. The ball proposal appears 5 hours 36 minutes 51 seconds later (`dfr2ssg`), and participants began the simpler target (`dfr69ae`). These are contrasting responses by different speakers; they do not establish that everyone changed their mind.

At H+59.22 a moderator replaced the overcrowded thread with a general discussion thread (`6348mb`). Twenty-seven minutes four seconds later, a participant explicitly said, “Since our Goku got overrun,” they had started a simple four-star ball (`dfr8yr8`). The thread then records a ball, an r/dbz logo, a dragon radar, individual pixels, template enforcement, requests for neighbor clearance, and reports that the ball and radar were in place. When “grandpa’s dragonball” was reported destroyed (`dfrg66v`), a proposal to ask the One Piece sub for room and help followed 13 minutes 25 seconds later (`dfrgh3l`). Near the end, the moderator thanked participants, pointed out the ball and radar, and mourned Goku (`dfsflnf`). A later reply disputed a claim that both smaller images had been wiped out (`dft4eqa`), so the record preserves conflicting reports rather than one uncontested outcome.

The last carry-forward act came 3 hours 43 minutes 1 second after the moderator’s closing comment: a participant said they had rebuilt the four-star ball on the “new canvas” at r/PlaceTwo and invited help (`dfsqlbt`). Uptake is not visible. Meanwhile, the new threads assigned to batches 13–15 had already returned to ordinary Dragon Ball discussion and routine moderation. That is an observed overlap and sequence: it does not show that the event caused ordinary talk to resume or that all replies to inherited r/place threads had stopped.

## Relations — what set off what

The clearest supported relation is the target change after loss. “Since our Goku got overrun” explicitly explains starting the simpler four-star ball (`dfr8yr8`); this is the participant’s own explanation, not a causal inference from timing. Earlier, the announcement “We lost it” (`dfqmytz`) receives competing answers: make another Goku (`dfqu85x`, `dfr3xb2`), grief Club Penguin (`dfr3n2b`), or make a four-star ball (`dfr2ssg`). Those answers establish a response episode but not a collective decision.

Destruction of the smaller target also prompted outreach in the same exchange: after blaming “the Swedes” for destroying the ball (`dfrg66v`), a reply proposes asking the One Piece sub for room and help (`dfrgh3l`). The passage supports renewed alliance-seeking; it does not establish who destroyed the ball or whether One Piece agreed.

Duplicate proposal posts prompted consolidation. “Post it to the ongoing thread” answers the aura proposal (`62yufg`, `dfq2fb0`), while “Let’s keep it in one thread” answers the scattered-balls proposal (`62yybj`, `dfq321h`). Near the event’s end, “We already have a thread here” answers another Goku request (`636zsx`, `dfrr5nd`), and the author backs off 26 seconds later with “ah well nevermind then!” (`dfrr6b6`). This last exchange shows the redirect changing that speaker’s stated plan; it does not prove that every late proposal was abandoned.

Neighbor relations changed what builders said they would do. A request to “come to an agreement” (`dfpuz40`) was developed into shared space and mutual protection (`dfpvdo9`), while another participant threatened to grief Goku (`dfpx1yu`). Later, template enforcement was explicitly justified by a “tenuous truce” (`dfrqdwu`). The cooperative and hostile positions belong to different speakers, so they are a descriptive contrast, not evidence that the community first agreed and then reversed itself.

Other sequences lack a supported causal link. The early upper-left effort (`62nwg3`) precedes the larger Goku thread (`62vk51`), and the Megaman, spirit-bomb, Vegeta, Dat Boi, and Saiyan God calls follow it, but none says the earlier effort set it off. They are reported as following, not causing, one another.

## Posts against comments

In batch 04, posts supplied apparent news, a fan trailer, and fake facts, while comments made the prank frame readable. The sharp contradiction is in `62smsf`: a commenter lets the trailer “slide” because it is April Fools (`dfoxjz1`), but its maker says it was made earlier and was not for April Fools (`dfp68st`). A similar sincerity test occurs when a low arc rating is treated as a possible joke and the poster instead explains the experience of watching weekly (`dfp49o7`, `dfp62pc`).

In batch 05, the edited main post presents a cooperative line—stay together and do not fight Club Penguin—while comments include bargaining, demands to move, and a threat to spend “all day and night” griefing Goku (`dfpx1yu`). The posts and comments therefore contradict one another about how orderly and cooperative the effort was. The comment layer also carries most operational work: coordinates, protection, votes, boundaries, loss, and rebuilding.

In batches 09–10, posts repeatedly propose aura, balls, repair, Vegeta, and Dat Boi; comments mainly manage where that talk belongs. One author says flair-only handling left them “confused as fuck for nearly an hour” (`dfqu7fe`), which qualifies any account of consolidation as frictionless. The record does not identify who applied that flair.

In batch 11, the general-thread post displays successful updates, while comments record erasure, rebuilding, truce maintenance, and disagreement over what survived. The post layer’s stable summary and the comment layer’s repeated repair are different views of the same work, not proof that one is false.

## Counter-reading

My reading is that r/dbz’s canvas work coalesced imperfectly around a moderator-consolidated Goku effort, then a smaller four-star ball and radar, while standalone proposals continued around it.

A plausible competing account is that the apparent shared-thread sequence overstates coordination and participation actually remained parallel standalone projects. If that account were stronger, the record should contain people rejecting or bypassing the shared thread, successful independent Vegeta, Dat Boi, Saiyan God, spirit-bomb, or other targets, or repeated uptake of those projects without reference to the main effort.

The strongest contrary item is `dfr48jv`: “wish mods would let us post new r/place threads,” followed by the claim that the speaker could post “a plan for everyone to follow.” It is a direct objection to single-thread handling and required a late code. Targeted searches also found two previously uncoded calls to “Make another goku” inside the general thread (`dfrd7yp`, `dfrdw13`), as well as standalone calls for Megaman (`62wikz`), sunbro’s spirit bomb (`62xsbj`), Vegeta (`630siu`), Dat Boi (`631dge`, `632hpd`), and a Saiyan God section (`637ksb`). These findings change the account by making dissent and continuing alternatives more prominent.

The main reading nevertheless survives in qualified form. The two newly found Goku calls sit inside the shared general thread, while the targeted standalone items show calls but no visible uptake or completed independent target. In contrast, redirects name the ongoing thread (`dfq2fb0`, `dfq321h`, `dfrr5nd`), and the duplicate Goku requester explicitly withdraws (`dfrr6b6`). The search returned no successful independent target in that vocabulary. This null result is limited to the archived, text-searchable record: removed bodies, deleted replies, linked images, and off-platform activity mean it cannot establish that no independent work occurred.

## Saturation

The themes appeared in a bounded sequence. “Playing along with April Fools fakes” begins in batch 01, expands through batches 03–06, and receives no later code. “Rallying people to put Dragon Ball on the canvas” begins in batch 02 and recurs from batch 05 through batch 12. “Building and policing the shared pixel design,” “bargaining and fighting over canvas space,” “keeping r/place talk in one thread,” and “replacing lost Goku with a smaller Dragon Ball” all first appear in batch 05; later batches repeat or extend them, especially batches 09–12. “Taking stock and carrying the pixel work onward” first appears in batch 11 and is extended in batch 12. Batches 13–15 add no fresh event code and instead sustain ordinary Dragon Ball traffic.

The record is strong for the long threads `62vk51` and `6348mb`, which were read whole and preserve proposals, replies, conflict, repair, and closure. It is thinner for many short calls whose bodies are removed or whose replies are deleted. Images and canvas links are not themselves readable evidence. A strawpoll carried voting outside the thread and returned only a choice; r/dbz Discord users are reported to have “got together” and produced a radar and ball (`dfrr5nd`), but their deliberation is unavailable. These gaps limit claims about uptake and design choice without erasing the visible practices.

After writing the account, I revisited the grouping. It held without regrouping: recruitment, shared construction, neighbor bargaining, thread consolidation, replacement after loss, and retrospective carry-forward remained distinct practices even when the same item could mention the same canvas vocabulary.

## Selection

All 499 in-window threads were disposed across 15 whole-thread slices. Threads were retained when their surviving text showed r/place conduct or r/dbz’s own April Fools practice; ordinary episode discussion, power debates, fan art, merchandise, games, and routine moderation were excluded. Long excluded windows carry exact exclusion citations, and every coded thread retains at least one live code. Context openings from earlier slices oriented coders but were not disposed or coded again.

The case keeps the local April Fools material separate from canvas conduct because no passage connects the two. It also retains unresolved calls and removed-body titles at the level their surviving words support, without converting a proposal into a completed project.

## Other communities named

r/place is the canvas venue throughout. r/bannedfromclubpenguin and r/ClubPenguin appear in negotiation, relocation, and hostile talk around overlapping drawings (`dfpuz40`, `dfq1en6`). r/TheBlueCorner is first asked for help and later discussed as a treaty partner; a visitor apologises and offers rebuilding help (`dfrtf31`), and another late post offers Goku art in exchange for “taking the blue side” (`635i18`). r/metroid is involved in boundary-setting around Samus (`dfqftl5`).

Later participants negotiate or seek help from r/RivalsOfAether, r/greenlattice, and r/OnePiece (`dfr98bb`, `dfrdmq8`, `dfrgh3l`). r/placestart is asked to carry a ball (`dfr69ae`), r/woahdude hosts a time-lapse (`dfrakue`), and r/PlaceTwo hosts the later rebuild (`dfsqlbt`). These are statements within r/dbz; they do not establish the other communities’ views or outcomes.

The strawpoll partially moved the choice of Goku’s form off-thread, while the result returned to the edited post. The r/dbz Discord is the clearest moved organising venue: `dfrr5nd` says its users got together and made a radar and ball at 900,200, but the record cannot show their deliberation. dbz.space and Kanzenshuu support April Fools material rather than organising canvas work.

## Absence

No surviving passage establishes uptake for the early upper-left image, the Megaman clash, the spirit-bomb appeal, Vegeta, Dat Boi, the late alliance offer, or the Saiyan God section. This is absence within the archived record, not proof that nobody placed pixels. Several opening bodies are removed, some replies are deleted, and linked images are unavailable.

The record also does not show that ordinary traffic caused event talk to end, that the community reached one view about rebuilding Goku, or that bluecorner, Club Penguin, Metroid, greenlattice, One Piece, or other named communities understood relations as r/dbz participants described them. The late ordinary slices support a descriptive return to normal subjects; inherited threads and off-platform venues limit any stronger absence claim.
