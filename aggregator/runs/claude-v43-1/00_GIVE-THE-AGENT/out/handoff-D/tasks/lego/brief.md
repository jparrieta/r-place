## Vernacular

`MOC` names a builder's own design. One participant defined `MOC-er` directly.

> I'm not so much a set builder, but a MOC-er (meaning, I build my own designs), but I do have six of the Modulars on display. (`dfnfbzk`)

`Dark age` or `dark ages` names a period when someone leaves the hobby before returning.

> When someone was interested in Lego, stopoed, and came back. Dark ages is the term for the period of non interest. (`dfiud1e`)

An `illegal` build is a disputed label for a connection outside intended tolerances or one that strains or breaks pieces.

> It's just a term used to describe build methods that Lego didn't intend, usually because it's outside piece tolerances or literally requires breaking pieces to make them fit. (`dfgb4dj`)

`SNOT` and `LDD` appeared as unexplained technical shorthand in a building-technique thread.

> It's SNOT. I'll try to screen cap the LDD so you can see it in better detail. (`dfcv67k`)

`White whale` names a long-sought set or figure. `Haul` names a batch of acquired Lego.

> This is my white whale. I wanted it back then but was in my "Lego is for kids, right?" phase. (`dfdf49l`)

> Just the instruction book, still a nice haul nonetheless the white whale still eludes. (`dfn08zf`)

`AFOL`, `CMF`, and `UCS` also appeared repeatedly without reliable expansions. One comment used `AFOL` as a proud self-label.

> Superb detail. Mocs like these make me proud to be an AFOL even if it's out of my league (`dfhx9zt`)
