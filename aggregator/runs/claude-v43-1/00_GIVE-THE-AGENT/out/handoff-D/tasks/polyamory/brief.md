## Vernacular

“Poly” is ordinary shorthand inside the room and is explicitly called jargon in a thread devoted to whether other shortenings should replace it.

> That's not how language works, poly is already a jargon term. (`dfcxub9`)

“NRE” is expanded in the record as “New Relationship Energy” and tied to the feeling of a new crush.

> Being addicted to the dopamin fuelling feelings of new crushes (NRE = New Relationship Energy). (`dffjjup`)

“Polycule” names a larger connected set of partners, while “meta” and “metamour” name a person connected through a partner. One weekend account supplies the network in ordinary use, and another marks a partner's new connection as creating a metamour.

> Spent Thursday night with my girlfriend, then movie night Friday night with what I think of as the whole polycule -- my wife and girlfriend, their partners and metas. (`dfgrs2e`)

> They are planning to continue to see each other, so I now officially have a metamour!  Yay! (`dfhap91`)

In one sustained advice exchange, “rules” and “boundaries” were treated as different things: rules govern someone else's actions, while boundaries govern one's own.

> By permission based I mean based on rules instead of boundaries. Rules are imposed to control the actions of others, boundaries are about controlling the actions of the self. (`dfecuc2`)
