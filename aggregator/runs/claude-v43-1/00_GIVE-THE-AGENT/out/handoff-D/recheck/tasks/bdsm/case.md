The first visible r/place episode begins much later, at d1 04-02 02:43, when `62xc1c` shared “a small tribute to bdsm on /r/place.” This episode runs from `62xc1c` to `dfqylen`. People cheered, claimed that nearby hearts turned black “thanks to our influence” (`dfpu8ny`), watched surrounding flag fights, and reported that the sign had fallen. On a black background”—and insisted “Spain can't have it”; 1.7 hours later, the direct reply `dfqylen` said “i think it got destroyed.” This is an observed exchange and outcome report, not evidence that the instruction caused the destruction.

A second episode, from `633n2g` to `dfrpotd`, began at d2 04-03 01:16 with an outside offer: “/r/placehearts is working on a heart for you” at supplied coordinates. Discussion folded that heart into the room's existing sign. `dfr8fxa` mourned that the “BDSM! Sign was destroyed,” while its direct reply `dfrb8fz`, 1.1 hours later, announced “It's back” at a new location. `dfree84` asked for chains and “something a bit more dark,” and `dfrpotd` later pointed to the sign “along with a nice lil' lock :).” After that, the supplied window returns to ordinary BDSM posts; for example, after placement had closed, `63afl5` asked at d3 04-03 23:15 what style people preferred a submissive to wear. The record therefore shows a short canvas episode inside a much larger ordinary week, not a wholesale shift in what the community discussed.

## Relations — what set off what

The clearest supported response begins with loss. `dfqly6u` said the tribute had “fallen to the Spanish onslaught”; its direct reply `dfqqmne`, 1.8 hours later, proposed moving it near the flag and invited a chat on Discord. The later passage explicitly answers the placement problem, so the response relation rests on its words as well as adjacency. Its claim that a bot was redrawing the work is the visitor's own explanation, not independently established bot use.

The heart offer in `633n2g` set off a design request: the direct reply `dfree84`, 4.9 hours later, asked “can we get one with chains or something a bit more dark?” Here the wording clearly answers what kind of heart should be made. By contrast, `dfrpotd`'s later pointer to a sign with a lock follows the request by 7.5 hours, but the speaker never says the lock was made because of that request; it is only a sequence.

Two corrections update loss reports without establishing causes. `dfqylen` answers `dfqtsub` by saying the sign “got destroyed,” and `dfrb8fz` answers `dfr8fxa`'s mourning with “It's back.” These exchanges support changing status reports—destroyed, then back at another location—not a claim that the community collectively changed its mind or that any particular speaker rebuilt it.

## Posts against comments

The posts say that a BDSM tribute existed (`62xc1c`) and that r/placehearts was making a heart for the room (`633n2g`). The comment layer says the artwork was unstable: it supplies alternative coordinates, reports destruction and return, situates the sign amid Spanish and French flag conflict, and asks for a darker chained design. This is a descriptive contrast between celebratory or helpful openings and comments preoccupied with keeping track of changing artwork.

One claim lives only in comments: `dfpu8ny` says black hearts appeared “thanks to our influence.” Neither opening post claims that influence, and the corpus provides no independent demonstration of it. Likewise, the allegation in `dfqqmne` that “you're using a bot to re-draw it” is a participant's accusation, not a supported causal account of how the sign persisted.

## Counter-reading

My reading is that r/bdsm's r/place practice was a small effort to mark the canvas with a BDSM sign and related imagery, carried mainly through location and status updates and responses to destruction rather than broad community mobilisation.

If that reading were wrong, the record would contain calls for many members to place or defend pixels, repeated rebuilding instructions, recruitment, or other explicit evidence of community-wide coordination.

The strongest contrary item is `dfqtsub`: “Go to 335, 767,” followed by the intended letters, colours, background, and “Spain can't have it.” It is unmistakably an instruction to act, so the account must include local coordination rather than describe the room as merely observing. It does not overturn the reading: it is one speaker's instruction in one of two event threads, and the more targeted corpus-wide search for `pixel`, `canvas`, `defend`, `rebuild`, `coordinate`, `mobiliz*`, `help us`, or `join us` returned no items. That null result is scoped to the supplied 308 in-window items and cannot exclude a stray buried mention in a long thread that triage did not open. The other returned contrary passages—`dfpu8ny`, `dfqqmne`, `633n2g`, and `dfrb8fz`—show persistence, outside help, and proposed negotiation, but no broader recruitment.

## Saturation

Practice saturation is narrow. The second event thread repeats the first thread's sign-loss, return, and location updates, while adding one new practice: accepting and reshaping a heart offered by r/placehearts. No later thread in the supplied window was retained as event conduct, so the record settles around sign maintenance, commentary on conflict, a design exchange, and one proposed Discord negotiation. With only two event threads, this is saturation of a small trace rather than evidence of a large or fully elaborated organising system.

Record saturation is limited in specific ways. Fifty-two corpus items have deleted or removed text, including a deleted bridge inside `62xc1c`; surviving replies still preserve the Spanish-conflict exchange around it. The Discord invitation in `dfqqmne` leaves any actual negotiation and decision unseen. The corpus reports 211 of 212 present parent links, but triage deliberately did not expose every reply in long out-of-scope threads, so absence of further mobilisation is suggestive within this record, not proof that no isolated mention existed.

## Selection

I read the briefing and all 86 thread openings before triage, then followed the corpus in its fixed order. Threads whose windows touched r/place were opened in full. `62xc1c` was retained because its title names r/place and its comments describe the BDSM sign; `633n2g` was retained because it offers a r/placehearts image and its comments continue the sign episode. All other threads were disposed as ordinary BDSM advice, images, advertisements, or discussion unrelated to the canvas. Every read out-of-scope thread with ten or more in-window items carries its exact exclusion citation.

## Other communities named

`62xc1c` names r/place as the venue of the tribute. `633n2g` names r/placehearts as giving help by making a heart for r/bdsm. The record shows the offer and r/bdsm's request for a darker, chained design, but not the other community's internal decision-making. `dfqqmne` also invites participants to a Discord server to discuss moving the artwork; whether anyone accepted is not visible.

## Absence

Deleted material does not erase the central episode: the retained words still show the sign's coordinates, colours, reported destruction, return, and the proposed heart. One deleted comment (`dfqqm1x`) bridges a joking “inquisitions” reply and `dfqratd`'s “spanish civil war” description, so no substantive relation is inferred across that missing turn. There is no visible moderator direction, sticky, mass call to participate, or post-event reckoning in the coded event material; given the small and partially triaged record, these are bounded absences rather than claims about everything community members may have done elsewhere.
