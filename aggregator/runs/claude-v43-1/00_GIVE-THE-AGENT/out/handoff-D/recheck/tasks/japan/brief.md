One reply explicitly called sento “public baths,” but the exchange also showed that participants could disagree about whether water or atmosphere made the distinction; this brief therefore keeps the terms rather than forcing a fuller definition.

> Also, any simple community sento, or a super sento? (`dfgd60z`)

> I'd define onsen versus sento based on the water, not the ambiance. (`dfgthm5`)

## What I could not tell

The opened record did not establish translations or expansions for *OLs* or *shisha kosho*, and it did not supply one stable tone or definition for *gaijin*. The quotations in Vernacular show the observed scope; anything more would require knowledge outside the supplied corpus.

The community's actual demographic composition could not be established. One participant described r/Japan as mostly expatriates and Japan-interested users with relatively few Japanese participants, but that is one user's framing rather than a census.

> Just so there's no misunderstanding, most of the people here on r/Japan are expats who live/lived in Japan and people who like Japan, there's only a limited amount of actual Japanese people. (`dfmrjpv`)

The opened sample also could not establish a scheduled recurring thread, the origin date of the JCJ bingo joke, or whether the visible high-volume authors held stable roles beyond the kinds of replies quoted above. With 190 threads unopened, claims of uniqueness or absence would exceed the evidence. (state)
