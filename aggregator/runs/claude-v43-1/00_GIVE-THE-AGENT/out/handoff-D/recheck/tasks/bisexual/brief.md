## Vernacular

“Bi-cycle,” also written “bicycle” and “bi cycle,” was the clearest repeated in-group term in the opened material. Speakers used it for periods in which attraction leans more toward one gender and then another.

> It is very normal for your sexuality to change in cycles, where in one period you like one gender more and in another period you like the other more (bicycle :)) (`dfe9uvt`)

One relationship post called a strong same-gender phase a “gay day.” That wording was visible in this thread and should not be assumed to be universal across the community.

> Do any of you get very gay days even when in a straight relationship or vice versa? (`61se2p`)

“Bi erasure” named the denial that bisexuality is a valid reading of someone’s attractions. The term was explicit in a parent-child exchange rather than supplied from outside the record.

> Also, I went on another site a few months ago and everyone on it just told me I was in denial about being a lesbian and that caused me a lot of anxiety. Do a lot of lesbians not believe in bisexuality? (`dfg60eh`)

> Yes totally! I think bi erasure is a pretty big thing. (`dfgh2c5`)

## Register

The opened advice threads were commonly warm and direct. One commenter explicitly invited the poster to stay in the community:

> Come hang out with us! This sub is super welcoming and fun. (`dfmrmy5`)

Warmth did not imply softness. Advice could be blunt and profane when a commenter thought personal safety or dependence was at stake.

> A lot of people are big fans of only telling the truth and coming out as fast as possible. That's fucking stupid if it is going to derail your life. (`dflhdt9`)

Disagreement could also be direct while still addressing another person’s reasoning.

> Your experiences and mine may be different - I left ample room for that in my comment - but you do not get to use your experiences to negate mine. (`dfd0btd`)

Humour included profane, sharply drawn storytelling rather than only reassurance.

> Fuck you, Suzy. It's not my fault she viewed you platonically. (`dfersj8`)

Posters sometimes returned after many replies to thank the group collectively, making the advice threads feel conversational rather than like isolated answers.

> A lot of responses, would love to respond to them all - thanks all for your kind words, each comment helped out a lot :) (`dfmn9cj`)
