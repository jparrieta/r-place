## Evidence base

The supplied archive contains 1,430 items in 133 threads. Body text was served from 36 of those threads; 345 of 1,430 items were seen, and all 345 were complete. All seven active calendar days were touched, and there were no empty days. I deliberately left the remaining thread bodies unopened; the title index was sampled rather than exhausted, and no served item remained partial. (state)

## Vernacular

“Visa run” is used for leaving Thailand and returning under a new entry permission.

> Exiting the country in order to get a new entry visa thus extending your time allowed in the country, popular visa runs seem to be to Laos or Cambodia. (`dfkb4nt`)

“Farang” appears as the label for foreigners in Thailand.

> Everyone uses these services for the same reasons - I'm not sure why you're differentiating between locals and foreigners, or why you seem surprised that 'even locals' are using them. Why wouldn't they? You think Uber set up shop here just to ferry farangs around? (`dfgq7jt`)

“Sexpat” is a hostile label, placed against “tourist areas” in one argument rather than used neutrally.

> sexpat and tourist areas are not completely interchangeable; tourist areas are scam heavy, sexpat areas amp it up even more. (`dfdsfzk`)

Local transport references are compressed into labels such as “BTS” and “MRT”; the opened record uses the labels without spelling them out.

> Now if this city could just figure out a payment card that is accepted by ALL public transports.. buses, taxis, BTS, MRT. (`dfi5814`)

“555” appears as a complete reaction, but the served text does not define it.

> 555 (`dfd3wen`)

## Recurring formats

Practical question threads recur in the opened sample: finding housing and receiving international post are two clear examples.

> Website/app to find apartments on rent/sale? (`61dbcg`)

> How safe is posting packages to Thailand? (`61j16i`)

In these opened examples, answers combined personal experience with a concrete recommendation rather than a formal FAQ response.

> Address in English is no problem. I have received everything I ever got sent here (maybe 50 items) and the address is always in English. (`dff30wx`)

> There's also http://www.findyourspace.co which I like most because of the way you do the search directly from the map. (`dfgm349`)

At least two opened posts consisted of a headline as the complete body.

> Bangkok BRT Won't Shut Down; Fares Increase to 15 Baht (`61s31k`)

> Public transport vans are going on strike from April 1 over the new safety regulations (`62civy`)

The 36 served threads did not establish a named daily, weekly, or other scheduled ritual. Because 97 threads were not served, that is an opened-sample limit rather than a claim about the full archive. (state)

## Register

The register changes sharply by thread. Practical advice can be plain, specific, and openly appreciated.

> I'll go with regular post then. Cheers for the info. (`dff5g0l`)

Correction can also include an explicit concession and an attempt to keep bad information from spreading.

> Ah. Thanks for explaining. I definitely misread the news about all of that. I should probably delete my posts so it doesn't confuse anyone. (`dffckwj`)

In contentious threads, disagreement becomes profane or personally dismissive.

> You have no idea what you are talking about and seriously you should fuck off out of here. (`dfe5ohh`)

> Your reading comprehension is like a rock. (`dfltq48`)

An elementary visa question drew at least two ribbing replies in that thread.

> What's a visa run and why do people do it? (`61z6fa`)

> Sigh! (`dfjcdyh`)

> What's google? (`dfjvyxz`)

This evidence supports an inconsistent register: helpful and self-correcting in some exchanges, abrasive in others. It does not establish that every basic question or every disagreement receives the same treatment. (state)
