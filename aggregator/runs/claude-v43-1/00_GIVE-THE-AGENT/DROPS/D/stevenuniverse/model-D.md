## D01 Critical mass

Calls recruited hands for several fan images rather than one settled project: “We should make SU stuff in Place” became a located Diamond Authority proposal, while a separate starter requested help constructing Peridot [R01] [P01] [c0001] [c0002] [c0093]. Proposing and recruiting sometimes occupied the same turn rather than successive stages [A02] [c0001] [c0093]. The representation call received “I'm trying to help” and completion reports; these support written uptake, not an estimate of silent placers or proof that the call alone caused construction [P15] [A12] [A01] [c0013] [c0016] [c0019].

Capacity was assessed against particular designs and threats: one respondent calculated that an oversized design needed 67 people, another found the whale hard to keep alive while asleep, and defenders demanded more people [K02] [c0203] [c0265] [c0447]. These are participants' estimates, not measured workforce requirements [K02] [c0203].

## D03 Maintenance

Completion immediately carried an upkeep obligation: “We did it! Keep at it though so it doesn't get swept away” and “guard it from trolls ( make repairs)” explicitly connect achievement with threatened loss [P05] [A03] [c0020] [c0022]. Upkeep recurred alongside construction rather than replacing it: later helpers were asked to restore, maintain and expand concurrently [P05] [P02] [A03] [c0269].

Repair required interpreting the intended image: an alarm that the symbol was being ruined received “it's just pink being shattered,” while later instructions asked people not to restore Pink over Polandball's text [P06] [P05] [c0182] [c0183] [c0314]. Encroachment was disowned as “splinter people not representing us”; this is a participant's attribution, not independent identification of every placer [P06] [c0394].

## D04 Order without law

These establish reported bargains, not universal compliance [P08] [c0307] [c0308]. Reciprocal help included a Slovak apology answered by a promise to repair its flag, and a whale-for-Goose protection offer [P16] [A14] [A13] [c0260] [c0261] [c0377] [c0380]. Calling CK, Paradox and Polandball for defense and reporting CK chat support instantiate coalition activity without establishing that the present threat created those ties [P17] [A19] [c0381] [c0382].

Agreements supplied explicit reasons for restraint: a truce announcement ordered attacks to stop, while instructions not to extend Pink into Polandball were followed by a report of restoring its letters against un-shattering [P07] [A05] [c0284] [c0353] [c0365]. Restraint depended on reaching and persuading placers: “haven't gotten memo” differs from “I don't give a fuck about peace” [P21] [A21] [A05] [c0308] [c0410]. Ignorance, design misunderstanding and deliberate refusal therefore cannot all be collapsed into disputes over representatives' authority [P21] [P06] [c0362] [c0314] [c0410].

The pink T was attributed to SU Discord permission and then altered during three-way negotiations: a limited guest arrangement, not sovereign ownership [P19] [c0356]. Moves also varied: Rose's flag was relocated around friendly art, whereas defenders later prioritized survivors before restoring lost gems [P10] [c0251] [c0445]. Shortage did not require relocation: one participant advised giving up while others still sought restoration and more people [A10] [K02] [c0396] [c0445] [c0447].

## D05 Collective identity

Lore supplied designs and standards of conduct: Cookie Cat represented rebellion, while the cracked Diamond and “what would steven do” justified giving Polandball space [K04] [P02] [P07] [c0153] [c0294]. Lore did not prescribe one stance: “Friendship doesn't solve all life's problems” defended war, while “You're not being very Steven” reproached an angry participant [K04] [P09] [P07] [c0337] [c0410] [c0411].
