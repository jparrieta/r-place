## D01 — Critical mass

Mobilization meant repeated calls to build, expand and rescue a tricolour, not one founding summons [P01] [c0015] [c0069] [c0070]. The starter role is visible retrospectively: Tech46 recalls taking up an existing tiny flag, while a participant credits his first thread and he answers that he copied ideas and needed everyone's participation [R01] [c0408] [c0420] [c0421]. A lurker explicitly reports being drawn out to defend, supporting recruitment into placing rather than merely inferring it from nearby posts [A01] [c0412].

Bare answers occur: “Following your lead capt'n!” answers the instruction to expand green and orange before white, and “I'll help with border” answers a border proposal [P15] [A12] [c0038] [c0039] [c0101] [c0102]. These pairs instantiate written acceptance; they cannot establish what every reader owed, how silent readers placed, or a community-wide preference distribution [A12] [c0039] [c0102]. The Irish-language thread also explains the canvas rather than recruiting only through the main planning venues [T4-003] [c0009] [c0010]. Memorial calls show side causes, without evidence that the calls recruited workers [P01] [c0158] [c0202].

Scarcity shapes choices: a participant rules out war with America because “We don't have the manpower,” while another reduces a proposed Paddy picture to “30+” for lack of space [K02] [c0503] [c0283]. Neither is evidence of moving after erasure: relocation remains unobserved [absent P10] [absent A10] [c0283] [c0478] [c0503].

## D02 — Coordination without command

The flag was a revisable work, not a settled specification: members corrected proportions, reported a temporary completed tricolour and proposed further emblems [P02] [P03] [c0025] [c0164] [c0121]. Coordinates and a black outline made common work locatable and legible [P04] [c0070] [c0165]. A concept was requested before another pint was built, but this is not evidence that a posted template permanently settled design talk [A11] [c0237] [c0238].

Automation added repair capacity and became an object of criticism [P14] [c0142] [c0284] [c0285]. It also preserved the wrong state: “Our own bots are stopping our progress,” a border heart had to wait for updated scripts and morning, and the final megathread said to stop outdated scripts [P14] [c0238] [c0269] [c0270] [c0366]. Thus standardization could block redesign; the design-to-template arrow needs a condition that human and automated plans are current together [A11] [c0238] [c0270]. Updating scripts to permit the heart links machine adjustment to sparing neighbours, not just to producing more placements [P14] [P07] [c0247].

The saved-edit exchange adds a distinct evidential step: after a claimed British-flag alteration, a reply explains that pixels in a second tab may not save; the poster checks an incognito window and reports partial persistence and subsequent loss [new: checking whether an apparent edit persisted] [c0169] [c0175] [c0176] [c0177]. This refines workarounds rather than treating a displayed edit as verified placement [P14] [T3-074] [c0175] [c0176].

Organization included explicit acts: an “Ambassador” was designated, a sanction threatened bans for writing “UP DA RA,” and moderators locked and redirected a thread [T4-011] [c0136] [c0139] [c0249]. This room is a counter-instance to an organizationally undecided local reading, not a disproof of the global claim about *most* rooms [C01] [c0139]. Moving coordination into Discord was offered as a way to stop people working against one another, but the archive records the instruction, not the missing chat's full decisions [T4-010] [c0244].

## D03 — Maintenance

Maintaining a vulnerable work repeatedly competed with enlargement: defence was declared the main focus, repair calls followed sabotage, and night workers were credited with preventing loss [P05] [c0135] [c0077] [c0078] [c0417] [c0418]. Building created something to keep, but completion was provisional rather than a one-way move from finished construction to upkeep [A03] [c0164] [c0233] [c0365]. A developer explicitly described a script as automatically repairing vandalism, supporting a maintenance-to-automation purpose; the stronger claim that interval-limited manpower necessarily forced the adoption is not independently established [A06] [c0142] [c0434].

Attribution was itself work: one participant admitted placing white in green, a reply attributed damage to a possible outsider, and an American apologized for attacking the Irish symbol under a mistaken attribution before switching to defence [P06] [c0042] [c0080] [c0508]. Reports naming script users or instigators remain participant attributions, not verified accounts of those actors [P06] [c0483] [c0294].

## D04 — Order without law

Defence gave a reason to negotiate: the designated envoy returned from talks with Undertale and Europe with a defence plan [R02] [P08] [A04] [A19] [c0136] [c0137]. Cooperation included protecting Temmie conditionally and planning a Celtic band with the Transflag, supporting coalition and joint-work processes without requiring a formal organization [P17] [c0143] [c0245]. An appeal to leave the FeedTheBeast work alone explicitly invoked an understanding and its prior occupancy, so the agreement supplied a reason to restrain an attack [P07] [A05] [A17] [c0205] [c0206].

The flag became a background for other groups' art [P19] [c0281] [c0466]. But “hosting” did not always begin with a host's permission: one participant says the engulfed works were there first and had greater entitlement [P19] [c0299]. Permission was also selective: a request from r/Drunk was rejected as plastering a “worst stereotype” on the flag [P19] [c0271] [c0272]. Prior occupancy and acceptable representation therefore qualify host sovereignty [P19] [c0287] [c0299].

Participants explained the arrangement as land in exchange for keeping the flag intact; “they will hold that land for you” is their strategic explanation, not an independently measured survival effect [P16] [A14] [c0422] [c0431]. Under threat, allied help was proposed, but the record does not establish the complete shortage-request-delivery chain for that episode [A13] [c0505]. Requests could be refused, including an American appeal answered with opposition to “Trumpland”; another reply separated Trump from America [P16] [c0305] [c0307] [c0308].

The tentative authority process is instantiated, not left pending: “no real authority” qualifies chat participants' neutrality, while an envoy-like directive to ignore the wall coexists with calls to attack it [P21] [c0274] [c0275] [c0496] [c0497]. ## D05 — Collective identity

Existing identity supplied both imagery and arguments: “30 pixels a day” reused a local joke, green was professed as a cause, and an anti-wall participant appealed to Irish people as “OG immigrants” [K04] [c0034] [c0027] [c0241]. The Irish-language venue carried canvas explanations within an existing weekly format rather than demonstrating that the event created that format [T4-004] [c0007] [c0010].

Historical-political identification can name a target in the attack turn itself: Republican slogans were invited over the Union Jack, and Wales and Scotland were recruited to destroy it [C02] [c0033] [c0228]. This supports an enacted inherited antagonism [K03], not an independently documented earlier inter-subreddit quarrel [K03] [c0033] [c0209]. Representation remained contested: “Up the Ra” was condemned as divisive, while “Cup the Bra” was offered as something the whole country could support [P02] [c0062] [c0110] [c0111].
