## Posts against comments

Posts make the public calls and status claims: build the flag (`62mwx2`), defend it, save the Trans-Crossroad, or declare “we won” (`6395kx`). Comments contain most of the work: patching (`dfnxquk`), checking miscolorers (`dfo0dt0`), arranging Discord (`dfodn30`), negotiating overlap (`dfod5p3`; `dfolp61`), and answering an invasion alert (`dfpmt7n`; `dfpncyz`). The layers therefore agree that the room acted, but posts compress the effort into rallying and celebration while comments show maintenance and limits.

One direct tension is in batch 02: the post says, “Let's make the mega man blast a trans flag!!!” (`62vkl1`), while a comment replies, “Let's not co-opt the other pieces on there” (`dfpfh6q`) [batch 02]. This is a participant disagreement about expansion, not a later community reversal. In batch 03, the r/UCSC post frames overlap as a drawing proposition (`63394b`), while a comment asks why it needs to be “on top of our flag” (`dfqymj9`); the moderator's conditional permission supplies a local compromise (`dfr4zc8`) [batch 03]. In another batch-03 thread, the post offers “Trans Womyn of r/place” (`63aoyo`), but the first coded comment asks only “why the y?” (`dfsqe5b`); the r/place account stays in the post layer while the comment layer disputes the wording [batch 03]. In batch 04, “me all fucking weekend” (`63cpxu`) is image-dependent and does not name the event; comments alone make the regained flag, destruction, panic, and spying explicit (`dft3w7x`; `dft8gr6`; `dftf2nl`) [batch 04].

## Counter-reading

My one-sentence reading is: this room turned a quick trans-flag proposal into shared building, repair, defense, negotiated coexistence, and Discord coordination, then collectively retold the surviving flag as a win and a source of support.

The most consequential competing account is that “we won” smooths over a project participants rejected, refused to defend, or regarded mainly as lost, ruined, or harmful to neighboring art. If my reading were wrong, the record should contain participants disowning the flag, refusing its calls rather than only limiting expansion, denying that many people helped, or calling the finished result a failure or injury.

The strongest contrary item is `dft8gr6`: “they destroyed 1/4 of the fucking flag and we all shit our pants.” It changes an uncomplicated victory story into one that includes major loss and collective panic, but its same passage first remembers laughing when opponents could not break through, and later recollection celebrates regaining the flag (`dft3w7x`). The qualification therefore survives as part of the victory account rather than overturning it.

I searched the complete record for flag/place/void passages paired with failure, loss, destruction, regret, hate, refusal, excessive size, and denials of help or contribution, and read every returned item through the corpus reader. Relevant results showed internal limits and anticipated backlash, not rejection of the project. One member says, “We don't want that part” and orders removal of a spontaneous branch (`dfomewy`); another anticipates “the wave of hate” over “that flag taking all that space” after April Fools (`dfp65hs`). These late findings add a corrected-boundary code and a single-code theme for anticipating backlash. The denial-of-help search returned only gratitude for the void making defense interesting (`dfsygg3`), not denial of participation. Within this query scope, no in-window result disowns the flag, denies broad help, or calls the finished result a failure. Because word-based searches cannot express every possible objection, this null result limits but does not eliminate the competing account.

## Saturation

The record is complete as an assigned census: four slices dispose all 193 threads, every event-touching retained thread was read whole by its coder, and the audit verified all pooled codes, pointers, exclusion citations, and forest nodes. It is not complete as access to the whole practice. Discord is visible through invitations and reports but its decisions are not; linked images are unseen; deleted bodies and unresolved parents create local gaps. No one reader held the whole week, one coder read each arbitrary slice, and there is no agreement measure. Relations assembled across boundaries are therefore weaker and are marked 01|02 or 03|04 above.

Analytically, batches 01–03 continued to add practice. The themes “building, extending, and joining the trans flag,” “patching pixels and correcting the flag,” “negotiating crossings and leaving neighboring art alone,” “moving flag coordination into Discord,” “rallying against attacks and the void,” “celebrating the flag growing and holding,” and “bringing room jokes, names, and reaction formats onto the canvas” first appear in batch 01. “Taking the flag as proof of support and not being alone” first appears in batch 02. “Retelling the win and thanking who made it” first appears in batch 03. Batch 04 repeats that settled retrospective practice rather than adding another one. The step-5 search opens one narrow single-code theme, “anticipating hate for taking all that space,” from a batch-02 item discovered by the lead. Thus later slices brought genuinely new meaning and post-event accounting through batch 03; the final slice largely repeated the retrospective.

## Selection

I read the briefing and the complete openings table before the branch decision. The gate assigned branch B, so four fleet coders read whole-thread slices in corpus order; I did not read raw threads while the fleet ran. Each coder retained threads where visible text showed r/place building, repair, defense, negotiation, reaction, or retrospective conduct, and excluded ordinary memes, transition talk, politics, or medical discussion when the triage window did not touch the canvas. After consolidation I read the pooled codes slice by slice beside each slice case, then read individual search hits during the permitted counter-reading stage. The complete audit verified 193 dispositions and all 31 required exclusion citations.

## Absence

Deleted or removed bodies are not reconstructed. Some surviving titles still support bounded conduct, including the first proposal (`62mwx2`) and wrong-blue admission (`62xt1i`), while AutoModerator notices show the room's hosting-rule enforcement rather than the linked image contents. The forest also exposes uncoded stretches and missing visual material; claims here rest on quoted participant words, not inferred images. The Discord record is partial by design, and only what participants brought back can support this account. The counter-reading's failure to find disowning or a failed-result account applies to the complete searchable text and the stated queries; it cannot establish what deleted bodies, images, unresolved parents, or off-platform talk would have contained.
