## Chronology

The record opens at d0 03-31 19:22 with the room already making blue into organised canvas work. Across the episode bounded by `62nfcq` and `62re9i`, people call themselves guards and defenders, try to bring several blue subreddits together, post coordinates and Discord invitations, divide cleaning from expansion, build a sign that points back to the subreddit, and negotiate borders and protected pictures. The earliest moderator rules are not simply “make everything blue”: `62phg8` says to expand from the border, outline protected art in blue, respect alliances, and “DO NOT ATTACK ART.” Other participants already answer that the whole field should be blue. This opening stretch is carried from the batch 01 and 02 slice accounts and their codes rather than from a whole-week raw reading by the lead.

At d0 04-01 07:45, the episode bounded by `62reah` and `62syt9` turns toward a concentrated purple-coloured attack. Alarm posts ask people to return to the corner and repair the sign. Replies repeatedly attribute the attack to Destiny's viewers rather than the established Purple Corner, and moderator tasks tell members not to retaliate against Purple Corner. By d0 04-01 13:02, `62skn7` says the stream has stopped and orders a sequence of removing remaining purple, repairing the mainland, and restoring the sign. Participants then resume proposals for expansion, alliances, and art, so defense changes the priority without ending the older argument over what blue land is for. This attack and recovery cross the batch 02, 03, and 04 slice boundaries.

From d0 04-01 14:22 at `62szdu` through d1 04-02 06:34 at `62y9lo`, the room tries to convert recovery into repeatable work. It forms task forces, rebuilds the sign, calls “CLEAN, CLEAN, CLEAN!,” negotiates a border with Germany, rejects some scripts, teaches newcomers what counts as debris, and receives a steady stream of requests for refuge, artwork, or alliances. Moderator tasks alternate among cleanup, sign repair, defense, and expansion. The practical compromise varies: art may stay behind a blue border or on a blue background, while the sign and a small core receive stronger protection. A local exchange makes the rule effective: at d1 04-01 22:53 `dfpimpg` says, “Don't destroy art even if it's not your thing”; the member agrees to stop, and at d1 04-01 22:59 `dfpiwbr` replies, “That is appreciated, thank you.” The instrumented interval is six minutes.

The episode from d1 04-02 06:34 at `62y9lo` through d2 04-03 06:08 at `635024` narrows the broad field into a defensible sign and pure-blue core while leaving art elsewhere negotiable. Members debate a 100-by-100 corner, “negative space” around pictures, a taskbar proposed by r/placestart, and whether a blue background is enough. At d1 04-02 15:10 the moderator post `6304nz` orders the room to stop concentrating on an unworkable corner and let people build art; 11 minutes 2 seconds later, its direct reply `dfqaco9` says, “The 100x100 blue corner is our art.” Later moderators reserve the far bottom-right as pure blue while permitting named work elsewhere, and `dfqj3ox` describes that corner as appeasing the room's “more reactionary” members. This is a descriptive contrast among positions, not evidence that the community changed its mind as one body.

From d2 04-03 06:16 at `63516e` through the shutdown episode ending with `638ms8`, reclamation and selective preservation collide most sharply. The moderator task `6357o1` says the room “got too big and lost control over the quality,” directs a retaking inside a border, and says allied or good-looking work should not be touched. Posts and replies nevertheless call for consuming Columbia and Bullet Kin; other replies invoke agreements, repair the art, or blame a “hivemind” that organisers cannot control. A communal history essay moves into Google Docs even before placement ends. At d3 04-03 17:00, shutdown is followed by claims of victory, thanks to sign defenders, mourning for lost art, arguments over broken promises, and invitations to replacement canvases. Those invitations follow shutdown by minutes in the slice account, but the record does not establish a single causal decision or an accepted destination.

The final episode, bounded by d3 04-03 18:26 at `638niu` and d4 04-04 16:30 at `63f73p`, turns pixel work into remembrance and attempted continuation. Participants preserve final images, call unwanted pixels “battle scars,” retell the purple fight, dispute whether leadership or purists saved the corner, apologise for or celebrate destroyed sprites, and test pxls.space and other clones. No successor becomes a shared course in the window. A proposal at `63e8aq` to memorialise destroyed art in the subreddit banner receives a moderator offer 2 hours 54 minutes 20 seconds later at `dftlvyr`: “If someone makes a banner ... I'd be happy to set it as the banner!” The record closes not on more placement but on keeping the Blue Corner alive through stories, symbols, and uncertain new canvases.

## Relations — what set off what

The loss of Mega Man is named as the reason to formalise protection. In `dfobhxs`, a participant warns that without a list, “more art may be swallowed up like MegaMan was”; a moderator promises to take that proposal to the team, and a later preservation post establishes a voting process. The passage supports a response relation between a named loss and rule-making, though it does not show that the rule reached every placer.

The purple-coloured incursion sets off defense, repair, and a dispute about retaliation. `dfoplpb` identifies “a strike by Omnidestiny and his fans,” after which replies and moderator posts distinguish those viewers from Purple Corner and redirect people toward rebuilding. The later post `62rttk` explicitly says to “pull our efforts away from green and red” to defend home. The relation is supported by participants naming the attack while changing the task, not merely by adjacent timestamps. [crosses 02|03] The broader claim that this attack made cleanup impossible is a participant's own explanation: `62w1jp` says manpower had been diverted while “rogue and splinter groups bombarded our territory.” That passage supports what this speaker attributed to the attack, not an independently established causal effect. [crosses 04|05]

Art-destruction calls sometimes produce enforceable replies. In `62vytk`, `dfpimpg` answers an attack on disliked art with “Don't destroy art even if it's not your thing”; the intervening reply accepts the authority, and `dfpiwbr` thanks the member six minutes after the moderator instruction. The words and acceptance support a locally closed correction, while later disputes show that it did not settle policy room-wide.

The later leadership dispute is a reply sequence and a stated disagreement, not proof that either turn caused a strategic shift. `6304nz` says to stop focusing on the corner because “it is not working” and art is being built there; its direct reply `dfqaco9`, 11 minutes 2 seconds later, insists that the 100-by-100 corner “is our art.” This exchange demonstrates incompatible definitions of the project. No later passage establishes that one caused the community as a whole to adopt the other's position.

The reclamation order gives its own reason: `6357o1` says Blue “got too big and lost control over the quality,” then calls for a border and clearing inside it. That supports a participant-authored explanation linking perceived noise to reclamation. Within the resulting fight, the loss of Bullet Kin explicitly sets off replacement work: `6368fl` says, “With the original bullet gone, I propose that we replace him.” The record shows the proposal, not its completion.

The memorial-banner response is direct. `63e8aq` proposes a banner to “immortalise the artwork we had inadvertently destroyed”; `dftlvyr` offers to install a suitable sprite banner 2 hours 54 minutes 20 seconds later. The offer answers the proposal, but no in-window item shows that the banner was made or installed. Shutdown, farewell posts, and replacement-canvas invitations also form an observed sequence; because no passage binds them into one decision, the account does not claim that shutdown caused a particular migration.

## Posts against comments

Early posts present alliances and protected art as rules; comments report cleaners erasing permitted pictures, call treaties weakness, and demand complete blue. The posts say art should be outlined and spared; the comment layer says the rules often do not reach people placing pixels. [batch 01]

Attack posts repeatedly say “purple” is invading; comments identify Destiny's viewers and insist Purple Corner did not break the alliance. The post layer supplies the alarm, while the comment layer corrects who should be blamed and whether retaliation is warranted. [batch 02]

Moderator posts issue unified current tasks—rebuild, clean, expand, fix the sign—while comments dispute each priority, reject some scripts, report inaccessible Discord coordination, and keep working on tasks the post has displaced. The posts say the room can follow one plan; the comment layer shows limited control and simultaneous plans. [batch 05]

Expansion posts treat more blue ground as progress; comments ask why the room is expanding while its home and sign remain damaged, or say pure blue is impossible and background control is enough. The layers expose a descriptive contrast between expansion, defense, and minimum blueness rather than a single development in collective opinion. [batch 06]

Later reclamation posts say allied and good-looking art “are not to be touched”; comments call for consuming Columbia and Bullet Kin, report attacking them, defend the agreements, and say organised Blue cannot control the “hivemind.” Official selective preservation and practical pure-blue clearing occur together. [batch 11]

After shutdown, posts declare victory or invite another battle; comments answer that protected art was lost, that damaged pixels are battle scars, that captchas and clone sites are exhausting, or that Place mattered because it ended. Posts offer conclusions, while comments preserve contested responsibility and resistance to continuation. [batch 12] [batch 13]

## Counter-reading

My reading is that this room coordinated a territorial project that repeatedly moved among expansion, defense, cleanup, and selectively hosting art without settling who could define Blue; after shutdown, participants carried that disagreement into memory and attempted continuations.

Before searching, I set out the strongest plausible competing account in the case's own vocabulary: pure-blue defense was the stable shared practice, while alliances and art protection were temporary overlays or leadership mistakes. If my reading were wrong, the record should contain repeated explicit rejections of protected art and negotiated compromise across the week, together with reports that pure-blue priorities prevailed and held.

The strongest contrary item is `636kzk`. Its “Scorched Canvas Policy” says “no more art” and “no more alliances” and blames weak leadership. `62wo8v` similarly says, “no more peace. no more deals. no more art. only blue,” while `638a2s` later says, “The corner remained blue.” These are consequential contrary evidence: pure-blue defense was not a marginal afterthought, and a blue core survived.

The search also returned evidence the competing account does not absorb. The early moderator rules in `62phg8` explicitly protect alliances and art; `dfqj3ox` calls the pure corner an accommodation to members who wanted no art; `dfpe1ep` prefers a large “dirty blue” territory to a small pure one; and `dfrypa0` wants a small pure corner while being glad a purge stopped at its borders. The last item is a hybrid position inside one speaker, not a midpoint inferred between speakers. The search therefore changes the emphasis but not the main reading: pure-blue defense was a durable pole and core claim, while art protection, alliances, and compromise were also repeated official and practical work. No query can establish the absence of broader agreement in a record with deletion and off-platform coordination; the conclusion rests on the contrary and qualifying passages that are present.

## Saturation

The pooled codes show both repetition and development. Coordinating pixels, expanding or cleaning blue ground, defending and repairing, deciding which art stays, negotiating allies and borders, arguing over authority, making Blue identity, and circulating calls all first appear in batch 01. Recounting and celebrating the fight first appears in batch 02, initially during the event; trying to continue on another canvas first appears in batch 08 and becomes prominent after shutdown in batches 12 and 13. Later slices repeatedly reshape established practices around a smaller core, new threats, and named art rather than replacing them. The genuinely new late work is sustained aftermath: accounting for destroyed art, memorialising the fight, and testing successors. The record does not saturate to “no novelty.”

The Reddit record is broad but not socially whole. All 13 slices were read, yet no reader held the whole week, one coder read each slice, and there is no agreement measure. Relations assembled from slice accounts and the forest are weaker than relations made by one continuous reader; the purple attack and its aftermath cross batches 02|03 and 04|05, and those links are carried only where the slice passages name the attack or response. Arbitrary boundaries also split exchanges between readers even when context openings preserved an edge.

Deletion and removal hide some reasons, images were not inspected as canvas evidence, and pixel authorship cannot be recovered from Reddit claims. Discord, Twitch, private messages, polls, Google Docs, streams, and successor sites are themselves visible choices of venue, but their internal deliberation is not. Reddit often receives targets, agreements, invitations, and summaries back; it cannot show who accepted them off-platform or whether anonymous placers followed them. Replies continuing after the window and proposed successor activity likewise limit what the apparent lack of a durable destination can establish.

## Other communities named

Other communities are part of the room's work, not one outside bloc. Rainbow Road appears as an ally and disputed border partner in `dfokqts`; Purple Corner is distinguished from the Destiny viewers blamed in `dfoplpb`; r/placestart is alternately a negotiated neighbour, a blue-taskbar partner, and a threat in `630mwf`; and r/EnterTheGungeon, Columbia, Samus, Tails, Rocket League, farmcarrots, osu!, and many smaller art communities appear as petitioners, protected works, helpers, or losses. The Black Void is both feared as an attacker and offered as an alternative identity.

Discord carries rapid targeting, task forces, alliance lists, and some decisions; Twitch supplies rallies and an attributed attack audience; Google Docs carries the communal history draft; and pxls.space and other clones receive attempts to continue. Exact names and relations remain in the pointer record. This account treats claims about what those communities promised or did as this room's claims unless a visible reply establishes the local exchange.

## Absence

Removed and deleted bodies are common across alarms, proposals, moderator posts, and short aftermath threads. Surviving titles can support narrow actions, and replies often show what people answered—permission, refusal, correction, mockery, or repair—but they cannot recover the missing rationale. Machine-generated previews and summoned bot text were treated as setting rather than community conduct.

The most important absence is authorship of pixels. Participants distinguish moderators, Reddit members, Discord organisers, outsiders, scripts, spare accounts, extremists, and a “hivemind,” but the record cannot identify who placed a given pixel. Nor can it show linked images directly, private Discord deliberation, the live Twitch audience, the whole Google Doc, or sustained activity on successor canvases. Those limits qualify claims about control, responsibility, and failed continuation; they do not erase the visible practices of issuing tasks, negotiating, replying, mourning, and remembering.
