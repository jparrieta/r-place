## Vernacular

“Deadhead” and the shorter “head” are self-labels; “old east coast head” marks experience within that label.

> Honestly, and rather unfortunately, that is most non-Deadheads' opinions of me when I tell them that I listen to the Dead. (`dfe70hq`)

> OMG, old east coast head here and was there. (`dfd4cuj`)

“d&c” abbreviates Dead and Company.

> Hope you're planning on catching some d&c this summer, worth every penny (`dfe714w`)

“SBD,” “AUD,” and “matrix” are recording-source labels used without explanation; people specify them when requesting a version of a live performance.

> Please specify whether you want sbd, aud or matrix. If there's not a matrix, then It'll be sbd. (`6296by`)

“Shakedown” is used for the vending and social area around a show, while “the lot” names the broader setting.

> Didint get a shakedown spot for our vending plans. Simple fix; Set up in the middle of the freakin road with all our shit. (`dferw81`)

> noone had ever seen a food like that on the lot. (`dferw81`)

“Hopping on the bus” means becoming part of the continuing fan scene.

> Lots of new fans hopping on the bus, new cover/tribute bands popping up all the time. (`dffiszs`)

## Register

The served conversations are casual, warm, and often profane. A young listener’s uncertainty drew reassurance and delight at hearing that a younger person loved the music.

> It's beautiful for me to hear young people say they love the music. (`dfe88z4`)

Serious advice can sit beside obvious deadpan exaggeration in the same thread.

> Yes. You have to do drugs at Dead shows. The security that is outside of the venues is there to check to make sure that everyone has at least one drug on them. (`dfe88z9`)
